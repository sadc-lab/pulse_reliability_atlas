\section{Pulse engine in context}\label{sec:s1}

The Pulse Physiology Engine~\cite{bray2019} is among the most actively maintained open-source whole-body human physiology simulators. It represents the body as a network of coupled electrical-circuit analogues --- vascular resistances as resistors, vessel and chamber compliances as capacitors, heart and respiratory muscles as time-varying pressure sources --- solved as a system of ordinary differential equations at a 20~ms fixed timestep. The cardiovascular, respiratory (a 5-compartment lung model: carina, left and right anatomic dead space, left and right alveoli)~\cite{pulserespmethod}, gas-exchange, endocrine, renal, and drug sub-models share state at each step, producing a self-consistent whole-body trajectory at about 5--10$\times$ real time, depending on machine specifications~\cite{pulsefaq}. The codebase is C++ with bindings for Java, C\#, and Unity~\cite{pulseabout} and an interface for Python~\cite{bray2019}, and deploys on Windows, Mac, Linux, and ARM~\cite{pulseabout}.

Pulse was forked in 2017 from the U.S.\ Department of Defense--funded BioGears engine by the original BioGears core developers (Clipp, Bray, Webb) and is now maintained by Kitware, Inc.\ under the Apache-2.0 license. Both projects still exist independently but have diverged substantially since 2017; Pulse v4.3.1 includes respiratory calibration for ventilated patients, mechanoreceptor feedback, lung-recruitment modelling, and improved hemorrhage response that are not present in BioGears.

The Pulse documentation explicitly characterises the engine as a ``low-fidelity clinical approach'' intended for training simulation rather than patient-specific clinical prediction. This self-description frames the present atlas's contribution (Section~\mainref{sec:intro}{1} of the main manuscript): a quantitative characterisation of Pulse's operational envelope that helps downstream users distinguish regions meeting the stated screening criteria from those requiring scenario-specific review. The current upstream stable head is \texttt{REL\_4\_3\_2} (released 2025-08-12), whose full diff against \texttt{REL\_4\_3\_1} is build-system-only (7 files / +46 / -17 lines: web-WASM dockcross support and a .NET binding tweak); no physiology, validation, patient-setup, or solver code changed. Results in this atlas on v4.3.1 therefore apply identically to v4.3.2.

Rows 5--6 were subsequently re-derived within this repository. On 2026-07-26, eight single-purpose probes on the male reference body tested both sides of each documented bound under the atlas's own engine image; both bounds are enforced at patient setup, before any ventilation begins. Heart rates of 49 and 111~bpm and respiratory rates of 7 and 21~bpm were each rejected with an explicit \texttt{[ERROR]} and exit code 1, no output being written, while 50, 110, 8 and 20 were accepted and ran to completion. Pulse names the bound in its own rejection text (for example, ``Maximum heart rate baseline allowed is 110 bpm''), and the corresponding branches are visible in \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp}. This does not alter the design-time provenance recorded in the table above, which is what justified the sampler ranges when they were locked; it removes the reliance on an external repository for anyone re-verifying the two bounds today.

Two earlier assertions from our own internal probing in the companion technical work were tested empirically before preregistration locked and could not be confirmed: (i)~the claim that Pulse crashes at FiO$_2$ = 1.0 (empirical Docker probing showed Pulse accepts FiO$_2$ = 1.0 and produces a stable trajectory; the transient hyperoxia regime is characterised separately in Section~\mainref{sec:results-hyperoxia}{2.4} of the main manuscript and in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s9}); and (ii)~the claim that Pulse has no sex-specific physiology beyond patient weight and height. Pulse's blood-volume baseline is not the counter-example: \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp} computes it from weight alone, so the divergence mechanism of Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript and Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s10} is silent on sex. A subsequent source-code audit identified other sex-dependent patient-setup pathways in Pulse. In particular, the Harris--Benedict calculation in \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp} sets a sex-dependent patient basal metabolic rate. Under the resting, normothermic conditions used in the atlas, the engine's energy model uses this value both to initialise total metabolic rate and as the set point toward which total metabolic rate is regulated; total metabolic rate in turn drives tissue oxygen consumption and carbon-dioxide production. The assertion is therefore withdrawn on that source-code evidence rather than on the blood-volume result. Because the 1{,}280-run sweep varies sex jointly with body mass and height, it cannot on its own quantify the independent effect of the sex flag.

\section{Parameter provenance and supplementary analyses}\label{sec:s3}

The Methods section of the main manuscript states the constants the atlas runs on: the hard physiological bounds and the drift thresholds that gate the four-flag classifier, the clamp patterns, the sampler settings, and the two reference bodies. This section does not restate them. It documents where each one came from --- the clinical guideline, physiological reference or code artefact behind it --- and then reports the analyses that characterise the design rather than define it: the cost of the pulse-pressure clip on diastolic elementary effects, the fate of the preregistered Sobol bolt-on, and robustness checks on the traffic-light baseline and on the most contestable of the hard bounds.

\subsection{Hard physiological bounds: per-bound provenance}\label{sec:s3-hard-bounds}

The six \texttt{(lower, upper)} pairs that gate the \texttt{implausible} flag are stated in Section~\mainref{sec:methods-detector}{4.4} of the main manuscript and are held in the \texttt{HARD\_BOUNDS} dictionary in \texttt{src/detector.py}. Each pair was locked in preregistration before any data was collected and none has been revised post-hoc. Each is regex-extracted from the source file at audit time by its \texttt{T1.06p.*} ledger entry, so any drift between the code and the manuscript is caught immediately. One display difference is worth naming: Pulse reports SpO$_2$ as a fraction in [0, 1], so the bound the main text writes as [50, 100]~\% is held in the code as 0.50 / 1.00.

Each bound falls into one of two logical categories. Category (a) is a physical or definitional ceiling, whose only instance is the SpO$_2$ upper bound at hemoglobin saturation. Category (b) is a clinical-plausibility threshold, beyond which a 60-second steady-state mean would be incompatible with a viable patient. No bound rests on an equipment specification. The per-bound primary-literature anchor is documented in Table~\ref{tab:hard-bounds-rationale}; the citation set mirrors the per-bound rationale audited in the companion technical monograph (§2.6).

\subsection{Drift thresholds for the not\_steady\_state flag}\label{sec:s3-drift}

The companion \texttt{DRIFT\_MAX\_DELTA\_60S} dictionary (Table~\ref{tab:drift}) sets the per-channel maximum tolerated $|\Delta| = |\text{slope} \times 60~\text{s}|$ that an output may exhibit across the final 60 seconds of the steady-state window before the run is flagged \texttt{not\_steady\_state} for drift. Drift is estimated by ordinary least-squares regression on the 13 sample points in the [60, 120]~s window (5-second sample interval); the $R^2$ of the fit is logged as diagnostic metadata but does not gate the flag. These thresholds are clinical-meaningfulness anchors --- beyond them, a bedside clinician would read the trajectory as actively changing rather than at homeostasis. Unlike the hard bounds, they are atlas-internal calibration choices rather than biological survival limits; the per-channel magnitudes are anchored in standard critical-care monitoring practice (Table~\ref{tab:drift-rationale}) rather than in mortality-incompatibility primary literature, and the citations in Table~\ref{tab:drift-rationale} identify the relevant clinical-monitoring framing rather than primary-literature endorsements of the specific drift magnitudes. Like the hard bounds, each value is regex-extracted from \texttt{src/detector.py} at audit time.

Two further \texttt{not\_steady\_state} triggers complement the drift check. The coefficient-of-variation threshold \texttt{CV\_THRESHOLD = 0.05} fires when the standard deviation of any output across the steady-state window exceeds 5~\% of its mean. The flatline-at-bound criterion fires when \texttt{std / |mean| < 0.01} AND the mean is within 0.1~\% of a hard bound, distinguishing numerical pinning at a bound from physiological saturation at that bound. Both constants are recorded in the companion code repository and are locked under the same preregistration discipline as the bounds and drift thresholds.

\subsection{Clamp patterns: provenance and the post-sweep patch}\label{sec:s3-clamp}

The \texttt{clamped} flag fires when Pulse's setup-rejection log matches any of a locked set of regular-expression patterns, each targeting one observed Pulse rejection message. The set printed below is the one currently in \texttt{src/detector.py}. The 1{,}280-run sweep reported here was classified with the \emph{four}-pattern version of this set, which was empirically calibrated against deliberate-clamp probes in the pilot study before the full sweep launched, replacing an earlier overly-broad regex set that produced 100~\% false-positive clamping by matching Pulse's normal solver convergence messages. The note after the listing records what changed afterwards, and why it leaves the published classification unaltered:

\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\small, breaklines=true]
r"baseline of [\d\.]+ (?:\S+ )?is too (?:high|low)"
r"maximum [\w\s]+ baseline allowed is"
r"minimum [\w\s]+ baseline allowed is"
r"must be modeled by adding/using a condition"
r"is outside the valid range"
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent Detector validation on twenty reference runs in the companion translation repository was 20 / 20 clean with the four-pattern set, which is the surface that survives the false-positive elimination from the original regex.

\noindent \textbf{Patch of 2026-07-26, applied after the sweep.} Two changes produce the five-pattern set above: the unit token between the value and ``is'' became optional, and a \texttt{minimum \ldots\ baseline allowed is} pattern was added to mirror the existing \texttt{maximum} one. Pulse's \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp} builds ten baseline-rejection strings, of which nine write the unit; the heart-rate lower bound is the sole exception, so a heart rate below 50~bpm was rejected by Pulse at setup yet did not raise \texttt{clamped}. Replaying both pattern sets over all 3{,}348 Pulse logs on disk flips exactly one run --- the probe written to expose the gap --- and no run in the published sweep, whose requested heart rates never leave [50, 110]~bpm. The patch therefore has no effect on the 1{,}280-run classification reported here; it is printed so that users adopting this detector do not inherit the gap. No run was lost to the gap in any case: a setup rejection exits non-zero with no CSV, so \texttt{diverged} fires instead.

\subsection{Body-dimension provenance for the two adult reference patient bodies}\label{sec:s3-bodies}

The two adult reference bodies are: a male body (age 18, 67.3~kg, 180~cm) and a female body (age 18, 56.2~kg, 163~cm). Both weights are U.S.\ Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) growth-chart 50th-percentile values at 214 months (17~y 10~m) for each sex~\cite{kuczmarski2002cdc}; the corresponding CDC values at exactly 18.0 years are 67.64 and 56.41~kg --- 0.34 and 0.21~kg above the values used. The preregistration's provenance erratum describes these weights as the CDC 50th-percentile values \emph{at 18 years (216.5 months)}; that description is inaccurate. The weights in use are the 50th percentile of the 214--214.99-month row, two rows earlier in the same CDC table, rounded from 67.28992603 and 56.22969564~kg. That off-by-two-rows lookup was inherited from the companion pediatric pipeline, and is identical for both sexes. This is a misstatement of provenance, not a protocol deviation. The preregistration names two bodies, 67.3~kg / 180~cm and 56.2~kg / 163~cm; those are exactly the two bodies simulated in all 1{,}280 runs, and no registered quantity was changed. The female height matches both the CDC 50th-percentile value~\cite{kuczmarski2002cdc} and Pulse's own \texttt{heightStandardFemale\_cm = 163.0} reference constant in \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp}; the male height is a clinically standard adult value chosen so the male case fixes the body mass index at 20.8 (it is not Pulse's source-code \texttt{heightStandardMale\_cm = 177.0} reference, which would have yielded a near-CDC-P50 BMI). Pulse computes the resulting blood-volume baseline via the allometric relation $\text{BV} = 65.6 \cdot \text{weight}_{\text{kg}}^{1.02}$, yielding 4{,}802.63~mL for the male body and 3{,}996.09~mL for the female body --- the 17~\% sex gap whose mechanism on the input-extreme divergence cluster is characterised in Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript.

\subsection{Four-flag classifier: priority order and rationale}\label{sec:s3-detector-order}

The detector's flag priority order \texttt{clamped > diverged > implausible > not\_steady\_state > clean} traces the chronology of failure modes a Pulse simulation can exhibit, from input handling through solver execution to output evaluation against biological and dynamic criteria. \texttt{clamped} is the input-contract breach: Pulse has silently substituted at least one setup input, so the simulation no longer corresponds to the Morris grid point that was requested and no downstream flag would refer to the input being studied --- disqualifying the run at this layer is non-negotiable. \texttt{diverged} is the mathematical failure: a non-zero exit code, NaN or infinite values in the CSV, a missing CSV, or a truncated simulation --- no biological interpretation is possible. \texttt{implausible} is the biological failure: the mathematics ran cleanly but the steady-state mean falls outside the hard physiological bounds stated in Section~\mainref{sec:methods-detector}{4.4} of the main manuscript --- mathematically valid output, biologically incompatible with a viable 60-second steady-state. \texttt{not\_steady\_state} is the dynamic failure: the output is mathematically valid and biologically coherent but the trajectory has not settled in the 60-second window (flatline at a bound, coefficient of variation above 0.05, or drift exceeding the per-channel thresholds in Table~\ref{tab:drift}) --- the result is interpretable as a transient, not as steady-state physiology.

\subsection{Cost of the pulse-pressure clip on diastolic elementary effects}\label{sec:s3-clip-cost}

Clipping DBP when each configuration is written, rather than when the sample is drawn, keeps the Morris sample geometrically intact. It also introduces a quantifiable distortion, reported here rather than left implicit: for the steps it touches, the engine receives a smaller diastolic change than the sample coordinate records, while the elementary-effect estimator divides by the full nominal step.

Of the 160 sampled points, 35 carry a requested DBP above the 0.75 ratio and are clipped. Two consequences follow, both fixed by the sample alone and therefore identical across all eight cells. First, 9 of the 20 diastolic steps are shortened. The nominal Morris step in DBP is two-thirds of the 20~mmHg range, or 13.33~mmHg; five of the nine fall to 6.25~\% of that (0.83~mmHg), one to 56.25~\% and three to 62.5~\%, and none is reduced to zero. Second, in 8 of the 140 steps the flipped input is SBP, whose change moves the $0.75 \times \mathrm{SBP}$ threshold and hence the applied DBP as well; for those steps two inputs change at once, violating the one-at-a-time assumption. The remaining 123 of 140 steps (87.9~\%) are untouched. After flag filtering, 41 of 78 clean diastolic pairs are shortened and 19 of 68 clean systolic pairs are affected.

The distortion does not overturn the reported findings. H1 holds in 48 of 48 cell--output combinations under either estimator (median top-3 fraction 0.928 nominal versus 0.910 applied-step; minimum 0.658 versus 0.696), and output MAP remains SBP-dominated in all eight cells. What does change is the standing of DBP itself: under the applied-step estimator it enters the top three in 9 of the 48 combinations from which the reported ranking excludes it, and its $\mu^{*}$ rises by a median factor of 2.4 (maximum 15.4). The reported $\mu^{*}$ for DBP should therefore be read as a lower bound. We do not substitute the applied-step estimator for the preregistered one. Dividing by the applied step changes the finite-difference denominator on the shortened diastolic transitions, but it does not restore the one-at-a-time property on the eight systolic transitions where the ratio constraint moves DBP as well, and the effective diastolic step then ranges from 0.83 to 13.33~mmHg, which is no longer the fixed-step design the Morris index is defined on. Step length is also structurally confounded with position in the sampled space: the shortest steps occur only at SBP = 90~mmHg, whereas all 11 full-length diastolic steps occur at SBP $\geq$ 100~mmHg, so no step at the lowest systolic setting is full length and no difference between the two estimators can be attributed to step length alone. The applied-step $\mu^{*}$ is therefore reported as evidence on the direction of the clip's effect, not as a term-by-term corrected index. Imposing the ratio at sampling time instead would forfeit the very sample geometry the design was built to protect.

Two further estimators test whether the reported systolic dominance depends on the affected steps. The \emph{uncontaminated-step} estimator drops every clean pair in which the applied DBP co-varies with SBP; the \emph{trajectory-matched} variant additionally restricts every input to the 12 of 20 trajectories whose systolic step is uncontaminated, so that no input is estimated on more data than another. Two \emph{combined} estimators apply the applied-step denominator and the contaminated-pair exclusion at once, a pairing evaluated neither above nor by the published index. SBP remained rank 1 for MAP in all eight cells under all six estimators (nominal-step, applied-step, uncontaminated-step, trajectory-matched, hybrid-combined and matched-combined). DBP is the runner-up in all eight cells under the combined estimators, and the smallest margin is a 1.22-fold $\mu^{*}$ ratio in S2\_M. Under the hybrid-combined estimator, DBP would overtake SBP in a cell only if its applied-to-nominal $\mu^{*}$ ratio exceeded that cell's uncontaminated-SBP-to-nominal-DBP ratio; the former ranged from 1.131 to 3.289 and the latter from 2.266 to 4.669, so the condition was met in no cell. The matched-combined analysis independently retained SBP at rank 1 in all eight cells.

A descriptive transition-stratified comparison narrows, without removing, the confounding between exclusion and position. Three systolic transitions carry both contaminated and uncontaminated steps, allowing the two groups to be compared where the systolic start and end values are identical (Table~\ref{tab:transition-stratified}). Within each of the three evaluable transitions the contaminated steps had the higher median absolute MAP elementary effect, and the stratified contrast remained positive in every leave-one-transition-out analysis. Requested DBP is perfectly collinear with contamination: structurally for the 100-to-120 and 120-to-100 transitions, whose 75~mmHg ceiling no lower grid level can exceed, and as an artefact of the realised Morris draw for 110-to-90. The observed ordering does not support a simple monotonic explanation by mean applied-DBP level alone: the largest contrast occurred for 110-to-90, despite its lower mean endpoint DBP, and mean endpoint DBP is in any case only a coarse proxy for the level at which the elementary effect is evaluated. These results are consistent with an influence of applied-DBP co-variation, but do not establish a causal contribution.

\subsection{Sobol bolt-on: preregistered decision}\label{sec:s3-sobol}

A second-tier variance-based analysis (Sobol) was preregistered as a conditional bolt-on on top of the Morris main-effect screening. The rule locked in \texttt{preregistration.md}~\S~Sampling method evaluates the top-three $\mu^{*}$-ranked inputs of each cell $\times$ output against their $\sigma / \mu^{*}$ ratio. At or below 0.4 the response is treated as linear and no Sobol is run; at or above 0.6 the input is promoted; the interval between is a declared gray zone. The ratio is the standard Morris proxy for non-linearity and input--input interaction.

Applied as written, the trigger fires on 72 of the 144 top-three inputs, spanning 43 of the 48 cell $\times$ output combinations. The same preregistration capped the bolt-on at 1{,}500 additional runs, using a cost model of $64 \times (2k + 2)$ per promoted configuration. A Sobol sample cannot be shared across cells, because each scenario--sex cell is a different model. The narrowest reading of that formula --- one promoted input at a base sample of 64 --- already costs 256 runs per cell, and 11{,}008 runs across 43 cells. The bolt-on exceeded its own preregistered envelope by more than sevenfold, and would have done so under the narrower dominant-input reading as well (8 cells, 2{,}048 runs). It is therefore reported rather than executed, and a ready-to-fire SALib implementation is preserved in the released code base.

The preregistered rule conditions on the point ratio and attaches no uncertainty qualifier. We therefore add a post-hoc sensitivity analysis, and label it as such: trajectories are resampled 1{,}000 times per combination and $\sigma / \mu^{*}$ is recomputed jointly on each draw, so that the resulting interval covers the ratio itself. Bootstrapping $\sigma$ alone and dividing by a fixed $\mu^{*}$ would ignore the covariance between the two and widen the interval in an uncontrolled direction. Under five independent seeds, 23 of the 144 top-three inputs keep a lower bound at or above 0.6 in every draw. Repeating the whole procedure at 2{,}000 and at 4{,}000 resamples returns the same 23 combinations, and at 500 one combination is lost, so the stable set does not depend on the resample count. The stable set spans 17 of the 48 cell $\times$ output combinations, all seven swept inputs, all four scenarios, and both reference bodies (12 female, 11 male). PEEP is the most frequent single input at 8 of 23, followed by respiratory rate at 5.

One structural limit governs how that map should be read. The elementary-effect estimator uses only runs that carry no flag, so a regime unstable enough to flag its own runs cannot also supply the pairs needed to characterise it. Delivered tidal volume is the clearest case. It is the most affected output in the stable set (8 of 23), yet none of those 8 falls in S1. That is the scenario where its oscillation is most prevalent: a delivered-Vt coefficient of variation above 0.05 on 139 of 320 runs, against 36 of 320 in S3. The median top-three input retains 3 clean trajectories in S1 and 16 in S3. Non-linearity is consequently easiest to establish where the engine is most stable, which is the opposite of where it matters most.

\subsection{Traffic-light baseline: robustness of the male-referenced scoring}\label{sec:s3-traffic}

The rule that scores each cell green, yellow or red is given in Section~\mainref{sec:methods-traffic}{4.5} of the main manuscript, and the three colour criteria are stated in full in the legend to Figure~2 there; the $10~\%$ and $30~\%$ deviation bands were locked as item~7 of \texttt{preregistration.md}. Reported here is the check on the one discretionary choice that rule makes --- scoring every cell, female cells included, against the healthy-male (S1, M) baseline.

We verified that this male-baseline choice does not drive the classification. Re-scoring the 24 female cells against a sex-specific (S1, F) baseline instead --- holding the flag-rate logic unchanged --- changes the colour of \emph{0 of 24} cells (reproducible via \texttt{scripts/baseline\_robustness\_check.py}). Female yellow/red verdicts are set either by the diverged/implausible flag rate or by ARDS-scale deviations (for example, output heart rate roughly doubling in the severe-ARDS cells) that dwarf the few-percent male--female difference, so no female cell crosses the $10~\%$ or $30~\%$ band on the sex difference alone.

\subsection{PaO$_2$ ceiling: reclassification under a wider bound}\label{sec:s3-pao2-sensitivity}

Within category (b), the exact numerical choice (e.g., PaO$_2$ lower 20 vs 25~mmHg) is a defensible judgment call within the cited literature rather than a unique biological constant. Section~3.8 of the companion technical monograph demonstrates the sensitivity-analysis pattern on the single most contestable bound (PaO$_2$ upper, raised from 600 to 700~mmHg per the alveolar-gas-equation ceiling); the 34 exceedances it starts from are reported in Section~\mainref{sec:results-hyperoxia}{2.4} of the main manuscript, and the reclassification itself is the subject of this subsection. Under this wider physiology-anchored bound, 32 of the 34 runs flagged \texttt{implausible} by the locked 600~mmHg PaO$_2$ ceiling become physiologically clean (31 at FiO$_2 \geq 0.95$; 21 on the female and 11 on the male reference body); the remaining two are healthy-female runs at maximal minute ventilation (Vt 800~mL $\times$ RR 20 = 16~L/min) whose PaO$_2$ suffers a genuine numerical runaway within the window --- stable at $\approx$480~mmHg for the first 60~s, then climbing to a physically impossible plateau above the 760~mmHg sea-level barometric pressure --- so they correctly stay implausible under either bound (the same high-minute-ventilation solver instability seen at extended windows, Section~\ref{sec:s9-guidance}). One limit of the wider bound is worth stating, since it bears on how the side-analysis should be read. 700~mmHg is a \emph{flat} ceiling derived at FiO$_2$ = 1.0, whereas the alveolar-gas-equation value scales with each run's own inspired fraction and PaCO$_2$. Of the 32 runs it reclassifies, 31 sit at FiO$_2 \geq 0.95$, where the flat ceiling is close to the run-specific one; the exception is a single healthy-female run at FiO$_2$ = 0.737, whose 679~mmHg steady-state mean stands well above the $0.737 \times (760 - 47) - 16.2 / 0.8 \approx 505$~mmHg its own inspired fraction and PaCO$_2$ permit. An FiO$_2$-aware ceiling would keep that run flagged. The flat 700~mmHg bound is therefore a deliberately permissive stress test of the locked classifier, not a better classifier. No locked threshold has been adjusted post-hoc; the only preregistration amendment (2026-04-22-b, flatline tolerance 1~\% $\rightarrow$ 0.1~\%) was made before the sweep launched.

\section{ARDS impairment-fraction instability zone}\label{sec:s5}

Section~\mainref{sec:results-secondary}{2.6} of the main manuscript refers to a documented 0.91--0.945 ARDS-impairment-fraction band in which Pulse's respiratory solver becomes unstable. This supplement section provides the full characterisation: how Pulse translates the single impairment-fraction scalar into respiratory-mechanics and gas-exchange changes, how the band was identified, how the present atlas's continuous-severity micro-sweep refines its edges, and how the divergence rate within the band stratifies by sex under midrange Morris inputs.

\subsection{Berlin-band calibration of the scenario impairment fractions}\label{sec:s5-berlin}

Pulse encodes ARDS as an abstract per-lung \texttt{Severity} scalar rather than by the clinical PaO$_2$/FiO$_2$ (P/F) definition, so the four scenario impairment fractions were assigned by \emph{forward calibration}: Pulse was run across severity at a fixed reference ventilation setting and the impairment fractions were chosen so that the resulting arterial P/F falls near the centres of the Berlin Definition~\cite{ards2012} mild and moderate bands and just inside the upper boundary of the severe band, which is open-ended below and therefore has no centre (mild 200--300, moderate 100--200, severe $\leq 100$~mmHg, at PEEP $\geq 5$~cmH$_2$O). This calibration was performed in the companion translation project's ARDS-calibration experiment (E6; full log in the companion technical monograph, Appendix~B.3) on the adult male reference body under VC-AC, tidal volume 6~mL/kg actual body weight (403.8~mL), PEEP 10~cmH$_2$O, FiO$_2$ 0.4. Table~\ref{tab:s5-berlin-calib} reports the result: impairment 0.26, 0.60, and 0.95 land at 256, 150, and 96~mmHg --- near the mild and moderate band centres, and just inside the ceiling of the severe band, which is open-ended below and therefore has no centre.

This calibration has since been reproduced inside the present repository (2026-07-26, 40 runs on the atlas's own engine image), so a headline result no longer depends on an external project. Evaluated over the companion's measurement window, the replication reproduces the published anchors to within 0.6~\% at the three scenario fractions (0.26 $\to$ 256.5, 0.60 $\to$ 150.5, 0.95 $\to$ 96.6~mmHg) and to within 2~\% across all five fractions compared. The same runs re-derive the instability band independently: impairment fractions 0.910, 0.920, 0.930, 0.940 and 0.945 all failed to complete, while 0.900 and 0.950 completed.

\subsection{Severity-to-physiology mapping in the Pulse respiratory model}\label{sec:s5-mapping}

Pulse represents ARDS as a single \texttt{Severity} scalar in $[0, 1]$ per lung compartment (the \texttt{AcuteRespiratoryDistressSyndrome} condition); the atlas sets it bilaterally and symmetrically to the scenario impairment fraction (left lung = right lung). That one scalar is fed independently into four hard-coded piecewise-linear interpolation curves in the respiratory-model source (\texttt{RespiratoryModel.cpp}: \texttt{UpdateResistances}, \texttt{UpdateAlveolarCompliances}, \texttt{UpdateDiffusion}, and \texttt{UpdatePulmonaryShunt}), each calibrated against the adult standard patient; there is no independent handle on resistance, compliance, or diffusion. The four curves act in concert: at S4 (impairment 0.95) Pulse multiplies distal alveolar resistance by $\approx 22\times$, cuts alveolar compliance to $\approx 45\,\%$ of baseline, collapses the alveolar diffusion surface area to $\approx 7.5\,\%$ of baseline, and opens the intrapulmonary shunt. The diffusion-surface collapse together with the widened shunt is the proximate driver of the severe arterial hypoxemia behind the input-HR / output-HR decoupling reported in main-manuscript Section~\mainref{sec:results-secondary}{2.6}. The shunt row is the least direct of the four: its damage term is non-monotonic across severity and is combined by a \texttt{MIN} operation with an alveolar-recruitment term (a function of actual alveolar volume, which PEEP raises) and a left/right calibration factor, so the effective shunt resistance is not simply the tabulated damage multiplier. Severity additionally feeds the respiratory driver pressure, lung volumes, and the inspiratory:expiratory ratio through smaller restrictive terms. The four curves are themselves smooth; the 0.91--0.945 solver-instability band characterised below is an emergent numerical property of the combined stiff respiratory state, not a discontinuity in any single curve.

\textbf{Forward-looking limitation (pediatric and neonatal).} Both the baseline resistance, compliance, and diffusion-surface values \emph{and} the severity-to-effect curves above are tuned to the adult standard patient, and the single-scalar design forbids retuning the three properties independently. Carrying these settings to pediatric mechanics --- where compliance per kilogram, airway resistance, and absolute gas-exchange surface differ substantially --- would mis-scale the absolute consequences, which is one reason Pulse hard-gates age $\geq 18$ at patient setup. Separately, this absence was re-verified directly: a case-insensitive search of the complete Pulse engine tree at \texttt{REL\_4\_3\_2} (commit \texttt{e8a3649}) --- physiologically identical to the 4.3.1 pin, the only delta being seven build-system files with no respiratory-source change --- returns zero occurrences of \texttt{surfactant}, and zero of the terms under which such a model would otherwise surface (surface tension, Laplace, derecruitment, alveolar stability, opening/closing pressure); no mention appears in the Pulse engineering forum or the public respiratory methodology. What Pulse reproduces are the macroscopic behaviours surfactant governs, without the underlying surface-tension mechanism: the respiratory model mimics the expected pressure--volume hysteresis of the healthy lung (per Pulse's own respiratory-methodology validation) through a phenomenological compliance curve, and represents alveolar recruitment as an empirical acinar-volume-to-shunt-fraction mapping calibrated to PEEP data rather than as surfactant-governed alveolar stability. Lung compliance is thus a lumped circuit element scaled by disease multipliers with no surface-tension term; neonatal respiratory distress syndrome --- fundamentally a surfactant-deficiency disease, including its response to surfactant instillation --- is therefore not representable in the current lumped-parameter alveolar-compliance model. These are pointers for native pediatric-engine work rather than limitations of the present adult atlas.

\subsection{Provenance of the documented band}\label{sec:s5-prov}

Three convergent boundary probes within the companion technical work (same Pulse v4.3.1 engine, same male reference body, non-overlapping impairment-fraction grids, run-by-run independent execution) characterise the same numerical-bifurcation range in the respiratory solver. A 28-run impairment sweep at ARDSnet-protective ventilation found fractions 0.92 and 0.94 failing to stabilise. A 10-run high-resolution boundary probe at 0.90 / 0.91 / 0.915 / 0.92 / 0.925 / 0.93 / 0.935 / 0.94 / 0.945 / 0.95 narrowed the failure range to 0.91--0.945 inclusive (eight consecutive failures, with 0.90 and 0.95 passing on either side). A complementary 9-run calibration refinement at 0.93--0.97 in 0.005 steps (four failures across 0.93--0.945, five passes across 0.95--0.97) confirmed the upper edge. The three probes are within the same companion project rather than independent third-party replications; the convergence is evidence of reproducibility under the same engine version and reference body, not of generalisation across labs. Pulse's own documentation does not flag this zone.

\subsection{Two divergence regimes}\label{sec:s5-regimes}

Together with Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript, the data distinguish two operationally separate divergence regimes. The input-driven regime (the main-manuscript headline) concentrates on the male reference body at low HR, low SBP, high RR, and FiO$_2$ = 1.0 --- the hypotensive--tachypneic--fully-oxygenated input pattern --- and produces divergence exclusively on the male reference body: 20 events across the four male cells versus zero across the 640 runs on the female reference body. The severity-driven regime (this continuous-severity sweep) affects both sexes at midrange inputs once impairment fraction enters [0.879, 0.945], with female divergence concentrated in the lower part of the band and male divergence in the upper. Both regimes are real solver-stability findings; the sex effect on H3 (Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript) is therefore best stated as ``regime-dependent'' rather than ``all-male'', and the practical guidance to downstream users is to avoid the 0.879--0.945 impairment-fraction band when stable steady-state outputs are required.

\subsection{Relative ventilation-dose mismatch between the two reference bodies}\label{sec:s6-dose}

The sweep varies tidal volume in absolute millilitres, over the same 300--800~mL range on both reference bodies. A matched input vector therefore delivers a matched \emph{absolute} tidal volume, but not a matched dose per unit of body size. Two normalisations make the mismatch explicit.

Per kilogram of \emph{actual} body weight (67.3 and 56.2~kg), the swept range corresponds to 4.5--11.9~mL\,kg$^{-1}$ on the male reference body and 5.3--14.2~mL\,kg$^{-1}$ on the female. At every matched setting the female body therefore receives 19.8~\% more tidal volume per kilogram.

Per kilogram of \emph{ideal} body weight the gap is larger. Pulse computes ideal body weight at patient setup from a sex-specific Devine relation --- $50.0 + 2.3\,(\text{height in inches} - 60)$~kg for a male body and $45.5 + 2.3\,(\text{height in inches} - 60)$~kg for a female body --- giving 74.99~kg at 180~cm and 55.10~kg at 163~cm. The swept range then corresponds to 4.0--10.7~mL\,kg$^{-1}$ of ideal body weight on the male body and 5.4--14.5~mL\,kg$^{-1}$ on the female, a 36.1~\% relative excess on the female body.

This is an interpretive constraint, not a defect in the sweep: sweeping tidal volume in absolute units is what keeps the Morris design identical across all eight cells, which is the property the balanced factorial rests on. It does mean, however, that a male--female contrast on any gas-exchange output combines three contributions the design does not separate --- the sex flag, body morphology, and ventilatory dose. PaCO$_2$ is where this matters most, because alveolar ventilation is its dominant determinant, and PaCO$_2$ carries three of the five surviving Wasserstein distances reported in Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript. Separating the three would require a design in which both bodies retain the same grid points \emph{and} receive matched relative ventilation doses; matching grid points alone does not suffice.

\section{VC-AC trigger-lockout full protocol}\label{sec:s8}

Section~\mainref{sec:results-vcac}{2.3} of the main manuscript reports that setting Pulse's \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerPressure} to $-2$~cmH$_2$O on the \texttt{SEMechanicalVentilatorVolumeControl} action collapses the median delivered-tidal-volume coefficient of variation from 0.351 to 0.016 across 64 paired reruns, with 78.1~\% of locked-trigger reruns dropping below the 0.05 steady-state threshold. This supplement section describes the experimental protocol that established the corrective intervention and shows the per-breath waveform evidence that the dyssynchrony signal is real (not a sampling artefact) and that the intervention removes it.

\subsection{Protocol design}\label{sec:s8-protocol}

The 64 paired reruns sampled all eight (scenario, sex) cells, with most coverage concentrated on the healthy-S1 and mild-ARDS-S2 cells where the dyssynchrony signal is densest (Table~1 of the main manuscript). For each rerun, the locked configuration was reproduced exactly from the original sweep, with one and only one modification: the \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerPressure} field on the VC-AC action was set to $-2$~cmH$_2$O (the trigger-pressure constant declared in \texttt{scripts/run\_s014\_trigger\_lockout.py} and regex-extracted into ledger anchor \texttt{T1.06d.s14\_trigger\_pressure}). All other settings --- Morris input vector, simulation duration, scenario, patient reference body --- remained identical. The intervention is therefore a clean one-parameter manipulation; any change in the steady-state Vt distribution is causally attributable to the trigger-pressure setting.

\subsection{Native 50 Hz waveform evidence (Figure~\ref{fig:s8-vt})}\label{sec:s8-waveform}

Pulse's standard CSV output exposes the delivered tidal volume as a breath-integrated time series sampled at 5-second intervals in the atlas-aggregated parquet. For waveform-level inspection of the trigger dyssynchrony, the native Pulse output (\texttt{output\_pulse\_native.csv} per run) provides the same signal at a 0.02-second sampling interval (50~Hz). At adult respiratory rates of approximately 0.25~Hz (one breath every four seconds), 50~Hz sampling is a 200-fold oversample of the breath-rate signal, well above the Nyquist criterion of approximately $2 \times 0.25 = 0.5$~Hz. The per-breath delivered tidal volume shown in Figure~\ref{fig:s8-vt} is therefore the directly observable breath-by-breath dynamic of the ventilator coupling, not an aliasing artefact.

\subsection{Mechanism and downstream consequence}\label{sec:s8-mech}

The default \texttt{SEMechanicalVentilatorVolumeControl} action in \texttt{AssistedControl} mode enables Pulse's respiratory-model-driven patient trigger when no explicit \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerPressure} or \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerFlow} is supplied (\texttt{SEMechanicalVentilatorVolumeControl.cpp} lines 120--128 in the upstream Pulse source). The spontaneous-drive trigger couples breath delivery to the modelled patient respiratory drive and so produces effort-dependent per-breath tidal-volume variance consistent with patient-ventilator asynchrony~\cite{figueroacasas2016} --- physiologically the more faithful regime --- but it breaks the steady-state-mean assumption the locked atlas classifier relies on. Setting the trigger pressure explicitly to $-2$~cmH$_2$O disables the spontaneous-trigger coupling, restoring metronomic breath delivery and dropping the Vt CV by a factor of approximately twenty in the median paired comparison. This is best read as a data-generation / exploration lever, not a clinical recommendation: locking the trigger to a fixed threshold leaves simulated inspiratory efforts below the threshold unrewarded, corresponding in a real patient to a shift from patient-responsive assist-control toward controlled ventilation --- clinically undesirable, and visible only with specialised monitoring such as esophageal pressure or diaphragm electrical activity. Downstream users running atlas-style VC-AC sensitivity sweeps that require deterministic tidal volume can therefore set the trigger pressure explicitly on every action, with that caveat in mind. The patient-ventilator dyssynchrony phenomenon itself is well documented in the clinical literature for real ICU patients on lower tidal-volume volume-control ventilation~\cite{figueroacasas2016}; the present atlas's contribution on this front is the Pulse-engine manifestation of that phenomenon and the $-2$~cmH$_2$O trigger lever for deterministic synthetic-data generation, not the clinical discovery of dyssynchrony itself.

\section{Hyperoxia 300-second convergence full protocol}\label{sec:s9}

Section~\mainref{sec:results-hyperoxia}{2.4} of the main manuscript reports that the locked 120-second simulation window is insufficient for arterial-oxygen-partial-pressure (PaO$_2$) convergence at FiO$_2$ = 1.0 in the healthy S1 cohort: 41 of 44 S1 reruns (93.2~\%) converged to a stable plateau under a 300-second rerun, with a converged-subset median PaO$_2$ of 643~mmHg (the per-run plateau median over all 41 drift-converged runs), sitting approximately 20~mmHg below the simple alveolar-gas-equation prediction at sea level of $\approx$~663~mmHg~\cite{west2020respiratory}. This supplement section provides the kinetic anchor for that claim and visualises the convergence trajectories.

\subsection{Late-phase convergence trajectories (Figure~\ref{fig:s9-pao2})}\label{sec:s9-trajectories}

Each run retains the unchanged 120-second ventilator-settling phase that precedes recording, so one rerun spans 420~s of Pulse-internal time in total; all reported times and figures use recorded simulation time, $t = 0$--$300$~s. \subsection{Practical guidance}\label{sec:s9-guidance}

The kinetic anchor (95~\% of the late-plateau gain reached by $t = 180$~s of recorded simulation on the 38 physiological-converged runs) gives an objective threshold for downstream Pulse users running hyperoxia simulations: recording at least 300 seconds of simulation and computing the steady-state mean over the final 60 seconds of that window ($t = 240$--$300$~s) puts the PaO$_2$ estimate within approximately 5~\% of the asymptote on the healthy cohort. The 120-second window of the locked atlas classifier was appropriate for the cardiovascular and ventilation channels (HR, MAP, Vt, PaCO$_2$ all settle in well under 120 seconds at FiO$_2$ = 1.0) but is the wrong window for PaO$_2$ at the inspired-oxygen boundary; the locked atlas classifier reports the relevant runs as \texttt{implausible} (PaO$_2$ above the 600~mmHg bound) under exactly the conditions where the slow late-phase relaxation has not yet completed.

A separate solver-instability mode emerges only at extended windows on healthy lungs at FiO$_2$ = 1.0: in the S021 cohort, 6 of 44 healthy-S1 reruns (14~\%) developed unphysical PaO$_2$ plateaus above 700~mmHg (four stabilising near 1{,}400~mmHg, above the sea-level barometric pressure of 760~mmHg), all six at the single tidal-volume level Vt~=~633.3~mL --- 6 of the 20 S1 reruns at that level, none of the 24 at the other three levels, and 0 of 8 at the highest level Vt~=~800~mL. Minute ventilation does not separate them: five of the six carry RR~=~20 ($\dot{V}_E \approx 12.7$~L/min, roughly twice the physiological norm of 6--8~L/min) but three further runs at that same $\dot{V}_E$ do not run away, and the sixth carries RR~=~16 at PEEP~=~0 ($\dot{V}_E \approx 10.1$~L/min), so no input-side ventilation threshold in this cohort separates affected from unaffected runs. What does separate them is the reference body: across the five input-identical male/female pairs at Vt~=~633.3~mL with RR~$\geq$~16, the female body ran away in 5 of 5 and the male in 1 of 5, and the single male case plateaus at 779~mmHg against 1{,}169--1{,}408~mmHg across the five female cases. This is the opposite direction to the all-male input-extreme divergence cluster of Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript and a further instance of the regime-dependence established in \S\ref{sec:s5-cont}; it is consistent with the systematically higher PaO$_2$ the female body reaches at identical inputs (mean 686 versus 663~mmHg in the locked 120-second window across the four RR~=~20 pairs) and with the $+21.3$~mmHg sex effect on S1 PaO$_2$ measured by the controlled 2$\times$2 sex$\times$mass design of S011. That design was run at midrange inputs and FiO$_2$ = 0.6, however, so the present cohort cannot separate sex from the 11.1~kg body-mass difference in this regime, and no mechanism is claimed. Three of these six stay below the drift threshold and are therefore counted among the 41 drift-converged runs, while the other three are the only three non-converged S1 reruns; the 44 S1 reruns thus partition into 38 physiological-converged, 3 runaway-converged, and 3 runaway-non-converged, and all six runaways are excluded from the physiological median trajectory of Figure~\ref{fig:s9-pao2}. The locked 120-second atlas window terminates these simulations before the runaway emerges; downstream users running extended-window hyperoxia simulations should accordingly screen each run's PaO$_2$ trajectory individually rather than rely on an input-side ventilation threshold.

\subsection{ARDS shunt depression and clean-regime oxygenation}\label{sec:s9-shunt}

\section{Sex-stratified blood-volume mechanism: absolute and weight-normalized framings}\label{sec:s10}

Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript reports that all 20 of 1{,}280 input-extreme-regime divergences fall on the male reference body, that Pulse's allometric blood-volume formula yields 4{,}802.63~mL for the 67.3~kg male body versus 3{,}996.09~mL for the 56.2~kg female body (a 16.8~\% absolute-mL sex gap), and that reducing the male reference body's \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline} toward its 4{,}082.24~mL setup-time floor eliminates the divergence cluster (20 / 20 at the default $\rightarrow$ 0 / 20 at both the 4{,}400 and 4{,}082.24~mL reductions). This supplement section disentangles the absolute-volume framing from the weight-normalized framing, because the two answer different methodological questions and a reviewer-relevant confounder lives between them.

\subsection{Absolute volumes (the framing used in the main manuscript)}\label{sec:s10-abs}

The Pulse allometric formula~\cite{pulsepatientmethod} is $\text{BV} = 65.6 \cdot \text{weight}_{\text{kg}}^{1.02}$~mL. On the two reference patient bodies this yields:

\begin{itemize}
\item Male (67.3~kg): $\text{BV} = 65.6 \cdot 67.3^{1.02} = 4{,}802.63$~mL.
\item Female (56.2~kg): $\text{BV} = 65.6 \cdot 56.2^{1.02} = 3{,}996.09$~mL.
\item Absolute-mL difference: $4{,}802.63 - 3{,}996.09 = 806.54$~mL, or 16.8~\% of the male default.
\end{itemize}

The Pulse setup-time floor for the male body sits at 4{,}082.24~mL --- computed as the allometric blood volume $\times$ 0.85 in \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp}, a 15~\% reduction below which Pulse requires hypovolemia to be modelled by an explicit condition --- 720.39~mL below the male default and 86.15~mL above the female default. The male body cannot be made BV-equivalent to the female body by a one-way \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline} transplant: the female default sits below the male floor.

The same gate has an upper arm. \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp} also rejects any baseline above the allometric value $\times$ 1.15, with an \texttt{[ERROR]} and exit, exactly as it rejects one below the floor. For the published 56.2~kg female reference body that ceiling is 4{,}595.50~mL, which ends 207.13~mL below the 4{,}802.63~mL male default at which the divergence cluster appears --- so the reciprocal transplant is refused as loudly as the forward one, and neither reference body can be moved to the other's default. This is a constraint of that reference body's mass-dependent blood-volume range, not a general constraint on female-configured bodies: a female-configured body at the male body's 67.3~kg computes the same 4{,}802.63~mL default and the same admissible range, because Pulse's blood-volume relation is not sex-branched.

\subsection{Weight-normalized volumes (the confounder-controlled framing)}\label{sec:s10-norm}

Comparing absolute blood volumes across patients of different mass conflates intrinsic sex differences with baseline morphological differences. Dividing each absolute BV by the corresponding reference body weight yields the weight-normalized blood-volume index (mL/kg), which controls for the morphological component:

\begin{itemize}
\item Male: $4{,}802.63 / 67.3 = 71.4$~mL/kg.
\item Female: $3{,}996.09 / 56.2 = 71.1$~mL/kg.
\item Weight-normalized difference: $0.3$~mL/kg, or $\approx 0.4$~\% of the male value.
\end{itemize}

The 16.8~\% absolute-mL sex gap is therefore almost entirely driven by the body-mass difference (67.3 vs 56.2~kg, a 16.5~\% gap) rather than by an intrinsic per-kg sex difference in Pulse's allometric BV calculation. This makes methodological sense: the underlying formula $\text{BV} = 65.6 \cdot \text{weight}_{\text{kg}}^{1.02}$ scales blood volume nearly linearly with weight (the 1.02 exponent is close to 1.0), so weight-normalized BV is approximately constant across body masses.

\subsection{Reviewer-relevant disambiguation}\label{sec:s10-disambig}

The atlas's sex-stratified-divergence finding is best stated in absolute-volume terms because the underlying mechanism --- Pulse's setup-time \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline} floor and the cardiovascular-circuit operating point at that floor --- is parameterised on absolute mL, not on mL/kg. The divergence-rate reduction from 20 / 20 at the default to 0 / 20 at both the 4{,}400 and 4{,}082.24~mL reductions is an absolute-volume effect; it is not what would be predicted from a per-kg physiological sex difference (because there is essentially none).

The same reasoning establishes that the male-vs-female divergence asymmetry in the input-extreme regime is not a claim about per-kg blood-volume physiology in real patients --- it is a claim about how Pulse's internal solver responds to absolute-BV values near its setup-time floor on the male reference body. Pooling male and female Pulse-generated synthetic cohorts in machine-learning pipelines without flag-stratified filtering still introduces a spurious sex covariate at the male reference body's divergence boundary (Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript), but downstream users adapting the result to clinical inference should not extrapolate to a per-kg sex-physiology claim that the data does not support.

\section{Engine-side infrastructure recommendations}\label{sec:s11}

The Discussion of the main manuscript closes with a forward-looking note on engine-side infrastructure suggestions for the Pulse maintainers. This supplement section itemises those suggestions as a short numbered list, derived from operating the engine at scale during the 1{,}280-run sweep and the surrounding follow-up experiments. The list is framed as observations from atlas-user experience, not as physiological prescriptions: every item targets tooling, documentation, or validation infrastructure, and each is anchored in a specific experiment from this work. None of the items below recommends changes to Pulse's underlying lumped-parameter physiology, which is the domain of the Kitware biomedical-engineering team.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Structured input-rejection events.} Pulse currently surfaces input clamping (e.g.\ SBP $>$ 120, DBP/SBP ratio out of range, HR or RR outside the initialisation band) as \texttt{[ERROR]}-prefixed log lines that downstream tools must regex-parse. A small structured-event API --- one typed record per rejected field, with the offending value, the enforced bound, and the field name --- would let sensitivity-analysis wrappers detect clamping deterministically instead of pattern-matching the regex set documented in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-clamp}.

\item \textbf{Surface the male reference body \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline} floor in user documentation.} Pulse hard-enforces a 4{,}082.24~mL blood-volume floor at setup on the 67.3~kg / 180~cm male reference body (default 4{,}802.63~mL), but this constant is not currently exposed in the public patient-setup documentation. We discovered it empirically while investigating the all-male divergence cluster (Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript); a one-line note in the patient-baseline reference table would have saved the investigation.

\item \textbf{A reference sensitivity-analysis recipe for user-facing operational inputs.} Pulse's repository carries a 2020 \texttt{STUDY\_SENSITIVITY\_ANALYSIS\_1\_0\_0} tag that performs Sobol sensitivity on internal lumped-parameter circuit values (cardiovascular and respiratory resistances and compliances) for calibration; this is a different parameter class from the user-facing operational inputs (HR, SBP, DBP, RR, Vt, FiO$_2$, PEEP) that sensitivity-conscious downstream users typically need to sweep. A companion recipe --- Morris or Sobol over operational inputs, shipped alongside the existing study --- would give the Pulse community a maintained reference implementation. The atlas's sampler and runner modules are an externally-developed prototype; a maintainer-blessed version would be more durable.

\item \textbf{A setup-time warning for the ARDS impairment-fraction instability zone ($\sim$0.91--0.945).} This instability zone is documented in our preregistration limitations table (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s2} row 4) and corroborated by three independent boundary probes (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5-prov}), but Pulse does not currently emit a setup-time warning when an ARDS action lands in the zone. A single console notice --- ``impairment fraction in reported instability band, results may not be reliable'' --- would prevent downstream users from collecting unusable data without blocking legitimate edge-case research.

\item \textbf{Per-channel steady-state convergence diagnostic in CSV output.} The transient hyperoxia regime characterised in Section~\mainref{sec:results-hyperoxia}{2.4} of the main manuscript (PaO$_2$ still rising at the end of the locked 120-second window at FiO$_2$ = 1.0, with full convergence at $\sim$300~s) suggests that an optional per-output ``drift over final 60~s'' column appended to result CSVs would let users detect non-steady-state runs without writing their own post-processing. The atlas's steady-state classifier already implements exactly this calculation (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-drift}); promoting the same diagnostic upstream would generalise it.

\item \textbf{Field-attributed setup-error messages.} Current setup-time blood-pressure and pulse-pressure-ratio rejection messages require some domain knowledge to map back to the offending input field. A one-token prefix indicating which input was rejected (\texttt{[ERROR][SystolicArterialPressure]} rather than the unprefixed text) would let downstream users and continuous-integration pipelines route the message without reading it.
\end{enumerate}

These suggestions are deliberately infrastructure-level: none of them touches Pulse's underlying physiology, ordinary-differential-equation topology, or lumped-parameter assumptions, all of which lie outside the scope of what an external operational-reliability study can credibly prescribe. We share them in the spirit of an open contribution to a shared community tool --- the atlas itself is offered to the Pulse community in exactly the same spirit, and the present paper would have been substantially easier to produce had each of items 1--6 already been in place.

\section{Reproducibility recipe}\label{sec:s12}

The atlas is designed for bit-exact reproduction by an independent investigator. This section documents the minimal end-to-end sequence required to regenerate the canonical 1{,}280-run sweep, the audit ledger, and the computed quantitative claims in the main manuscript and this supplement. The code and the audit-ledger script are released under the Apache-2.0 license at \url{https://github.com/santinon/pulse_reliability_atlas}; the deposited data carry the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence (CC-BY-4.0), which is the conventional choice for a data archive.

\subsection{Environment and engine pinning}\label{sec:s12-env}

The atlas pins Pulse v4.3.1 inside a single Docker image (\texttt{pulse-atlas-engine:latest}); the image tag is fixed for the entire 1{,}280-run sweep so that every simulation runs against bit-identical engine binaries. The host environment is Python 3.12+ managed via \texttt{uv} for deterministic dependency resolution. The canonical hardware envelope is a laptop-class machine (MSI laptop; Intel i5-11400H, 6 physical / 12 logical cores; 8~GB RAM; Intel UHD Graphics integrated, plus an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Laptop GPU (4~GB); Windows 11 Home, version 25H2). Pulse is a CPU-only physiology simulator and neither graphics device is used by the atlas; the discrete GPU is reported here only for completeness so a reader matching the build does not infer CUDA dependence. The parallel-Docker throughput at this envelope is two to three concurrent containers (verified empirically; the canonical sweep was executed with three parallel workers, lowered to two mid-sweep to control thermal load on the laptop chassis).

Executing the atlas is a substantial computation. The full 1{,}280-run sweep was carried out end-to-end three times: twice on the reference machine described above --- the canonical sweep, and a later re-execution logged at 23~h~01~m elapsed, inclusive of idle intervals across resume points --- and once on the independent host documented in \S\ref{sec:s12-independent}, whose elapsed time, by coincidence, also totalled 23~h~01~m. Aggregate Pulse container time is approximately 27~h per sweep on the reference machine and 46~h on the slower independent host.

\subsection{Three-layer bit-exact verification}\label{sec:s12-verify}

Bit-exact reproduction of the canonical sweep output is verified at three independent layers, each checked against its own committed artefact. Layers 1 and 3 can be re-executed from a fresh clone without re-running the sweep; layer 2, by construction, requires re-executing it:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Canonical Morris sample.} The 1{,}280-row Morris sample is regenerated deterministically from the SALib sampler with \texttt{trajectories = 20, levels = 4, seed = 42}; the regenerated sample matches the committed \texttt{data/runs/full\_sweep/morris\_sample.parquet} byte-for-byte.
\item \textbf{Per-cell aggregate parquets.} The 8 per-cell aggregated parquets (\texttt{data/runs/full\_sweep/<scenario>\_<sex>/aggregated.parquet}) reproduce identically when the full sweep is re-executed against the pinned Docker image on the canonical compute envelope.
\item \textbf{Derived statistical artefacts.} The Morris $\mu^{*}$ indices, bootstrap confidence intervals, Wasserstein distances, Spearman correlations, and BH-FDR survivors all regenerate bit-equivalently from the canonical aggregated parquets; this is what the audit ledger (\texttt{scripts/audit\_claims.py}) re-derives at every run.
\end{enumerate}

The audit ledger is the primary reproducibility instrument: a green ledger run confirms that the computed quantitative claims in the main manuscript and supplement are consistent with the canonical artefacts. The ledger reports a target of 382 anchors; on the authors' full data tree all 382 are evaluated (382/382 PASS), whereas on a public clone one anchor (T1.06u, the S021 time-to-95\%-plateau, which requires the heavy raw per-run trajectories that are gitignored) is automatically skipped, giving 381/382 PASS with one explicitly-reported SKIPPED. A failed claim is a structured triage item (Type A transcription drift, Type B orphaned number, Type C derivation mismatch) with a precise file:line pointer for forensic tracing.

\subsection{Independent reproduction on separate hardware}\label{sec:s12-independent}

Beyond the three-layer re-derivation above --- which regenerates the analysis from the committed canonical artefacts --- the end-to-end pipeline was independently re-executed from scratch by a co-author (Y.M.) on a second x86\_64 host --- an AMD Ryzen~5 CPU running Linux under WSL2 --- distinct from the canonical Intel x86\_64 / Windows reference environment.

First, the full 1{,}280-run Morris sweep was re-run there (two parallel Docker workers; wall time 23~h~01~m). All five run-level flags matched on 1{,}280/1{,}280 runs, every continuous steady-state value was bit-identical, and only three derived H4 statistics differed at $\sim 10^{-14}$ floating-point round-off, with an identical Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate survivor set.

Second, the 22-run S1\_M pilot was re-run on the same host with per-run output capture. Its aggregate was bit-identical to the canonical pilot ($\max |\Delta| = 0$ across all six channel means) with 100\,\% five-flag concordance on 22/22 runs.

Both reproductions are archived under \texttt{audits/}: \texttt{reproduction\_2026-06-07/} carries the reproduced full-sweep aggregate, the derived analysis outputs, and a numerical comparison report (\texttt{reproduction\_check\_2026-06-07.md}); \texttt{reproduction\_thinkpad\_2026-06-10/} carries a provenance README (host, Docker image tag and digest, pinned Pulse v4.3.1, exact commands), the 20 per-run output CSVs, and the comparison tables.

\subsection{Reproducibility scope and limits}\label{sec:s12-scope}

The verification and the independent reproductions above span the Intel x86\_64 reference environment and a second, AMD x86\_64 host (Linux/WSL2), all bit-identical --- empirical evidence that bit-identity holds across CPU vendor and host operating system within the x86\_64 family. Cross-\emph{architecture} reproduction was probed but \emph{not} closed. The 22-run S1\_M pilot was additionally re-executed on co-author Y.M.'s Apple Silicon host (MacBook Air, Apple~M2, ARM64; macOS~26.5.1; archived under \texttt{audits/reproduction\_macos\_2026-06-13/}), but this does \emph{not} constitute a native-ARM64 test: the pinned base image \texttt{kitware/pulse:4.3.1} is published for \texttt{linux/amd64} only, so on Apple Silicon the engine binary executes as \emph{emulated} x86\_64 under Docker Desktop's virtualization layer (the container reports \texttt{x86\_64}, with an explicit \texttt{amd64}-vs-\texttt{arm64} platform-mismatch warning). Under this emulated execution the pilot reproduced the canonical aggregate to $\max|\Delta| = 2.9\times10^{-9}$ across all six channel means with 100\,\% five-flag concordance on 22/22 runs --- near machine precision, and notably \emph{not} bit-identical (the native x86\_64 reproductions above agree to $\max|\Delta| = 0$); the residual is attributable to the x86$\rightarrow$ARM emulation layer's floating-point handling rather than to a natively compiled ARM64 build. Genuine native-ARM64 reproduction would require compiling Pulse~v4.3.1 from source for \texttt{linux/arm64} (no prebuilt ARM64 image exists) and therefore remains future work; the present Apple Silicon run establishes only that the atlas pipeline executes end-to-end on that platform and that emulated x86\_64 execution preserves the canonical outputs to within $\sim 10^{-9}$. Bit-identity within the same architecture family is expected given Pulse's fixed 20~ms ODE timestep and standard math-library usage, and is now empirically supported across two x86\_64 CPU vendors; cross-architecture bit-identity is plausible but not guaranteed without explicit verification. Image immutability is currently anchored at the Docker tag level (\texttt{pulse-atlas-engine:latest} plus the pinned Pulse v4.3.1 Git tag); for archival re-execution five or more years from now, cryptographic digest pinning (\texttt{@sha256:...}) of the Docker image is the more robust mechanism and is recommended for any such future re-execution attempt.

\section{SHAP cross-verification of the Morris sensitivity rankings}\label{sec:s13}

The locked \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flag fires on 530 of 1{,}280 runs across the full sweep; Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s4} decomposes that 530 into the three contributing sub-modes (SpO$_2$-saturation pinning, VC-AC tidal-volume dyssynchrony, and a residual dominated by severe-ARDS slow convergence and FiO$_2$ = 1.0 transient hyperoxia). This section adds a complementary cross-verification: an independent machine-learning analysis recovers the same input-importance rankings that the preregistered Morris elementary-effects design establishes, by a different statistical route and on a different subset of the data.

\subsection{Methodology: epistemological scope}\label{sec:s13-scope}

The Morris elementary-effects design is interventional: each trajectory perturbs one input at a time along a fixed grid, so the resulting $\mu^{*}$ ranking is a causal-mechanistic statement about how Pulse responds to deliberate perturbations of each input. By contrast, a SHAP analysis~\cite{lundberg2017} on a tree-based reference body model trained on the observational distribution of Morris outputs is a fundamentally different statistical object: it summarises how a black-box predictor associates input features with output predictions, on the data distribution as actually observed. The two methods answer different questions, and the primary analysis in this work is the interventional Morris design --- the SHAP analysis is included only as a secondary cross-verification.

\subsection{Cross-verification result on the clean subset}\label{sec:s13-result}

A supplementary RandomForest regressor + SHAP feature-importance ranking was computed on the $n = 663$ clean-subset rows (the runs with no \texttt{clamped}, \texttt{diverged}, \texttt{implausible}, or \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flag): one RandomForest per output, trained on the pooled clean subset with \texttt{sex} and \texttt{scenario} as additional features. SHAP values were obtained with \texttt{TreeExplainer}, which computes them exactly for tree ensembles~\cite{lundberg2020tree} rather than by sampling approximation. Per-(scenario, sex) cell rankings were then obtained by partitioning this global model's SHAP values by cell and re-ranking---not by fitting separate per-cell models, whose per-cell $n$ (42--144 clean runs) is too small for stable training. The SHAP-top-3 input ranking was then compared against the Morris-$\mu^{*}$-top-3 ranking on the same cells. Three summary statistics characterise the agreement:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{17 of 18 pooled (output $\times$ top-3) cases agree} at the full 3-of-3 ranking level (ledger anchor \texttt{T2.s023.pooled}).
\item \textbf{23 of 48 per-cell (scenario $\times$ sex $\times$ output) cases agree at the full 3-of-3 level} (ledger anchor \texttt{T2.s023.fullmatch}).
\item \textbf{92~\% of per-cell comparisons agree at $\geq$ 2-of-3 level} (44 of 48; ledger anchor \texttt{T2.s023.ge2\_pct}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Interpretation}\label{sec:s13-interp}

The strong pooled-level agreement (17 of 18) is the central cross-verification: a SHAP analysis on the clean-subset data distribution recovers the same dominant inputs that the interventional Morris design identifies, on six independent outputs. The lower per-cell full-match rate (23 of 48) is expected, because per-cell sample sizes (42--144 clean runs per cell) are smaller and the SHAP ranking depends on the observed input-output distribution within that cell, which is heavily influenced by which Morris trajectories happened to land on clean runs. The 92~\% $\geq$ 2-of-3 agreement at the per-cell level is the appropriate level of comparison: a top-3 ranking is preserved if SHAP recovers two of the three inputs the Morris design ranks first.

The SHAP cross-verification works because the underlying physiology is real, not the other way round. Morris establishes the mechanistic input-importance ranking through a preregistered one-at-a-time perturbation design; SHAP independently recovers the same ranking by a different (data-driven, observational, tree-based) route. The agreement is evidence that the Morris-derived rankings are not artefacts of the elementary-effects design --- they correspond to input dependencies that any reasonable statistical method would identify on the same data.

\section{Compensatory tachycardia: input HR vs output HR under hypoxemia}\label{sec:s15}

Section~\mainref{sec:results-secondary}{2.6} of the main manuscript reports that, under increasing ARDS severity, Pulse's output heart rate decouples from the user-specified input HR baseline as the engine's documented baroreceptor and chemoreceptor reflexes assert themselves against severe arterial hypoxemia. This supplement section provides the per-scenario quantitative breakdown that establishes the decoupling and the operational-interpretation guidance for Morris elementary-effects on input HR.

\subsection{Per-scenario output-HR distributions}\label{sec:s15-distributions}

Aggregated across all clean Morris runs (those passing the four-flag steady-state classifier without firing \texttt{diverged}, \texttt{implausible}, or \texttt{not\_steady\_state}), the output HR distribution by ARDS scenario is shown in Table~\ref{tab:s15-comphr}.

\subsection{Mechanism and Morris-EE interpretation}\label{sec:s15-mechanism}

In S1 (healthy lungs), Pulse settles output HR near a physiological norm of approximately 74~bpm regardless of the user-requested input baseline --- the requested HR is achieved without compensatory engagement because oxygenation is adequate. By S3 (60~\% alveolar impairment) and S4 (95~\% alveolar impairment), the engine's documented baroreceptor and chemoreceptor reflexes drive output HR into the 145--160~bpm range despite identical input sampling, an approximately 70~bpm sympathetic overshoot above the $[50, 110]$~bpm input-range midpoint, whose magnitude reflects the severity of arterial hypoxemia rather than the input baseline. The compensation is strong enough to dominate the explicit input HR baseline. Notably the overshoot is non-monotonic in severity: as Table~\ref{tab:s15-comphr} shows, output HR peaks at S3 (moderate) and eases at S4 (severe), plausibly reflecting incipient cardiovascular decompensation at 95~\% alveolar impairment, where sustained maximal sympathetic drive can no longer be maintained.

The operational consequence for Morris elementary effects on input HR in the ARDS scenarios is that the measured $\mu^{*}$ on output HR captures the coupling between compensation gain and input setting, not the steady-state effect of a resting heart rate. The H1 result that cardiovascular outputs are HR-dominated remains robust; the interpretation, however, is that ``the compensation responds proportionally to the input baseline'' rather than ``the input HR is the steady-state HR.'' Users running Pulse for ARDS device-validation should treat output HR as the engine's reflex response to the active scenario, not as a setpoint imposed by the input.

\section{Output stability across the 120-second measurement window}\label{sec:s16-window}

Two channels are unsettled at the edges of the operational envelope: PaO$_2$ drifts at FiO$_2$ = 1.0 (Section~\ref{sec:s9}) and delivered tidal volume oscillates under the default VC-AC trigger (Section~\ref{sec:s8}). A natural concern is whether these localised instabilities contaminate the other steady-state channels the Morris screen relies on. We audited this directly: for every run in each vulnerable population we computed the drift $|\Delta_{60\mathrm{s}}|$ of each output over the final-60~s window --- estimated by the same preregistered ordinary-least-squares slope $\times$ 60~s that the \texttt{not\_steady\_state} detector uses (Section~\ref{sec:s3-drift}) --- and compared it against the locked per-channel drift thresholds.

In the hyperoxia arm (FiO$_2 \geq 0.95$; 195 runs with valid output), drift stays within threshold on the cardiovascular and carbon-dioxide channels: heart rate exceeds its 5~bpm threshold on only 2.6~\% of runs (median 0.20~bpm), MAP on 0~\% (median 0.05~mmHg), and PaCO$_2$ on 3.6~\% (median 0.58~mmHg). Only oxygenation and tidal-volume delivery are materially affected: PaO$_2$ exceeds its 15~mmHg threshold on 19.5~\% of runs (median 4.3~mmHg, 95th percentile 40.2~mmHg) and delivered Vt on 8.7~\%. In the tidal-volume-oscillation arm (delivered-Vt CV $>$ 0.05; 303 runs) the oscillation likewise does not propagate: heart rate drifts beyond threshold on 2.3~\% of runs, MAP on 0.7~\%, PaO$_2$ on 7.3~\%, and PaCO$_2$ on 2.0~\% --- Pulse buffers the breath-to-breath volume swing upstream of the cardiovascular and gas-exchange loops.

The audit bounds the two disclosed instabilities to exactly the channels that disclose them: the [60, 120]~s steady-state window is valid for HR, MAP, and PaCO$_2$ across the full sweep, and is inadequate only for PaO$_2$ and delivered Vt at the FiO$_2$ = 1.0 and high-oscillation extremes --- precisely the conditions Sections~\ref{sec:s9} and~\ref{sec:s8} already characterise. The two edge-of-envelope findings are thus a bounded, explicitly scoped window-length limitation, not a hidden invalidator of the rest of the atlas. The full per-arm channel breakdown, the dual-vulnerability intersection, and the underlying S012 experiment log are given in the companion technical monograph.
