\section{Pulse engine in context}\label{sec:s1}

The Pulse Physiology Engine~\cite{bray2019} is an actively maintained open-source whole-body human physiology simulator. It represents the body as a network of coupled electrical-circuit analogues (vascular resistances as resistors, vessel and chamber compliances as capacitors, the heart as a time-varying capacitor~\cite{pulsecardiomethod} and the respiratory muscles as a time-varying pressure source), solved as a system of ordinary differential equations at a fixed 20~ms time step. The cardiovascular, respiratory (a 5-compartment lung model: carina, left and right anatomic dead space, left and right alveoli)~\cite{pulserespmethod}, gas-exchange, endocrine, renal and drug sub-models share state at each step, producing a self-consistent whole-body trajectory at about 5--10$\times$ real time, depending on the machine~\cite{pulsefaq}. The C++ codebase has bindings for Java, C\# and Unity~\cite{pulseabout} and an interface for Python~\cite{webb2022vent}, and runs on Windows, Mac, Linux and ARM~\cite{pulseabout}.

Four implementation choices bear directly on how the atlas is read. First, the circuits are built from paths carrying idealised elements (resistances, capacitances, diodes), and the resulting non-linear networks are solved by one generic circuit solver shared across all systems~\cite{pulsecircuit}. Second, the engine advances on a fixed transient-analysis time step of 0.02~s (50~Hz), recalculating every system state at each step~\cite{pulsesystem}; this is the same 20~ms step as above, confirmed by an independent source. Third, execution is deterministic, so one configuration replays to one trajectory, which allows the per-cell curation map to be reproduced rather than merely reported (\S\ref{sec:s12}). Fourth, patients and diseases enter at setup rather than continuously: body dimensions drive allometric baselines (quantities that scale with body size) bounded by hard acceptance limits~\cite{pulsepatientmethod}, and a single ARDS severity scalar on $[0,\,1]$ drives the respiratory modifiers through piecewise-linear functions calibrated at five nodes (0.0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9 and 1.0)~\cite{pulserespmethod}. This five-node calibration is why the main design fixes severity at scenario points rather than sweeping it (\S\ref{sec:s5-mapping}); the continuous-severity micro-sweep of \S\ref{sec:s5-cont} is a separate, targeted experiment.

Pulse was forked in 2017 from the U.S.\ Department of Defense--funded BioGears engine by members of the original BioGears team and is now maintained by Kitware, Inc.\ under the Apache-2.0 license~\cite{pulseabout,pulsefaq}. The two projects have diverged substantially since then: Pulse v4.3.1 models mechanoreceptor feedback and lung recruitment, two features we did not identify in the examined BioGears respiratory or nervous-system implementations or in its respiratory methodology documentation.

The Pulse documentation characterises the engine as using ``a low fidelity clinical approach'' and states it is ``not necessarily for high fidelity predictive purposes''~\cite{pulsesystem}. This self-description frames the atlas's contribution (Section~\mainref{sec:intro}{1} of the main manuscript): a quantitative characterisation of Pulse's operational envelope that helps users distinguish regions meeting the stated screening criteria from those requiring scenario-specific review. \texttt{REL\_4\_3\_2} (released 2025-08-12) differs from \texttt{REL\_4\_3\_1} only in its build system (7 files / +46 / -17 lines: web-WASM dockcross support and a .NET binding tweak); no physiology, validation, patient-setup or solver code changed, so results on v4.3.1 apply identically to v4.3.2. The next release, \texttt{REL\_4\_4\_0} (2026-09-30, after the sweep), changes the physiology. Its release notes~\cite{pulse440} list, among other changes, a distinction between anatomic and physiologic pulmonary shunting, revised respiratory resistance and compliance calculations, refactored respiratory compartments and volume distribution, and the removal of an inflection point in the CO$_2$ saturation calculation at high dissolved CO$_2$; the Eigen library used by the circuit solver moves from version 3.4.0 to 5.0.1. In its source, the patient-setup acceptance limits used here are unchanged, as is the rule that enables the respiratory-model patient trigger by default in VC-AC; the ARDS-severity-to-shunt mapping of \S\ref{sec:s5-mapping} is rewritten, and the cardiovascular and nervous-system model sources also change. The results of this atlas may therefore not carry over unchanged to v4.4.0.

Rows 5--6 were later re-derived within this repository. On 2026-07-26, eight single-purpose probes on the male reference body, run under the atlas's own engine image, tested both sides of each documented bound; both bounds are enforced at patient setup, before any ventilation begins. Heart rates of 49 and 111~bpm and respiratory rates of 7 and 21~bpm were each rejected with an explicit \texttt{[ERROR]} and exit code 1, with no output written, whereas 50, 110, 8 and 20 were accepted and ran to completion. Pulse names the bound in its rejection text (e.g.\ ``Maximum heart rate baseline allowed is 110 bpm''), and the corresponding branches are visible in \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp}. This does not alter the design-time provenance recorded in Table~\ref{tab:limits}, which justified the sampler ranges when they were locked, but it removes any reliance on an external repository for re-verifying the two bounds.

Two earlier assertions from our internal probing in the companion technical work were checked before preregistration was locked and could not be confirmed. (i)~Pulse does not crash at FiO$_2$ = 1.0: Docker probing showed that it accepts FiO$_2$ = 1.0 and produces a stable trajectory (the transient hyperoxia regime is characterised in Section~\mainref{sec:results-hyperoxia}{2.4} of the main manuscript and in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s9}). (ii)~The claim that Pulse has no sex-specific physiology beyond weight and height was withdrawn, but not because of blood volume: \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp} computes the blood-volume baseline from weight alone, so the blood-volume dependence of Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript and Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s10} is silent on sex.

A subsequent source-code audit identified other sex-dependent patient-setup pathways. In particular, \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp} sets a sex-dependent basal metabolic rate using the Roza--Shizgal revision of the Harris--Benedict equations~\cite{roza1984metabolic} (not the 1919 originals, whose constants differ). Under the resting, normothermic conditions of the atlas, the engine's energy model uses this value both to initialise total metabolic rate and as the set point toward which it is regulated; total metabolic rate in turn drives tissue oxygen consumption and carbon-dioxide production. Because the 1{,}280-run sweep varies sex jointly with body mass and height, it cannot by itself quantify the independent effect of the sex flag.

\subsection{Scope limits of the present atlas}\label{sec:s2-scope}

The limitations above are Pulse's, verified against its source and documentation. Those below belong to the present study: they bound what the atlas establishes and are summarised in the Discussion of the main manuscript.

\textbf{(i) Engine version.} The atlas pins to Pulse v4.3.1. Results apply identically to the build-only v4.3.2 release; v4.4.0, released after the sweep with revised physiology models (\S\ref{sec:s1}), and newer physiology branches will require re-mapping.

\textbf{(ii) Patient anatomy.} Only two young, lean adult reference bodies, male and female, were used (age 18, the youngest age Pulse accepts; body-mass index about 21); variation in age, and in body mass and height within each sex, is not characterised.

\textbf{(iii) Operational envelope.} Only adult critical-care ventilation in VC-AC mode is covered. Two areas are out of scope by design: pressure-targeted ventilator modes such as pressure-controlled and pressure-support; and transient within-run dynamics, particularly in runs that never reach steady state. The mode restriction also limits transfer to the ventilator-control application of Ward et al.~\cite{ward2024ventilator}, which drives the engine through a pressure-targeted action space: only three of its six ventilator settings (FiO$_2$, respiratory rate and PEEP) are inputs swept here.

\textbf{(iv) Discarded input pairs.} The Morris elementary-effect estimator discards any input pair in which either run is flagged (Section~\mainref{sec:methods-detector}{4.4} of the main manuscript). This removes all clean FiO$_2$ pairs in the two Healthy cells and the Severe-F cell, because every FiO$_2$ step has at least one flagged endpoint: in healthy lungs, high-FiO$_2$ runs flagged \texttt{not\_steady\_state} or \texttt{implausible} by SpO$_2$-saturation pinning and, at FiO$_2$ = 1.0, transient hyperoxia; in Severe-F, low-FiO$_2$ runs flagged \texttt{implausible} or \texttt{not\_steady\_state} by severe hypoxemia or a still-drifting SpO$_2$. The FiO$_2$ rows of Figure~3 of the main manuscript are therefore blank in these cells, and the cross-sex Spearman comparison is limited to Mild and Moderate (\S\ref{sec:s6-rho}). These are deterministic consequences of the preregistered implausibility bound and steady-state window at the edges of the envelope, not a Pulse defect. More trajectories cannot recover the discarded pairs, and re-running the grid with the 300-second window that resolves the hyperoxia plateau would break preregistration integrity. An independent same-size Morris resample of one cell (Mild-M, seed 44) preserved the highest-$\mu^{*}$ input for all six outputs but showed sample dependence in the finer ranking, and changed that cell's divergence count from 4 / 160 to 7 / 160, so the number of divergent configurations a sweep encounters is itself sample-dependent (\S\ref{sec:s3-resample}). No trajectory-count convergence analysis was performed, so the stability of $\mu^{*}$, $\sigma$ and the full top-three ordering at larger trajectory counts is not established.

\textbf{(v) What the hard bounds judge.} The hard physiological bounds behind the \texttt{implausible} flag (\S\ref{sec:s3-hard-bounds}) judge whether a \emph{sustained steady-state mean} is biologically plausible over the evaluation window, not whether a value is instantaneously attainable; rare clinical extremes, often transient or peri-mortem, can legitimately exceed them. The classifier keeps such runs and records the flag they fired rather than discarding them. The bounds are also adjustable choices rather than fixed biological constants: the deliberately conservative PaO$_2$ ceiling was re-examined under a wider, physiologically anchored limit (\S\ref{sec:s3-pao2-sensitivity}). They provide a run-level screening standard for synthetic training cohorts, not a claim that exceptional clinical trajectories are impossible.

\textbf{(vi) A non-rectangular input domain.} Pulse enforces DBP/SBP $\leq$ 0.75, which makes the sampled SBP--DBP domain non-rectangular. Configuration-time clipping shortened 9 of 20 DBP steps and made applied DBP co-vary in 8 of 20 SBP steps, so these effects are not strict fixed-step, one-at-a-time Morris estimates. SBP nevertheless remained rank 1 for MAP in all eight cells under six sensitivity estimators (\S\ref{sec:s3-clip-cost}), which establishes ranking robustness but not causal isolation of the clipping contribution. Shunt fraction was not recorded in the atlas, so shunt-mediated interpretations throughout this work remain physiologically motivated rather than directly tested.

\subsection{Hard physiological bounds: per-bound provenance}\label{sec:s3-hard-bounds}

The six \texttt{(lower, upper)} pairs that gate the \texttt{implausible} flag are stated in Section~\mainref{sec:methods-detector}{4.4} of the main manuscript and held in the \texttt{HARD\_BOUNDS} dictionary of \texttt{src/detector.py}. All were locked in the preregistration before any data were collected, and none has been revised post hoc. Each is regex-extracted from the source file at audit time by its \texttt{T1.06p.*} ledger entry, so any drift between code and manuscript is caught immediately. Pulse reports SpO$_2$ as a fraction in [0, 1], so the bound written as [50, 100]~\% in the main text is held in the code as 0.50 / 1.00.

Each bound belongs to one of two categories: (a)~a physical or definitional ceiling, whose only instance is the SpO$_2$ upper bound at hemoglobin saturation; or (b)~a clinical-plausibility threshold for a 60-second steady-state mean, i.e.\ a screening standard, not a claim that values beyond it are impossible in a patient (\S\ref{sec:s2-scope}). No bound rests on an equipment specification. Table~\ref{tab:hard-bounds-rationale} gives the rationale for each bound, with its primary-literature anchor where one exists; the citation set mirrors the per-bound rationale audited in the companion technical monograph (§2.6).

\subsection{Drift thresholds for the not\_steady\_state flag}\label{sec:s3-drift}

The \texttt{DRIFT\_MAX\_DELTA\_60S} dictionary (Table~\ref{tab:drift}) sets, for each channel, the maximum tolerated $|\Delta| = |\text{slope} \times 60~\text{s}|$ over the final 60 seconds of the steady-state window before a run is flagged \texttt{not\_steady\_state} for drift. Drift is estimated by ordinary least-squares regression on the 13 samples of the [60, 120]~s window (5-second interval); the $R^2$ of the fit is logged as diagnostic metadata but does not gate the flag. Beyond these thresholds, a bedside clinician would read the trajectory as actively changing rather than at homeostasis. Unlike the hard bounds, they are atlas-internal calibration choices anchored in standard critical-care monitoring practice, not biological survival limits; the citations in Table~\ref{tab:drift-rationale} support this monitoring framing, not the specific drift magnitudes. Like the hard bounds, each value is regex-extracted from \texttt{src/detector.py} at audit time.

Two further \texttt{not\_steady\_state} triggers complement the drift check. The coefficient-of-variation threshold \texttt{CV\_THRESHOLD = 0.05} fires when the standard deviation of any output across the steady-state window exceeds 5~\% of its mean. The flatline-at-bound criterion fires when \texttt{std / |mean| < 0.01} AND the mean is within 0.1~\% of a hard bound; it is intended to flag numerical pinning at a bound, although physiological saturation can satisfy it too. Both constants are locked under the same preregistration discipline as the bounds and drift thresholds, but as detector conventions: unlike the 0.1~\% bound tolerance, which was calibrated against existing Pulse runs, neither has a recorded derivation, source or empirical calibration. The coefficient-of-variation threshold applies identically to all six outputs. For delivered tidal volume, where it fired most often, the median coefficient of variation across the 64 paired reruns of \S\ref{sec:s8} fell from 0.351 before trigger locking to 0.016 afterwards, so the 0.05 convention lies between the two medians; prevalence estimates conditioned on that threshold, including the 20.6~\% primary-flag rate, nevertheless remain threshold-dependent.

\subsection{Clamp patterns: provenance and the post-sweep patch}\label{sec:s3-clamp}

The \texttt{clamped} flag fires when Pulse's setup-rejection log matches any pattern in a locked set of regular expressions, each written to match a Pulse rejection message; one of them, \texttt{is outside the valid range}, matches no message in the Pulse 4.3.1 source and fired on no log on disk. The set printed below is the one currently in \texttt{src/detector.py}. The 1{,}280-run sweep was classified with the earlier \emph{four}-pattern version, calibrated against deliberate-clamp probes in the pilot before the full sweep; it replaced an overly broad regex set that produced 100~\% false-positive clamping by matching Pulse's normal solver-convergence messages. The note after the listing records what changed afterwards and why the published classification is unaffected:

\noindent On the calibration runs, the four-pattern set fired on both deliberate-clamp probes of the pilot (SBP = 140 and DBP = 85~mmHg, each rejected at setup with exit code 1) and on none of the eight smoke-test runs; its behaviour on every Pulse log on disk is replayed in the note below.

\noindent \textbf{Patch of 2026-07-26, applied after the sweep.} Two changes produce the five-pattern set above: the unit token between the value and ``is'' became optional, and a \texttt{minimum \ldots\ baseline allowed is} pattern was added to mirror the existing \texttt{maximum} one. Of the ten baseline-rejection strings built by Pulse's \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp}, nine include the unit; the exception, the heart-rate lower bound, meant that a heart rate below 50~bpm was rejected at setup without raising \texttt{clamped}. Replaying both pattern sets over all 3{,}349 Pulse logs on disk changes exactly one run (the probe written to expose the gap) and no run of the published sweep, whose requested heart rates never leave [50, 110]~bpm. The patch therefore has no effect on the reported classification; it is printed so that users of this detector do not inherit the gap. No run was lost to the gap in any case: a setup rejection exits non-zero with no CSV, so \texttt{diverged} fires instead.

\noindent \textbf{One explicit downstream adjustment, outside this flag by construction.} Pulse makes a single deterministic change to a submitted ventilator setting in this sweep, and it is neither silent nor a setup-time event. When the requested inspired-oxygen fraction plus the non-nitrogen ambient fractions exceeds one, the ventilator model writes \texttt{[ERROR] FiO2 setting + ambient fractions other than N2 is greater than 1.0. Setting FiO2 to max value of 0.9996.} to the log and sets the ventilator compartment's oxygen volume fraction to 0.9996. The cause is compositional rather than a bound on the input: Pulse's ambient gas mixture carries carbon dioxide at $4 \times 10^{-4}$, so an oxygen fraction of exactly 1.0 leaves no room for it. Of the 208 runs requesting FiO$_2$ = 1.0, 195 reach the ventilator action and \emph{all 195} are adjusted once, when the action is applied; the other 13 diverge during patient stabilisation, before the action is submitted (Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript). No requested FiO$_2$ below 1.0 is affected, and the substituted value is identical in every affected run. The adjustment (0.04~\% in relative terms) moves the sea-level alveolar-gas ceiling from 663.0 to 662.7~mmHg, so the $\approx$663~mmHg used elsewhere remains correct. It reclassifies no run as \texttt{clamped}, a category preregistered on setup-time rejections matched by the locked patterns, and the serialised ventilator action still carries FiO$_2$ = 1.0 (ingestion is exact for all 10{,}160 contracts that reached it). Nor does it bear on hypothesis H2, which concerns \emph{silent} clamping: this adjustment is announced in the log.

\subsection{Body-dimension provenance for the two adult reference patient bodies}\label{sec:s3-bodies}

The two adult reference bodies are male (age 18, 67.3~kg, 180~cm) and female (age 18, 56.2~kg, 163~cm). Both weights are U.S.\ Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) growth-chart 50th-percentile weight-for-age values for each sex~\cite{kuczmarski2002cdc}, taken from the row \texttt{Agemos}~=~216.5 of the primary CDC data file, whose 50th percentiles (67.28992603~kg for males, 56.22969564~kg for females) round to the preregistered 67.3 and 56.2~kg. CDC lists age at the half-month point standing for the whole month, so \texttt{Agemos}~=~216.5 denotes 216.0--216.99 months, the interval beginning at exactly 18.0 years. The preregistration's provenance erratum, which describes these weights as the CDC values \emph{at 18 years (216.5 months)}, is therefore correct.

The female height matches both the CDC 50th-percentile value~\cite{kuczmarski2002cdc} and Pulse's \texttt{heightStandardFemale\_cm = 163.0} reference constant in \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp}. The male height is a clinically standard adult value chosen to fix the male body mass index at 20.8; it is not Pulse's \texttt{heightStandardMale\_cm = 177.0} constant, which would have yielded a BMI close to the CDC 50th percentile. Pulse computes the blood-volume baseline via the allometric relation $\text{BV} = 65.6 \cdot \text{weight}_{\text{kg}}^{1.02}$, giving 4{,}802.63~mL for the male body and 3{,}996.09~mL for the female body, the 17~\% gap whose effect on the input-extreme divergence cluster is characterised in Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript.

\subsection{Four-flag classifier: priority order and rationale}\label{sec:s3-detector-order}

The detector's priority order \texttt{clamped > diverged > implausible > not\_steady\_state > clean} follows the chronology of failure modes in a Pulse simulation, from input handling through solver execution to output evaluation. \texttt{clamped} is an input-contract breach: Pulse did not accept at least one setup input as submitted, so the run no longer corresponds to the requested Morris grid point and no downstream flag would refer to the input being studied. \texttt{diverged} is a mathematical failure (non-zero exit code, NaN or infinite values in the CSV, missing CSV or truncated simulation) that precludes any biological interpretation. \texttt{implausible} is a plausibility failure: the computation ran cleanly, but a steady-state mean falls outside the hard bounds of Section~\mainref{sec:methods-detector}{4.4} of the main manuscript (a screening outcome; \S\ref{sec:s3-hard-bounds}). \texttt{not\_steady\_state} is a dynamic failure: the run completed with finite output but did not satisfy the steady-state criterion in the 60-second window (flatline at a bound, coefficient of variation above 0.05, or drift beyond the thresholds of Table~\ref{tab:drift}), so its 60-second mean is not treated as a steady-state summary.

\subsection{Cost of the pulse-pressure clip on diastolic elementary effects}\label{sec:s3-clip-cost}

Clipping DBP when each configuration is written, rather than when the sample is drawn, keeps the Morris sample geometrically intact but introduces a quantifiable distortion, reported here: for the affected steps, the engine receives a smaller diastolic change than the sample records, whereas the elementary-effect estimator divides by the full nominal step.

Of the 160 sampled points, 35 request a DBP above the 0.75 ratio and are clipped. This has two consequences, both fixed by the sample and therefore identical in all eight cells. First, 9 of the 20 diastolic steps are shortened: the nominal DBP step is two-thirds of the 20~mmHg range (13.33~mmHg); five of the nine fall to 6.25~\% of it (0.83~mmHg), one to 56.25~\% and three to 62.5~\%, and none to zero. Second, in 8 of the 140 steps the changed input is SBP, which moves the $0.75 \times \mathrm{SBP}$ threshold and hence the applied DBP, so two inputs change at once, violating the one-at-a-time assumption. The remaining 123 of 140 steps (87.9~\%) are unaffected. After flag filtering, 41 of 78 clean diastolic pairs are shortened and 19 of 68 clean systolic pairs are affected, leaving 49 systolic pairs free of concomitant diastolic movement (per cell: 4, 4, 7, 6, 10, 9, 4 and 5 for Healthy-M, Healthy-F, Mild-M, Mild-F, Moderate-M, Moderate-F, Severe-M and Severe-F). Because each trajectory carries exactly one systolic step, dropping the contaminated pairs or the trajectories that carry them removes the same systolic pairs, so these counts apply to both the uncontaminated-step and trajectory-matched estimators: the systolic robustness checks below rest on as few as four elementary-effect pairs in Healthy-M, Healthy-F and Severe-M.

The distortion does not overturn the reported findings. H1 holds in 48 of 48 cell--output combinations under either estimator (median top-3 fraction 0.928 nominal versus 0.910 applied-step; minimum 0.658 versus 0.696), and output MAP remains SBP-dominated in all eight cells. What changes is the standing of DBP itself: under the applied-step estimator, it enters the top three in 9 of the 48 combinations from which the reported ranking excludes it, and its $\mu^{*}$ rises by a median factor of 2.4 (maximum 15.4). The reported DBP $\mu^{*}$ should therefore be read as a lower bound relative to the applied-step estimator on the same pairs (dividing by a shorter step can only raise $|\mathrm{EE}|$, so this direction is arithmetic rather than empirical), not as a lower bound on DBP's influence in general, which the flag-filtered pair set does not establish.

We do not substitute the applied-step estimator for the preregistered one. Dividing by the applied step corrects the denominator on the shortened diastolic transitions, but it does not restore the one-at-a-time property on the eight systolic transitions where the ratio constraint also moves DBP, and the effective diastolic step then ranges from 0.83 to 13.33~mmHg, which is no longer the fixed-step design on which the Morris index is defined. Step length is also structurally confounded with position in the sampled space: the shortest steps occur only at SBP = 90~mmHg, whereas all 11 full-length diastolic steps occur at SBP $\geq$ 100~mmHg, so no difference between the two estimators can be attributed to step length alone. The applied-step $\mu^{*}$ is therefore reported as evidence on the direction of the clip's effect, not as a term-by-term corrected index. Imposing the ratio at sampling time instead would forfeit the sample geometry the design was built to protect.

Two further estimators test whether the reported systolic dominance depends on the affected steps. The \emph{uncontaminated-step} estimator drops every clean pair in which the applied DBP co-varies with SBP; the \emph{trajectory-matched} variant also restricts every input to the 12 of 20 trajectories whose systolic step is uncontaminated, so that all inputs are estimated on the same amount of data. Two \emph{combined} estimators apply the applied-step denominator and the contaminated-pair exclusion together. SBP remained rank 1 for MAP in all eight cells under all six estimators (nominal-step, applied-step, uncontaminated-step, trajectory-matched, hybrid-combined and matched-combined). Under the combined estimators, DBP is the runner-up in all eight cells, with a smallest margin of 1.22-fold ($\mu^{*}$ ratio) in Mild-M. Under the hybrid-combined estimator, DBP would overtake SBP in a cell only if its applied-to-nominal $\mu^{*}$ ratio (range 1.131--3.289) exceeded that cell's uncontaminated-SBP-to-nominal-DBP ratio (range 2.266--4.669), which occurred in no cell. The matched-combined analysis independently kept SBP at rank 1 in all eight cells.

A descriptive transition-stratified comparison narrows, without removing, the confounding between exclusion and position. Three systolic transitions carry both contaminated and uncontaminated steps, so the two groups can be compared at identical systolic start and end values (Table~\ref{tab:transition-stratified}). In each of the three, contaminated steps had the higher median absolute MAP elementary effect, and the stratified contrast remained positive in every leave-one-transition-out analysis. Requested DBP is perfectly collinear with contamination: structurally for the 100-to-120 and 120-to-100 transitions, whose 75~mmHg ceiling no lower grid level can exceed, and by chance in the realised Morris draw for 110-to-90. The ordering does not support a simple monotonic explanation by mean applied-DBP level alone: the largest contrast occurred for 110-to-90 despite its lower mean endpoint DBP, which is in any case only a coarse proxy for the level at which the elementary effect is evaluated. These results are consistent with an influence of applied-DBP co-variation but do not establish a causal contribution.

\subsection{Sobol bolt-on: preregistered decision}\label{sec:s3-sobol}

A second-tier variance-based (Sobol) analysis was preregistered as a conditional bolt-on to the Morris screen. The rule locked in \texttt{preregistration.md}~\S~Sampling method evaluates the top-three $\mu^{*}$-ranked inputs of each cell $\times$ output combination by their $\sigma / \mu^{*}$ ratio, the standard Morris proxy for non-linearity and input--input interaction: at or below 0.4 the response is treated as linear and no Sobol analysis is run; at or above 0.6 the input is promoted; values in between form a declared gray zone.

Applied as written, the trigger fires on 72 of the 144 top-three inputs, spanning 43 of the 48 cell $\times$ output combinations. The preregistration capped the bolt-on at 1{,}500 additional runs, with a cost model of $64 \times (2k + 2)$, where $k$ is the number of promoted inputs. A Sobol sample cannot be shared across cells, because each scenario--sex cell is a different model, but it is shared across the six outputs of a cell, which come from the same Pulse runs. The 43 triggered combinations fall in eight cells, each with three to six distinct promoted inputs, for 6{,}144 runs under the formula as written. Computing only the first-order and total indices required by the analysis plan, at $64 \times (k + 2)$, lowers this to 3{,}584; a full seven-input design in every firing cell would cost 8{,}192 and 4{,}608, respectively. All these figures exceed the preregistered envelope, by a factor of 2.4 to 5.5, so the bolt-on is reported rather than executed.

The released code base keeps a restricted exploratory Sobol demonstrator (\texttt{scripts/s019\_sobol\_bolton.py}), written during an earlier, dominant-input reading of the trigger (six cells selected on the dominant input, all seven swept inputs, base sample 256, first-order indices only; 13{,}824 runs). It shares the locked input space of the Morris sweep but does not implement the preregistered design: the 6{,}144-run figure above is the cost of that design, not of the demonstrator.

The preregistered rule conditions on the point ratio without any uncertainty qualifier, so we add a post hoc sensitivity analysis, labelled as such: for each top-three input, trajectories are resampled 1{,}000 times and $\sigma / \mu^{*}$ is recomputed jointly on each draw, so that the interval covers the ratio itself. Bootstrapping $\sigma$ alone and dividing by a fixed $\mu^{*}$ would ignore their covariance and distort the interval in an uncontrolled direction. Under each of five independent bootstrap seeds, 23 of the 144 top-three inputs keep the lower bound of this joint interval at or above 0.6, and repeating the procedure with 500, 2{,}000 and 4{,}000 resamples returns the same 23 inputs. This stable set spans 17 of the 48 cell $\times$ output combinations, all seven swept inputs, all four scenarios and both reference bodies (12 female, 11 male); PEEP is the most frequent input (8 of 23), followed by respiratory rate (5).

One structural limit governs how this map should be read. Because the elementary-effect estimator uses only unflagged runs, a regime unstable enough to flag its own runs cannot supply the pairs needed to characterise it. Delivered tidal volume is the clearest case: it is the most affected output in the stable set (8 of 23), yet none of the 8 falls in Healthy, the scenario where its oscillation is most prevalent (delivered-Vt coefficient of variation above 0.05 on 139 of 320 runs, versus 36 of 320 in Moderate). The median top-three input retains 3 clean trajectories in Healthy and 16 in Moderate. Departure from linearity (curvature or input--input interaction, which this ratio does not separate) is therefore easiest to establish where the engine is most stable, the opposite of where it matters most.

\subsection{Traffic-light baseline: robustness of the male-referenced scoring}\label{sec:s3-traffic}

The rule that colours each cell green, yellow or red is given in Section~\mainref{sec:methods-traffic}{4.5} of the main manuscript and its three criteria in the legend to Figure~2; the $10~\%$ and $30~\%$ deviation bands were locked as item~7 of \texttt{preregistration.md}. Here we check one discretionary choice of that rule: scoring every cell, female cells included, against the Healthy-M baseline. A single baseline keeps male and female cells on one scale, but a female cell's deviation then also contains the expected difference between the two bodies.

This choice does not drive the classification. Re-scoring the 24 female combinations against a sex-specific Healthy-F baseline, with the flag-rate logic unchanged, changes the colour of \emph{0 of 24} (reproducible via \texttt{scripts/baseline\_robustness\_check.py}). Female yellow and red colours are set either by the diverged/implausible flag rate or by ARDS-scale deviations (e.g.\ output heart rate roughly doubling in severe ARDS), which exceed the male--female differences of the two Healthy cells (0.3 to 11.3~\%, largest for PaCO$_2$). Only one female combination, PaCO$_2$ in Healthy-F, crosses a band on the sex difference alone (11.3~\% from Healthy-M, above $10~\%$), and its cell's hard flags already make it yellow.

\subsection{PaO$_2$ ceiling: reclassification under a wider bound}\label{sec:s3-pao2-sensitivity}

Within category (b), the exact numerical choice (e.g.\ a PaO$_2$ lower bound of 20 vs 25~mmHg) is a defensible judgement call within the cited literature rather than a unique biological constant. Section~3.8 of the companion technical monograph applies a sensitivity analysis to the most contestable bound, raising the PaO$_2$ upper bound from 600 to 700~mmHg according to the alveolar-gas-equation ceiling; the 34 exceedances it starts from are reported in Section~\mainref{sec:results-hyperoxia}{2.4} of the main manuscript. Under this wider bound, 32 of the 34 runs flagged \texttt{implausible} by the locked 600~mmHg ceiling no longer exceed the widened PaO$_2$ plausibility bound (31 at FiO$_2 \geq 0.95$; 21 on the female and 11 on the male reference body). The remaining two are Healthy-F runs at maximal minute ventilation (Vt 800~mL $\times$ RR 20 = 16~L/min) whose PaO$_2$ undergoes a genuine numerical runaway within the window, stable at $\approx$480~mmHg for the first 60~s and then climbing to a physically impossible plateau above the 760~mmHg sea-level barometric pressure; they correctly remain implausible under either bound (the same solver-instability mode seen at extended windows, Section~\ref{sec:s9-guidance}).

The wider bound has one limit that bears on how this side-analysis should be read. 700~mmHg is a \emph{flat} ceiling derived at FiO$_2$ = 1.0, whereas the alveolar-gas-equation value scales with each run's own inspired fraction and PaCO$_2$. Of the 32 reclassified runs, 31 are at FiO$_2 \geq 0.95$, where the flat and run-specific ceilings are close; the exception is a Healthy-F run at FiO$_2$ = 0.737, whose final-60-s mean of 679~mmHg far exceeds the $0.737 \times (760 - 47) - 16.2 / 0.8 \approx 505$~mmHg permitted by its own inspired fraction and PaCO$_2$, and which an FiO$_2$-aware ceiling would keep flagged. The flat 700~mmHg bound is therefore a deliberately permissive stress test of the locked classifier, not a better classifier. No locked threshold has been adjusted post hoc; the only amendment to a locked threshold (2026-04-22-b, flatline tolerance 1~\% $\rightarrow$ 0.1~\%) was made before the sweep launched.

\subsection{Morris terminology: trajectory, elementary effect, $\mu^{*}$ and clean pair}\label{sec:s3-terms}

These four terms are used throughout the main manuscript and are defined here in full; Table~\mainref{tab:terms}{1} of the main manuscript defines the three terms needed to read its figures.

\textbf{Trajectory.} One Morris path through the seven-dimensional input space. From a random starting point, each step changes exactly one input by a fixed Morris step (two-thirds of that input's swept range) until all seven inputs have each been changed once. A trajectory therefore visits $7+1 = 8$ points, and \emph{consecutive} points differ in a single input, apart from the pulse-pressure clip quantified in \S\,\ref{sec:s3-clip-cost}. With 20 trajectories, each cell contains $20 \times 8 = 160$ simulations.

\textbf{Elementary effect (EE).} A finite-difference estimate of one input's influence on one output: the change in steady-state output divided by the size of the step that produced it, expressed as a fraction of the input's swept range. Every step here is the same fixed jump of two-thirds of that range (Morris grid, 4 levels). Each elementary effect comes from one consecutive pair of runs in a trajectory (the run just before and the run just after the step); for each (cell, input, output) combination, elementary effects are then reduced across trajectories into $\mu^{*}$.

\textbf{$\mu^{*}$.} Mean of the absolute elementary effects of one input on one output across all trajectories in a cell. The Morris input-importance metric reported in Figure~\mainref{fig:morris}{3} of the main manuscript; large $\mu^{*}$ indicates strong influence of the input on the output in that cell.

\textbf{Clean pair.} The two runs on either side of one Morris step when \emph{both} are flag-clean, i.e.\ neither fired any of the four flags (Section~\mainref{sec:methods-detector}{4.4} of the main manuscript). Elementary effects are computed only from clean pairs: if either run is flagged, the pair contributes nothing.

\textbf{Two statistical terms of Section~\mainref{sec:methods-stats}{4.6} of the main manuscript.} The \emph{false-discovery rate}, controlled there by the Benjamini--Hochberg procedure, is the expected fraction of false positives among the rejected tests. The \emph{Spearman rank correlation} $\rho$ between the male and female $\mu^{*}$ vectors is $+1$ when the two orderings agree and $-1$ when they are reversed.

\section{ARDS impairment-fraction instability zone}\label{sec:s5}

Section~\mainref{sec:results-secondary}{2.6} of the main manuscript refers to a documented ARDS impairment-fraction band (0.91--0.945) in which Pulse's respiratory solver becomes unstable. This section first reports the Berlin-band calibration of the scenario fractions, then describes how Pulse translates the impairment-fraction scalar into respiratory-mechanics and gas-exchange changes, how the band was identified, how the atlas's continuous-severity micro-sweep refines its edges, and how divergence within the band stratifies by sex at midrange Morris inputs.

\subsection{Berlin-band calibration of the scenario impairment fractions}\label{sec:s5-berlin}

Pulse encodes ARDS as an abstract per-lung \texttt{Severity} scalar rather than by the clinical PaO$_2$/FiO$_2$ (P/F) definition, so the four scenario impairment fractions were assigned by \emph{forward calibration}: Pulse was run across severities at a fixed reference ventilation, and the fractions were chosen so that arterial P/F falls near the centres of the Berlin Definition~\cite{ards2012} mild and moderate bands and just inside the upper boundary of the severe band, which is open-ended below and therefore has no centre (mild 200--300, moderate 100--200, severe $\leq 100$~mmHg, at PEEP $\geq 5$~cmH$_2$O). This was the original ARDS severity calibration of the companion translation project, a 28-run impairment sweep of 2026-03 (full log in the companion technical monograph, Appendix~B.3), on the adult male reference body under VC-AC with tidal volume 6~mL/kg actual body weight (403.8~mL), PEEP 10~cmH$_2$O and FiO$_2$ 0.4. Impairment fractions 0.26, 0.60 and 0.95 gave 256, 150 and 96~mmHg (Table~\ref{tab:s5-berlin-calib}). The companion's later E6 experiment (2026-04), with the same ventilator mode, tidal volume, PEEP and FiO$_2$, obtained lower values (252, 148 and 75~mmHg) in the same three Berlin bands.

This calibration has since been reproduced within the present repository (2026-07-26; 40 runs on the atlas's own engine image), so a headline result no longer depends on an external project. Evaluated over a [108, 168]~s window derived from the timing recorded with E6 (a 60-s settle, then a 108-s recording; E6 itself averaged its last five 10-s samples), the replication reproduces the March anchors within 0.6~\% at the three scenario fractions (0.26 $\to$ 256.5, 0.60 $\to$ 150.5, 0.95 $\to$ 96.6~mmHg) and within 2~\% across all five fractions compared. This agreement is empirical rather than a protocol match: the March log records 5-minute runs after a 120-s settle without stating its averaging window, and E6, under its recorded protocol, reported the lower values above. The same runs independently re-derive the instability band: fractions 0.910, 0.920, 0.930, 0.940 and 0.945 all failed to complete, whereas 0.900 and 0.950 completed.

\subsection{Severity-to-physiology mapping in the Pulse respiratory model}\label{sec:s5-mapping}

Pulse represents ARDS as a single \texttt{Severity} scalar in $[0, 1]$ per lung compartment (the \texttt{AcuteRespiratoryDistressSyndrome} condition), which the atlas sets bilaterally and symmetrically to the scenario impairment fraction. This scalar feeds four hard-coded piecewise-linear interpolation curves in the respiratory-model source (\texttt{RespiratoryModel.cpp}: \texttt{UpdateResistances}, \texttt{UpdateAlveolarCompliances}, \texttt{UpdateDiffusion} and \texttt{UpdatePulmonaryShunt}), each calibrated on the adult standard patient; resistance, compliance and diffusion cannot be set independently. Table~\ref{tab:s5-mapping} gives the resulting multipliers at the three ARDS scenario severities, linearly interpolated from the source calibration nodes.

The four curves act together: in the Severe scenario (impairment 0.95), Pulse multiplies distal alveolar resistance by $\approx 22\times$, reduces alveolar compliance to $\approx 45\,\%$ and the alveolar diffusion surface area to $\approx 7.5\,\%$ of baseline, and opens the intrapulmonary shunt. The collapse of the diffusion surface and the wider shunt provide a plausible model-internal explanation for the severe arterial hypoxemia that accompanies the compensatory tachycardia of Section~\mainref{sec:results-secondary}{2.6} of the main manuscript; shunt fraction was not recorded, so this reading is not directly tested. The shunt row is the least direct: its damage term is non-monotonic in severity and is combined, through a \texttt{MIN} operation, with an alveolar-recruitment term (a function of actual alveolar volume, which PEEP raises) and a left/right calibration factor, so the effective shunt resistance is not simply the tabulated multiplier. Severity also feeds the respiratory driver pressure, lung volumes and the inspiratory:expiratory ratio through smaller restrictive terms. The four curves are smooth; the 0.91--0.945 solver-instability band characterised below is an emergent numerical property of the combined stiff respiratory state, not a discontinuity in any single curve.

\textbf{Adult-only tuning.} Both the baseline resistance, compliance and diffusion-surface values \emph{and} the severity-to-effect curves are tuned to the adult standard patient, and the single-scalar design prevents retuning the three properties independently. Applying them to pediatric mechanics would mis-scale the absolute consequences, which is one reason why Pulse hard-gates age $\geq 18$ at patient setup.

The absence of a surfactant model was also verified directly. A case-insensitive search of the complete Pulse engine tree at \texttt{REL\_4\_3\_2} (commit \texttt{e8a3649}; physiologically identical to the 4.3.1 pin, differing only in seven build-system files) returns no occurrence of \texttt{surfactant} or of the related terms under which such a model would surface (surface tension, Laplace, derecruitment, alveolar stability, opening/closing pressure), and neither the Pulse engineering forum nor the public respiratory methodology mentions it. Pulse reproduces the macroscopic behaviours that surfactant governs without the underlying mechanism: the expected pressure--volume hysteresis of the healthy lung (per Pulse's respiratory-methodology validation) comes from a phenomenological compliance curve, and alveolar recruitment is an empirical acinar-volume-to-shunt-fraction mapping calibrated to PEEP data. Lung compliance is thus a lumped circuit element scaled by disease multipliers, with no surface-tension term, so neonatal respiratory distress syndrome, fundamentally a surfactant-deficiency disease (including its response to surfactant instillation), cannot be represented in the current model.

\subsection{Provenance of the documented band}\label{sec:s5-prov}

Three convergent boundary probes within the companion technical work (same Pulse v4.3.1 engine and male reference body, different impairment-fraction grids, independent runs) characterise the same numerical-bifurcation range in the respiratory solver. A 28-run impairment sweep at the reference ventilation of \S\ref{sec:s5-berlin} found that fractions 0.92 and 0.94 failed to stabilise. A 10-run high-resolution probe (0.90, then 0.91 to 0.95 in 0.005 steps) narrowed the failure range to 0.91--0.945 inclusive (eight consecutive failures, with 0.90 and 0.95 passing on either side). A complementary 9-run refinement at 0.93--0.97 in 0.005 steps (four failures at 0.93--0.945, five passes at 0.95--0.97) confirmed the upper edge. Because all three probes belong to the same project, their convergence shows reproducibility under one engine version and reference body, not generalisation across laboratories. Pulse's documentation does not flag this zone: published Pulse validation work and HowTo examples do not examine impairment fractions within 0.91--0.945.

\subsection{Two divergence regimes}\label{sec:s5-regimes}

Together with Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript, these data distinguish two operationally separate divergence regimes. The input-driven regime (the main-manuscript finding) concentrates at low HR, low SBP, high RR and a requested FiO$_2$ of 1.0, which is never applied because all 20 divergences occur during patient stabilisation, before the ventilator action is submitted (Section~\mainref{sec:results-overview}{2.1} of the main manuscript); it affects only the male body (20 events in the four male cells, none in the 640 female runs). The severity-driven regime (this sweep) affects both sexes at midrange inputs once the impairment fraction enters [0.879, 0.946], with female divergence concentrated in the lower part of the band and male divergence in the upper part. Both are genuine solver-stability findings. The sex effect of H4 (Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript) is therefore best described as regime-dependent rather than all-male, and users requiring stable steady-state outputs should avoid impairment fractions of 0.879--0.946.

\subsection{Relative ventilation-dose mismatch between the two reference bodies}\label{sec:s6-dose}

The sweep varies tidal volume in absolute millilitres over the same 300--800~mL range on both reference bodies. A matched input vector therefore delivers a matched \emph{absolute} tidal volume, but not a matched dose relative to body size, as two normalisations show.

Per kilogram of \emph{actual} body weight (67.3 and 56.2~kg), the swept range corresponds to 4.5--11.9~mL\,kg$^{-1}$ on the male body and 5.3--14.2~mL\,kg$^{-1}$ on the female body, so at every matched setting the female body receives 19.8~\% more tidal volume per kilogram.

Per kilogram of \emph{ideal} body weight, the gap is larger. Pulse computes ideal body weight at patient setup with the sex-specific Devine relation ($50.0 + 2.3\,(\text{height in inches} - 60)$~kg for a male body and $45.5 + 2.3\,(\text{height in inches} - 60)$~kg for a female body), giving 74.99~kg at 180~cm and 55.10~kg at 163~cm. The swept range then corresponds to 4.0--10.7~mL\,kg$^{-1}$ of ideal body weight on the male body and 5.4--14.5~mL\,kg$^{-1}$ on the female body, a 36.1~\% relative excess for the female body.

This is an interpretive constraint, not a defect in the sweep: sweeping tidal volume in absolute units keeps the Morris design identical across all eight cells, the property on which the balanced factorial rests. However, any male--female contrast on a gas-exchange output then combines three contributions that the design does not separate: the sex flag, body morphology and ventilatory dose. This matters most for PaCO$_2$, whose dominant determinant is alveolar ventilation and which accounts for three of the five surviving Wasserstein distances reported in Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript. Separating the three would require both bodies to retain the same grid points \emph{and} receive matched relative ventilation doses; matching grid points alone does not suffice.

\section{VC-AC trigger lockout}\label{sec:s8}

Section~\mainref{sec:results-vcac}{2.3} of the main manuscript reports that setting Pulse's \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerPressure} to $-2$~cmH$_2$O on the \texttt{SEMechanicalVentilatorVolumeControl} action reduces the median delivered-tidal-volume coefficient of variation from 0.351 to 0.016 across 64 paired reruns, with 78.1~\% of locked-trigger reruns falling below the 0.05 steady-state threshold. This section describes the protocol and shows per-breath waveform evidence that the tidal-volume oscillation is real (not a sampling artefact) and that the intervention strongly reduces it.

\subsection{Protocol design}\label{sec:s8-protocol}

The 64 paired reruns sampled seven of the eight (scenario, sex) cells (none from Moderate-M), mostly the Healthy (26 reruns) and Mild (19) cells. Each rerun reproduced the original configuration exactly, with a single modification: the \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerPressure} field of the VC-AC action was set to $-2$~cmH$_2$O (the constant declared in \texttt{scripts/run\_s014\_trigger\_lockout.py} and regex-extracted into ledger anchor \texttt{T1.06d.s14\_trigger\_pressure}). All other settings (Morris input vector, simulation duration, scenario, reference body) were unchanged, so the intervention is a clean one-parameter manipulation and any change in the steady-state Vt distribution is attributable to the trigger pressure. Two configurations share the highest pre-lock delivered-Vt coefficient of variation, 1.826 (S1\_F\_0117 and S1\_F\_0118, run indices 117 and 118 in the Healthy-F cell); the lock reduces them to 0.018 and 0.024.

\subsection{Native 50 Hz waveform evidence (Figure~\ref{fig:s8-vt})}\label{sec:s8-waveform}

In the atlas-aggregated parquet, delivered tidal volume is a breath-integrated time series sampled every 5 seconds. For waveform-level inspection, the native Pulse output of each run (\texttt{output\_pulse\_native.csv}) provides the same signal every 0.02~s (50~Hz). At adult respiratory rates of about 0.25~Hz (one breath every four seconds), this is a 200-fold oversampling of the breath rate, well above the Nyquist criterion of about $2 \times 0.25 = 0.5$~Hz, so the per-breath delivered volume in Figure~\ref{fig:s8-vt} is the directly observable breath-by-breath dynamic of the ventilator coupling, not an aliasing artefact.

\subsection{Cycle-resolved check of the 5-second classifier statistic}\label{sec:s8-cycle}

The delivered-Vt coefficient of variation used throughout this work is the locked classifier statistic, computed from 5-second samples over the final 60-second window; it is not a direct measure of the physiological amplitude of patient--ventilator dyssynchrony. At a breath rate near 0.25~Hz, sampling at 0.2~Hz cannot reliably resolve breath-to-breath dynamics, so a post hoc cycle-resolved analysis was performed on the native 50~Hz traces already recorded for every run; no additional simulation was required.

Across the full sweep, the two resolutions reach the same steady-state verdict on 1{,}239 of 1{,}260 runs (98.3~\%): 297 runs are flagged by both, 942 by neither, 15 cross the 0.05 threshold only at cycle resolution, and 6 only under the 5-second statistic. Of the 15 cycle-only crossings, ten fall below a factor of 3; five are substantial (factors of 4.0 to 113.9), and all five fall on the female reference body. Nine runs deliver a numerically constant volume across the window and carry zero cycle-level variability at the resolution of the recorded trace.

The effect on the locked flag count is small. Six of the 15 cycle-only crossings are already flagged by another channel or criterion, and three of the six 5-second-only crossings would lose their flag entirely. A counterfactual reclassification using cycle-resolved Vt variability would therefore change the \texttt{not\_steady\_state} total from 530 to 536, an increase of 1.1~\%. The locked classification and every count reported elsewhere in this work are unchanged.

On the 64 paired trigger-lockout configurations of \S\,\ref{sec:s8-protocol}, delivered-Vt variability decreases after lockout in every pair, at both resolutions. Four descriptive summaries were computed on the 63 configurations with a non-zero post-lockout cycle-level CV (Table~\ref{tab:s8-estimands}). The qualitative effect is consistent, but quantitative agreement between resolutions depends on the estimand: the relative gap ranges from 0.6~\% to 8.6~\%, and its direction varies (two summaries make the 5-second statistic overstate the effect and two make it understate it). Ratios are particularly sensitive to the small post-lockout CV values. Configuration S1\_F\_0016 is excluded from all four summaries: after lockout its delivered volume is identical at every breath to the precision of the recorded trace (819.027985~mL), so its post-lockout CV is zero and no finite reduction ratio is defined, and the two difference summaries use the same 63 configurations so that the four rows are comparable. This zero is observed, not imputed, and the run remains in the 64-pair medians and counts given at the opening of this section.

One limitation of the cycle-resolved statistic was measured rather than assumed. Recovering cycles as change points of the recorded volume merges two consecutive breaths that deliver numerically identical volumes, so a repeated volume is counted once rather than twice. Across the 1{,}251 runs whose delivered volume is not numerically constant, the recovered cycle count and the engine's reported respiration rate agree to a median of 5.4~\%, but the 95th percentile of the disagreement is 38~\% and the maximum 95~\%. Of the 126 runs that disagree by more than 20~\%, four are cycle-only crossings and none is a 5-second-only crossing.

A sensitivity analysis bounds the consequence, and its direction was measured rather than reasoned about. Scoring the same window on every native 50~Hz sample weights each recorded volume by its duration, which at fixed breath length is per-breath weighting and is therefore unaffected by merging. The two estimators are nearly identical in magnitude, run for run: on the 1{,}250 runs whose two values differ by more than a relative $10^{-6}$ (the remaining ten, nine of them constant-volume runs, are identical to the precision of the trace), the change-point value is the larger on 640 and the smaller on 610, with a median ratio of 1.0005. Merging therefore does not systematically inflate or deflate the statistic; it only affects which runs fall on either side of the threshold.

Against the 5-second statistic, the all-sample estimator agrees on 295 flagged and 945 unflagged runs, crosses the threshold alone on 12 and is crossed alone by the 5-second statistic on 8 (98.4~\% agreement, versus 98.3~\% above), and it disagrees with the change-point estimator on 9 of the 1{,}260 runs. Its counterfactual reclassification would change the \texttt{not\_steady\_state} total from 530 to 533, versus 530 to 536 above. The 15 / 6 split is therefore not an artefact of the segmentation rule, and the reported counterfactual is the larger of the two, so it overstates rather than understates the cost of changing resolution.

These results support the intervention effect on simulated breath-to-breath volume variability, while showing that the classifier CV should not be read as a validated physiological measure of dyssynchrony, nor as a universally reliable measure of variability magnitude at the individual-run level.

\subsection{Mechanism and downstream consequence}\label{sec:s8-mech}

The default \texttt{SEMechanicalVentilatorVolumeControl} action in \texttt{AssistedControl} mode enables Pulse's respiratory-model-driven patient trigger when no explicit \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerPressure} or \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerFlow} is supplied (\texttt{SEMechanicalVentilatorVolumeControl.cpp}, lines 120--128 of the upstream Pulse source at \texttt{REL\_4\_3\_1}). This spontaneous-drive trigger couples breath delivery to the modelled respiratory drive and produces effort-dependent breath-to-breath tidal-volume variance consistent with patient--ventilator dyssynchrony~\cite{figueroacasas2016}, but it breaks the steady-state-mean assumption on which the locked classifier relies. Setting the trigger pressure explicitly to $-2$~cmH$_2$O replaces it with a fixed pressure threshold, which sufficiently strong efforts can still cross, and reduces the Vt CV more than twentyfold in the median paired comparison.

This setting is a data-generation and exploration lever, not a clinical recommendation. In a real patient, the threshold is a clinical choice: with any fixed threshold, inspiratory efforts too weak to reach it fail to trigger a breath (ineffective triggering), a form of patient--ventilator dyssynchrony~\cite{thille2006} that is clinically undesirable~\cite{blanch2015}; such dyssynchrony is detectable on ventilator flow and pressure waveforms but most directly confirmed with specialised monitoring such as esophageal pressure~\cite{akoumianaki2014} or diaphragm electrical activity~\cite{sinderby1999}. Users running atlas-style VC-AC sensitivity sweeps that need lower tidal-volume variability can set the trigger pressure explicitly on every action, with this caveat in mind. Dyssynchrony itself is well documented in ICU patients receiving lower-tidal-volume volume-control ventilation~\cite{figueroacasas2016}; the atlas contributes a Pulse tidal-volume oscillation consistent with it and the $-2$~cmH$_2$O trigger lever for synthetic-data generation, not the clinical discovery of dyssynchrony.

\section{Hyperoxia 300-second convergence}\label{sec:s9}

Section~\mainref{sec:results-hyperoxia}{2.4} of the main manuscript reports that the locked 120-second window is too short for PaO$_2$ convergence at FiO$_2$ = 1.0 in the Healthy cohort: in 300-second reruns, 40 of 44 (90.9~\%) converged to a stable plateau, with a median plateau PaO$_2$ of 643~mmHg over the 40 drift-converged runs, about 20~mmHg below the simple alveolar-gas-equation prediction at sea level ($\approx$~663~mmHg)~\cite{west2020respiratory}. This section provides the kinetic anchor for that claim and shows the convergence trajectories.

\subsection{Late-phase convergence trajectories (Figure~\ref{fig:s9-pao2})}\label{sec:s9-trajectories}

The 60 reruns comprise the 22 Morris points requested at FiO$_2$ = 1.0 that diverged in none of the eight cells, run in both Healthy cells (44 reruns), and the first four of these points in sample order (run indices 6, 7, 12 and 13), run in the two Moderate and two Severe cells (16 reruns). Each S021 rerun re-executes the same configuration from time zero with the recorded duration raised from 120 to 300 seconds, sampled every 5 seconds into the atlas-aggregated CSV. The 120-second ventilator-settling phase that precedes recording is unchanged, so one rerun spans 420~s of Pulse-internal time; all reported times use recorded simulation time ($t = 0$--$300$~s). Because Pulse is deterministic, the aggregated CSVs of all 60 reruns are byte-identical to those of the corresponding 120-second runs over the shared interval $t = 0$--$120$~s; the reruns are deterministic re-executions, not saved-state continuations. Each thin trace is one of the 40 drift-converged Healthy reruns; the thick line is the median of the 37 with a physiological plateau (the three runaways are excluded; \S\ref{sec:s9-guidance}).

\subsection{Practical guidance}\label{sec:s9-guidance}

The kinetic anchor (95~\% of the late-plateau gain reached by $t = 180$~s on the 37 physiological-converged runs) gives an objective threshold for hyperoxia simulations: recording at least 300 seconds and averaging over the final 60 seconds ($t = 240$--$300$~s) places the PaO$_2$ estimate within about 0.1~\% of the value at $t = 300$~s on the median trajectory, and within 1.6~\% for any individual run. The 120-second window of the locked classifier suits HR, MAP and PaCO$_2$, whose drift-threshold exceedances are uncommon at FiO$_2$ $\geq$ 0.95 (\S\ref{sec:s16-window}), but is too short for PaO$_2$ at the inspired-oxygen boundary, where the classifier flags runs as \texttt{implausible} (PaO$_2$ above 600~mmHg) under exactly the conditions where the slow late-phase relaxation has not yet completed.

A separate solver-instability mode appears in extended runs on healthy lungs at FiO$_2$ = 1.0; two runs at FiO$_2$ = 0.737 show the same runaway within the 120-s window (\S\ref{sec:s3-pao2-sensitivity}). In the S021 cohort, 6 of 44 Healthy reruns (14~\%) developed unphysical PaO$_2$ plateaus above 700~mmHg (four near 1{,}400~mmHg, above the 760~mmHg sea-level barometric pressure), all at the single tidal-volume level Vt~=~633.3~mL (6 of the 20 Healthy reruns at that level; none of the 24 at the other three levels, including 0 of 8 at Vt~=~800~mL). Minute ventilation does not separate them: five of the six have RR~=~20 ($\dot{V}_E \approx 12.7$~L/min, about twice the physiological norm of 6--8~L/min) but three other runs at that $\dot{V}_E$ do not run away, and the sixth has RR~=~16 at PEEP~=~0 ($\dot{V}_E \approx 10.1$~L/min), so no input-side ventilation threshold separates affected from unaffected runs. Three of the six stay below the drift threshold and are counted among the 40 drift-converged runs, and three are non-converged; the 44 Healthy reruns thus partition into 37 physiological-converged, 3 runaway-converged, 3 runaway-non-converged and one non-runaway non-converged run, and all six runaways are excluded from the median trajectory of Figure~\ref{fig:s9-pao2}. The locked 120-second window ends before the runaway emerges in these six runs; users running extended-window hyperoxia simulations should therefore screen each PaO$_2$ trajectory individually rather than rely on an input-side ventilation threshold.

What does separate the runaways is the reference body. Across the five input-identical male/female pairs at Vt~=~633.3~mL with RR~$\geq$~16, the female body ran away in 5 of 5 and the male in 1 of 5, and the single male case plateaus at 770~mmHg against 1{,}126--1{,}408~mmHg for the five female cases. This is the opposite direction to the all-male input-extreme divergence cluster of Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript and a further instance of the regime-dependence established in \S\ref{sec:s5-cont}. It is consistent with the systematically higher PaO$_2$ the female body reaches at identical inputs (mean 686 versus 663~mmHg in the locked 120-second window across the four RR~=~20 pairs) and with the sex effect on PaO$_2$ in the Healthy scenario measured by the controlled 2$\times$2 sex$\times$mass design of S011, which held body mass and height fixed across sexes.

The two arms of that design should be weighted differently: $+13.7$~mmHg in the 56.2~kg comparison, where both phenotypes lie within Pulse's stated height and BMI ranges, versus $+21.3$~mmHg in the 67.3~kg comparison, whose synthetic 180~cm female exceeds Pulse's nominal female height ceiling of 175.5~cm and triggers the engine's own model-validity warning; the smaller, fully in-envelope figure is the one to weigh. In the Severe scenario, however, the same in-envelope 56.2~kg comparison reverses: female-minus-male PaO$_2$ = $-1.97$~mmHg ($-3.8$~\%), versus $+3.97$~\% in Healthy, and the 67.3~kg female run diverged outright in Severe, leaving that arm unmeasured. S011 therefore demonstrates a scenario-dependent sensitivity to the sex-conditioned setup at the tested operating point, not a scenario-invariant direction or magnitude of effect. Even the in-envelope arm has three limits: the two bodies are not percentile-matched (163~cm is both Pulse's female 50th-percentile standard and its male 3rd-percentile floor), it was run at a single midrange operating point at FiO$_2$ = 0.6, and Pulse's sex flag switches parameters across several subsystems at once (\S\ref{sec:s10}). The S021 hyperoxia cohort, which contrasts the two standard reference bodies, therefore cannot separate sex from the 11.1~kg body-mass difference in this regime, and no mechanism is claimed.

\subsection{Final-60-s oxygenation across ARDS severity, and the clean-regime FiO$_2$ dose response}\label{sec:s9-shunt}

The 300-second rerun experiment also replayed four paired Morris input vectors on both reference bodies in the Moderate and Severe scenarios at FiO$_2$ = 1.0, alongside the 44-run Healthy hyperoxia set. The per-run mean PaO$_2$ over the final 60~s ($t = 240$--$300$~s) falls monotonically with severity (Table~\ref{tab:s9-shunt}), nearly ninefold from the Healthy median to 73~mmHg in Severe (144~mmHg in Moderate). This pattern is consistent with worsening gas exchange as the alveolar diffusion surface collapses and intrapulmonary-shunt behaviour changes (\S\ref{sec:s5-mapping}), increasingly separating arterial PaO$_2$ from the calculated alveolar oxygen tension at unchanged FiO$_2$ = 1.0. It supports interpreting the elevated Healthy final-60-s oxygenation as a property of the healthy-lung configuration in these simulations rather than a generic FiO$_2$ = 1.0 artefact.

\section{Blood-volume dependence and sex-conditioned setup parameters}\label{sec:s10}

Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript reports that all 20 input-extreme divergences (of 1{,}280 runs) fall on the male reference body, that Pulse's allometric blood-volume formula gives 4{,}802.63~mL for the 67.3~kg male body versus 3{,}996.09~mL for the 56.2~kg female body (a 16.8~\% absolute gap), and that lowering the male \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline} toward its 4{,}082.24~mL setup floor eliminates the divergence cluster (20 / 20 at the default $\rightarrow$ 0 / 20 at both 4{,}400 and 4{,}082.24~mL). This section separates the absolute-volume framing from the weight-normalised framing, because the two answer different methodological questions and a relevant confounder lies between them.

\subsection{Absolute volumes (the framing used in the main manuscript)}\label{sec:s10-abs}

The Pulse allometric formula~\cite{pulsepatientmethod} is $\text{BV} = 65.6 \cdot \text{weight}_{\text{kg}}^{1.02}$~mL. On the two reference patient bodies this yields:

\begin{itemize}
\item Male (67.3~kg): $\text{BV} = 65.6 \cdot 67.3^{1.02} = 4{,}802.63$~mL.
\item Female (56.2~kg): $\text{BV} = 65.6 \cdot 56.2^{1.02} = 3{,}996.09$~mL.
\item Absolute-mL difference: $4{,}802.63 - 3{,}996.09 = 806.54$~mL, or 16.8~\% of the male default.
\end{itemize}

Pulse's setup floor for the male body is 4{,}082.24~mL (the allometric blood volume $\times$ 0.85 in \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp}, a 15~\% reduction below which Pulse requires hypovolemia to be modelled by an explicit condition), 720.39~mL below the male default and 86.15~mL above the female default. A one-way \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline} transplant therefore cannot make the male body blood-volume-equivalent to the female body.

The same gate has an upper arm: \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp} rejects any baseline above the allometric value $\times$ 1.15, with an \texttt{[ERROR]} and exit, exactly as it rejects one below the floor. For the 56.2~kg female reference body, this ceiling is 4{,}595.50~mL, 207.13~mL below the 4{,}802.63~mL male default at which the divergence cluster appears, so the reciprocal transplant is refused too and neither body can be moved to the other's default. This is a property of that body's mass-dependent range, not of female-configured bodies in general: a female-configured body of 67.3~kg computes the same 4{,}802.63~mL default and admissible range, because Pulse's blood-volume relation is not sex-branched.

\subsection{Weight-normalised volumes (the confounder-controlled framing)}\label{sec:s10-norm}

Comparing absolute blood volumes across bodies of different mass conflates sex differences with morphology. Dividing each absolute volume by body weight gives a weight-normalised blood-volume index (mL/kg) that controls for morphology:

\begin{itemize}
\item Male: $4{,}802.63 / 67.3 = 71.4$~mL/kg.
\item Female: $3{,}996.09 / 56.2 = 71.1$~mL/kg.
\item Weight-normalised difference: $0.3$~mL/kg, or $\approx 0.4$~\% of the male value.
\end{itemize}

The 16.8~\% absolute sex gap is therefore driven almost entirely by the body-mass difference (67.3 vs 56.2~kg, a 16.5~\% gap), not by a per-kilogram sex difference in Pulse's calculation. This follows from the formula $\text{BV} = 65.6 \cdot \text{weight}_{\text{kg}}^{1.02}$, which scales blood volume almost linearly with weight (the 1.02 exponent is close to 1.0). This per-kilogram equivalence is a property of Pulse's formula, not a physiological finding: applied to the same two bodies, Nadler's sex-specific equations give 73.0 and 63.8~mL\,kg$^{-1}$, against a clinically reported range of 64--79.3~mL\,kg$^{-1}$ averaging 75 overall and 66 in women~\cite{nadler1962}. Pulse therefore does not reproduce the modest per-kilogram sex difference reported clinically, a limitation of the engine's blood-volume model rather than of the atlas.

\subsection{Interpretation of the divergence finding}\label{sec:s10-disambig}

The sex-stratified divergence finding is best stated in absolute-volume terms, because the quantity on which it depends, Pulse's setup-time \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline}, is parameterised in absolute millilitres, not per kilogram. The reduction from 20 / 20 divergences at the default to 0 / 20 at both 4{,}400 and 4{,}082.24~mL is an absolute-volume effect, not one that a per-kilogram sex difference would predict, since Pulse's allometric relation encodes essentially none.

By the same reasoning, the male--female divergence asymmetry in the input-extreme regime is not a claim about per-kilogram blood-volume physiology in real patients but about how Pulse's solver responds to absolute blood volumes near the male body's setup floor. Pooling male and female Pulse-generated cohorts in machine-learning pipelines without flag-stratified filtering still introduces a spurious sex covariate at the male divergence boundary (Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript), but the result should not be extrapolated to a per-kilogram sex-physiology claim that the data do not support.

\subsection{Output quality of the rescued runs}\label{sec:s10-rescued}

Eliminating divergence concerns one flag only; removing a numerical failure does not guarantee the quality of the trajectory that replaces it. Re-classifying the 20 override reruns with the same four-flag detector finds 12 of 20 \texttt{clean} at 4{,}400~mL and the same 12 at the 4{,}082.24~mL floor, with identical flag assignments; the other 8 retain a flag. Counts are independent presence counts: all 8 fire \texttt{not\_steady\_state}, and one also fires \texttt{implausible}.

Each residual flag corresponds to a mode already characterised in the atlas (the original runs diverged without output, so the comparison is with the atlas's mode taxonomy, not with a pre-override classification of the same configurations). Seven of the eight are flagged for SpO$_2$ flatline at saturation (\S\ref{sec:s2-scope}), three of them also for PaO$_2$ drift beyond the 15~mmHg tolerance over the final 60~s (the window-length artefact of \S\ref{sec:s9}), and three of the eight at 4{,}400~mL (four at the floor) for the delivered-Vt oscillation of \S\ref{sec:s8}. The \texttt{implausible} run, S1\_M\_0159 at FiO$_2$ = 1.0, has a steady-state mean PaO$_2$ of 657~mmHg at 4{,}400~mL and 686~mmHg at the floor, above the locked 600~mmHg ceiling for the same hyperoxia reason. All 8 are at one of the two highest inspired-oxygen levels (three at FiO$_2$ = 0.737, five at 1.0), matching the concentration of the original cluster at the input extremes (FiO$_2$ = 1.0 requested in 13 of the 20).

The override therefore removes the solver failure without making every configuration usable: users re-running these inputs at reduced blood volume must still apply the flag screen. This limits the intervention as a rescue but does not weaken the blood-volume result, since the \texttt{diverged} flag the experiment manipulates falls to zero at both levels.

\subsection{Breadth of the sex-conditioned parameterisation}\label{sec:s10-breadth}

Pulse's blood-volume relation is sex-independent (\S\ref{sec:s10-norm}), but elsewhere in patient setup the sex flag is far from a narrow switch; the sex-conditioned basal-metabolic-rate pathway of \S\ref{sec:s2} is one component of a much wider parameterisation. A source audit of Pulse v4.3.1, restricted to the configuration executed by the atlas (full inventory, with the arithmetic traced to each consumer: \texttt{audit/pulse\_sex\_conditioned\_parameters.md}), finds sex-conditioned quantities in several subsystems:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Body composition.} Standard body-fat fraction 0.21 male versus 0.28 female, which sets adipose tissue mass and, through lean body mass, a normalisation applied to ten further tissue masses.
\item \textbf{Oxygen-carrying capacity.} Total haemoglobin 15.0 versus 14.0~g\,dL$^{-1}$, giving haematocrit 0.4412 versus 0.4118, passed to the saturation calculator and used to initialise the blood gases of every vascular compartment.
\item \textbf{Cardiovascular sizing.} A cardiac-output target of 5{,}600 versus 4{,}900~mL\,min$^{-1}$, from which every regional vascular flow target is derived and, by Ohm's law, every branch resistance.
\item \textbf{Regional perfusion.} Seven flow fractions carry their own sex-specific values on top of that sex-conditioned total.
\item \textbf{Tissue properties.} Thirteen sex-specific tissue masses, feeding tissue volumes, compartment masses, vascular--tissue resistances, permeability and capillary coverage area.
\item \textbf{Anatomic dead space.} No sex branch of its own, but computed as $0.002 \times$ ideal body weight, and Pulse's Devine ideal-body-weight relation is sex-specific (female intercept 4.5~kg below male at any height, hence 9~mL less dead space).
\end{itemize}

Two properties of this parameterisation govern how any cross-body contrast should be read. First, a sex-conditioned setup constant does not necessarily produce a difference in the recorded window: the cardiac-output \emph{field} initialised at 5{,}600 / 4{,}900~mL\,min$^{-1}$ is recomputed from stroke volume and heart rate at the end of every cardiac cycle, and three of the four runtime consumers of basal metabolic rate cancel the sex term exactly at rest, where total metabolic rate equals basal (two divide one by the other, and the third acts only when total exceeds basal).

Second, the direction of a surviving difference depends jointly on the contrast and on the consumer. At equal body mass, the female reference body has about 9.8~\% \emph{more} lung tissue mass than the male despite the smaller sex constant, because the lean-body-mass normalisation reverses the ordering; at the two bodies' native masses, the same quantity is about 8.3~\% lower; and wherever that mass enters as a reciprocal, as in the vascular--tissue resistances, the sign inverts again. A statement such as ``Pulse gives one sex more of quantity $X$'' is therefore ill-defined unless it names the contrast and the consumer.

For the reference-body comparison of Section~\mainref{sec:results-sex}{2.5} of the main manuscript, the consequence is a limit on attribution rather than a new mechanism. The controlled 2$\times$2 sex$\times$mass check retains a gas-exchange contrast when body mass and height are held fixed (\S\ref{sec:s9}), but switching the sex flag changes all of the above at once, so this contrast is the combined effect of the sex-conditioned parameterisation and cannot be assigned to any single pathway (basal metabolic rate, haemoglobin, cardiac output, tissue mass or dead space) without an ablation experiment holding one pathway fixed while the others vary. No such experiment is reported here.

One upstream detail, recorded for completeness and immaterial to every comparison in this work: Pulse uses 88.632 for the male intercept of the basal-metabolic-rate equation where Roza and Shizgal report 88.362~\cite{roza1984metabolic}. On the male reference body the resulting difference is 0.27~kcal\,day$^{-1}$, or 0.015~\%.

\section{Engine-side infrastructure recommendations}\label{sec:s11}

The Discussion of the main manuscript mentions engine-side suggestions for the Pulse maintainers; they are listed below. They derive from operating the engine at scale during the 1{,}280-run sweep and the follow-up experiments, and are offered as observations from user experience rather than physiological prescriptions: each targets tooling or validation infrastructure rather than Pulse's lumped-parameter physiology, which is the domain of the Kitware biomedical-engineering team, and each arises from this work.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Structured input-rejection events.} Pulse currently reports input rejections (e.g.\ SBP $>$ 120, DBP/SBP ratio out of range, HR or RR outside the initialisation band) as \texttt{[ERROR]}-prefixed log lines that downstream tools must parse with regular expressions. A small structured-event API (one typed record per rejected field, with the field name, the offending value and the enforced bound) would let sensitivity-analysis wrappers detect rejections deterministically instead of pattern-matching the regex set documented in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-clamp}.

\item \textbf{A reference sensitivity-analysis recipe for user-facing operational inputs.} The 2020 \texttt{STUDY\_SENSITIVITY\_ANALYSIS\_1\_0\_0} tag of Pulse's repository performs Sobol analysis on internal lumped-parameter circuit values (cardiovascular and respiratory resistances and compliances) for calibration, a different parameter class from the operational inputs (HR, SBP, DBP, RR, Vt, FiO$_2$, PEEP) that downstream users typically need to sweep. A companion recipe (Morris or Sobol over operational inputs) shipped alongside the existing study would give the community a maintained reference implementation; the atlas's sampler and runner modules are an external prototype, and a maintainer-endorsed version would be more durable.

\item \textbf{A setup-time warning for the ARDS impairment-fraction instability band ($[0.879, 0.946]$).} The narrower 0.91--0.945 zone was documented in our preregistration (scenario table and hypothesis H3) and in the verified-limitations table (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s2}, row 4), and corroborated by three boundary probes (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5-prov}); the continuous-severity sweep extends it to 0.879--0.946 (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5-cont}; measured on v4.3.1, untested under v4.4.0), so the warning should cover the full $[0.879, 0.946]$ band. Pulse currently emits no setup-time warning when an ARDS action falls in this band; a single console notice (``impairment fraction in reported instability band, results may not be reliable'') would prevent users from collecting unusable data without blocking legitimate edge-case research.

\item \textbf{Per-channel steady-state convergence diagnostic in CSV output.} The transient hyperoxia regime of Section~\mainref{sec:results-hyperoxia}{2.4} of the main manuscript (PaO$_2$ still rising at the end of the 120-second window at FiO$_2$ = 1.0, with convergence by about 300~s in 40 of 44 Healthy reruns) suggests that an optional per-output ``drift over final 60~s'' column in result CSVs would let users detect non-steady-state runs without writing their own post-processing. The atlas classifier already implements this calculation (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-drift}); promoting it upstream would generalise it.

\item \textbf{Field-attributed setup-error messages.} The current setup-time pulse-pressure-ratio rejection message reports a systolic-versus-diastolic fraction without naming the input to change, so mapping it back to the offending input field requires some domain knowledge (the blood-pressure messages already name the systolic or diastolic baseline). A one-token prefix indicating which input was rejected (\texttt{[ERROR][SystolicArterialPressure]} rather than the unprefixed text) would let downstream users and continuous-integration pipelines route the message without reading it.
\end{enumerate}

None of these suggestions touches Pulse's physiology, ordinary-differential-equation topology or lumped-parameter assumptions, which lie beyond what an external operational-reliability study can credibly prescribe. We offer them, like the atlas itself, as an open contribution to a shared community tool; this work would have been substantially easier had items 1--5 already been in place.

\section{Reproducibility recipe}\label{sec:s12}

The atlas is designed for bit-exact reproduction by an independent investigator. This section gives the minimal end-to-end sequence that regenerates the canonical 1{,}280-run sweep, the audit ledger and the computed quantitative claims of the main manuscript and this supplement. The code and the audit-ledger script are released under the Apache-2.0 license at \url{https://github.com/sadc-lab/pulse_reliability_atlas}; the deposited data carry the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence (CC-BY-4.0).

\subsection{Environment and engine pinning}\label{sec:s12-env}

The atlas pins Pulse v4.3.1 in a single Docker image (\texttt{pulse-atlas-engine:latest}), whose tag was fixed for the entire 1{,}280-run sweep so that every simulation ran against bit-identical engine binaries. The host environment is Python 3.12+ managed with \texttt{uv} for deterministic dependency resolution. The canonical hardware is a laptop-class machine (MSI laptop; Intel i5-11400H, 6 physical / 12 logical cores; 8~GB RAM; integrated Intel UHD Graphics and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Laptop GPU (4~GB); Windows 11 Home, version 25H2). Pulse is a CPU-only simulator and uses neither graphics device; the discrete GPU is listed only so that readers do not infer a CUDA dependence. On this machine, two to three containers can run concurrently; the canonical sweep used three parallel workers, reduced to two mid-sweep to limit thermal load.

The full 1{,}280-run sweep was executed end-to-end three times: twice on the reference machine (the canonical sweep and a later re-execution logged at 23~h~01~m elapsed, including idle intervals across resume points) and once on the independent host of \S\ref{sec:s12-independent}, whose elapsed time coincidentally also totalled 23~h~01~m. Aggregate Pulse container time is about 27~h per sweep on the reference machine and 46~h on the slower independent host.

Two otherwise valid runs (\texttt{S4\_M\_0123} and \texttt{S4\_M\_0124}) have wall-time metadata spanning the same suspension of the reference machine (about 6.84~h), which a naive sum of \texttt{wall\_time\_s} counts twice: summing all 1{,}280 records in \texttt{aggregated.parquet} gives about 41.1~h of container time, versus about 27.4~h when these two timing records are excluded. Subtracting the suspension once from the sweep's 20.78~h calendar span leaves about 13.9~h of active elapsed time. The simulation outputs of both runs are unaffected and are included in every scientific analysis.

\subsection{Three-layer bit-exact verification}\label{sec:s12-verify}

Bit-exact reproduction of the canonical sweep is verified at three independent layers, each against its own committed artefact. Layers 1 and 3 can be re-executed from a fresh clone without re-running the sweep; layer 2, by construction, requires re-executing it:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Canonical Morris sample.} The 160-point Morris design, expanded across the eight scenario $\times$ sex cells into the 1{,}280-run sweep, is regenerated deterministically from the SALib sampler with \texttt{trajectories = 20, levels = 4, seed = 42}; the regenerated sample matches the committed \texttt{data/runs/full\_sweep/morris\_sample.parquet} byte-for-byte.
\item \textbf{Per-cell aggregate parquets.} The 8 per-cell aggregated parquets (\texttt{data/runs/full\_sweep/<scenario>\_<sex>/aggregated.parquet}) reproduce identically when the full sweep is re-executed against the pinned Docker image on the canonical compute envelope.
\item \textbf{Derived statistical artefacts.} The Morris $\mu^{*}$ indices, bootstrap confidence intervals, Wasserstein distances, Spearman correlations, and BH-FDR survivors all regenerate bit-equivalently from the canonical aggregated parquets; this is what the audit ledger (\texttt{scripts/audit\_claims.py}) re-derives at every run.
\end{enumerate}

The audit ledger is the primary reproducibility instrument: a passing run confirms that the computed quantitative claims of the main manuscript and supplement are consistent with the canonical artefacts. The ledger targets 394 anchors. On the authors' full data tree, all 394 pass; on a public clone, one anchor (T1.06u, the S021 time to 95~\% of the plateau, which requires raw per-run trajectories excluded from version control) is skipped automatically, giving 393/394 PASS with one reported SKIPPED. A failed claim produces a structured triage item (Type A transcription drift, Type B orphaned number, Type C derivation mismatch) with a file:line pointer for tracing.

\subsection{Independent reproduction on separate hardware}\label{sec:s12-independent}

Beyond the three-layer re-derivation above, which regenerates the analysis from the committed canonical artefacts, a co-author (Y.M.) re-executed the end-to-end pipeline from scratch on a second x86\_64 host (AMD Ryzen~5 CPU, Linux under WSL2), distinct from the canonical Intel x86\_64 / Windows reference environment.

First, the full 1{,}280-run Morris sweep was re-run there (two parallel Docker workers; wall time 23~h~01~m). All four run-level flags and the derived clean status matched on 1{,}280/1{,}280 runs, every continuous steady-state value was bit-identical, and only three derived H4 statistics differed at $\sim 10^{-14}$ floating-point round-off, with an identical Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate survivor set.

Second, the 22-run Healthy-M pilot was re-run on the same host with per-run output capture. Its aggregate was bit-identical to the canonical pilot ($\max |\Delta| = 0$ across all six channel means) with 100\,\% concordance of the four flags and the derived clean status on 22/22 runs.

Both reproductions are archived under \texttt{audits/}: \texttt{reproduction\_2026-06-07/} carries the reproduced full-sweep aggregate and the derived analysis outputs, with its numerical comparison report beside it (\texttt{reproduction\_check\_2026-06-07.md}); \texttt{reproduction\_thinkpad\_2026-06-10/} carries a provenance README (host, Docker image tag and digest, pinned Pulse v4.3.1, exact commands), the 20 per-run output CSVs (the two deliberate clamp probes write none), and the comparison tables.

\subsection{Reproducibility scope and limits}\label{sec:s12-scope}

The verification and independent reproductions above span the Intel x86\_64 reference environment and an AMD x86\_64 host, with bit-identical simulation outputs, which is empirical evidence that bit-identity holds across CPU vendor within the x86\_64 family. On both hosts the engine ran in a Linux container under WSL2; only the Python layer ran under a different operating system (Windows versus Linux). Cross-\emph{architecture} reproduction was probed but \emph{not} established. The 22-run Healthy-M pilot was also re-executed on Y.M.'s Apple Silicon host (MacBook Air, Apple~M2, ARM64; macOS~26.5.1; archived under \texttt{audits/reproduction\_macos\_2026-06-13/}), but this is \emph{not} a native ARM64 test: the pinned base image \texttt{kitware/pulse:4.3.1} is published for \texttt{linux/amd64} only, so the engine ran as \emph{emulated} x86\_64 under Docker Desktop's virtualisation layer (the container reports \texttt{x86\_64}, with an explicit \texttt{amd64}-vs-\texttt{arm64} platform-mismatch warning).

Under emulation, the pilot reproduced the canonical aggregate to $\max|\Delta| = 2.9\times10^{-9}$ across all six channel means, with 100\,\% concordance of the four flags and the derived clean status on 22/22 runs: near machine precision, but \emph{not} bit-identical (the native x86\_64 reproductions agree to $\max|\Delta| = 0$). The residual is attributable to floating-point handling in the x86$\rightarrow$ARM emulation layer rather than to a natively compiled ARM64 build. A genuine native-ARM64 reproduction would require compiling Pulse~v4.3.1 from source for \texttt{linux/arm64} (no prebuilt ARM64 image exists) and remains future work; the Apple Silicon run establishes only that the pipeline executes end-to-end on that platform and that emulated x86\_64 execution preserves the canonical outputs to within $\sim 10^{-9}$.

Bit-identity within an architecture family is expected given Pulse's fixed 20~ms time step and standard math-library usage, and is now empirically supported across two x86\_64 CPU vendors; cross-architecture bit-identity is plausible but not guaranteed without explicit verification. Image immutability is currently anchored at the Docker tag level (\texttt{pulse-atlas-engine:latest} plus the pinned Pulse v4.3.1 Git tag); for archival re-execution five or more years from now, pinning the Docker image by cryptographic digest (\texttt{@sha256:...}) is more robust and is recommended.

\section{SHAP cross-check of the Morris top-three input sets}\label{sec:s13}

This section reports a complementary cross-check: a machine-learning analysis of the clean subset of the same Pulse-generated data, following a different statistical route, recovers largely the same top-three input sets as the preregistered Morris elementary-effects design.

\subsection{Methodology: epistemological scope}\label{sec:s13-scope}

The Morris elementary-effects design is interventional: each trajectory perturbs one input at a time along a fixed grid, so $\mu^{*}$ measures how Pulse responds to deliberate perturbations of each input (apart from the pulse-pressure clip, which breaks strict one-at-a-time steps on some SBP and DBP transitions; \S\ref{sec:s3-clip-cost}). A SHAP analysis~\cite{lundberg2017} of a tree-based surrogate model trained on the observed distribution of Morris outputs is a fundamentally different statistical object: it summarises how a black-box predictor associates input features with predictions on the data distribution as observed. The two methods answer different questions; the interventional Morris design is the primary analysis, and the SHAP analysis is included only as a secondary cross-check.

\subsection{Cross-verification result on the clean subset}\label{sec:s13-result}

A supplementary random-forest regressor with SHAP feature-importance ranking was fitted on the $n = 663$ clean runs (no \texttt{clamped}, \texttt{diverged}, \texttt{implausible} or \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flag): one forest per output, trained on the pooled clean subset with \texttt{sex} and \texttt{scenario} as additional features. SHAP values were computed exactly for tree ensembles with \texttt{TreeExplainer}~\cite{lundberg2020tree} rather than approximated by sampling. Per-(scenario, sex) rankings were obtained by partitioning the global model's SHAP values by cell and re-ranking, not by fitting per-cell models, whose per-cell $n$ (42--144 clean runs) is too small for stable training.

The SHAP top-three input set was compared with the Morris $\mu^{*}$ top-three set in the same cells as a set overlap (the number of shared inputs), because the comparison targets top-three membership, not the complete ordering. Three summary statistics characterise the agreement:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Across the six pooled output-level comparisons, the Morris and SHAP top-three sets share 17 of 18 possible input memberships: five outputs match 3/3 and PaO$_2$ matches 2/3} (ledger anchor \texttt{T2.s023.pooled}).
\item \textbf{23 of 48 per-cell (scenario $\times$ sex $\times$ output) cases share all three inputs} (ledger anchor \texttt{T2.s023.fullmatch}).
\item \textbf{92~\% of per-cell comparisons share at least two of the three} (44 of 48; ledger anchor \texttt{T2.s023.ge2\_pct}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Interpretation}\label{sec:s13-interp}

The pooled agreement (17 of 18) is the central result: a SHAP analysis of the clean data recovers largely the same dominant inputs as the interventional Morris design across the six outputs. These six are not independent evidence, however: every output comes from the same runs, and Pulse computes SpO$_2$ from the oxygen partial pressure through the haemoglobin dissociation relation, which also takes PaCO$_2$ and pH, so three of the six are coupled by construction. The lower per-cell full-match rate (23 of 48) is expected, because per-cell samples are smaller (42--144 clean runs) and SHAP attribution depends on the observed input--output distribution within each cell, which is heavily influenced by which Morris trajectories happened to remain clean. The 92~\% figure counts cells sharing at least two of the three inputs; it is a partial overlap, not a preserved ranking.

This cross-check assesses the robustness of the Morris top-three sets to an alternative analysis of the same data; it is neither external physiological validation nor an independent replication, since both analyses read the same simulated runs. What the agreement supports is bounded: the Morris-derived top-three sets are not specific to the elementary-effects estimator, because a data-driven, observational, tree-based route selects largely the same inputs on the same clean subset. Two further limits apply: the surrogate is fitted on the entire clean subset, so its training fit says nothing about predictive accuracy (out-of-sample estimates follow in \S\ref{sec:s13-oos}), and the comparison concerns selected inputs, not their order.

\subsection{Out-of-sample performance of the surrogate}\label{sec:s13-oos}

Because the surrogate is fitted on the whole clean subset, its training $R^{2}$ (0.993 to 0.99997) is uninformative for a forest grown to purity, so two out-of-sample estimates were computed (Table~\ref{tab:s13-oos}). The out-of-bag score, which a bootstrapped forest yields for free, ranges from 0.949 to 0.9998 but is optimistic here: it treats rows as exchangeable, whereas consecutive points of a Morris trajectory differ in only one of seven inputs, so a held-out row almost always has a near-twin in the bag. The honest estimate holds out \emph{whole trajectories}, using grouped cross-validation over the 20 trajectories with four complete trajectories withheld at a time. Because the same 160-point design is replayed in all eight cells, a group is a trajectory across cells, not within one; grouping per cell would place identical input vectors on both sides of the split. The run-to-trajectory mapping is verified against \texttt{morris\_sample.parquet} on all 1{,}280 rows before grouping, and the analysis aborts if any row disagrees.

The surrogate generalises to unseen trajectories, but unevenly, in a physiologically coherent pattern. Delivered tidal volume and heart rate stay above 0.99 with almost no fold-to-fold spread; a single input nearly determines these channels. The three blood-gas channels average 0.87, with a fold-to-fold spread twenty to thirty times that of heart rate, and they are also where the out-of-bag estimate is most optimistic (by 0.078 to 0.087, against 0.0003 for delivered Vt). Both measures agree: gas exchange depends jointly on several inputs, so it benefits most from a near-twin in the training fold and suffers most when whole trajectories are withheld.

Pooled predictive accuracy does not, however, license reading the per-cell agreement as confirmation of per-cell structure. Partitioning one global model's SHAP values by cell yields far less between-cell variation than the interventional design: across the eight cells, the SHAP top-three sets take nine distinct values in total (ten under two other forest seeds) versus 25 for Morris, and for three of the six outputs (MAP, SpO$_2$ and PaCO$_2$) SHAP returns the same trio in all eight cells. Per-cell agreement is therefore partly mechanical: when one estimator is almost invariant, agreement measures how closely the other stays to that invariant answer. The pooled comparison (17 of 18), computed once per output rather than per cell, does not share this defect. The 23 of 48 full-set agreement is stable across three independent forest seeds, so these figures characterise the ranking comparison, not the surrogate's randomness.

\subsection{Sensitivity of the top-three sets to the clean-pair screen}\label{sec:s13-screen}

The indices reported throughout this work use only elementary-effect pairs with \emph{both} endpoints four-flag clean, as preregistered. This screen keeps 663 of the 1{,}280 runs and 454 of the 1{,}120 available pairs, which raises the question of whether the rankings reflect the engine or the screen. The question can be answered with the recorded data by re-deriving the same indices under a second screen; the SHAP cross-check above cannot answer it, because its surrogate is trained on the same 663 runs.

The second screen keeps every pair whose endpoints fired neither \texttt{diverged} nor \texttt{implausible}: 1{,}134 runs and 969 pairs. \textbf{This is not a less-biased estimator and is not reported as one.} The 471 additional runs are exactly those whose only flag is \texttt{not\_steady\_state}, i.e.\ runs whose steady-state mean is not a valid summary. Admitting them trades a selection bias for a measurement error rather than reducing bias, and the extra data are most abundant where they are least trustworthy: of the 3{,}090 additional elementary-effect observations, 1{,}152 fall in Healthy, the scenario with the worst delivered-volume oscillation. What follows is therefore a sensitivity analysis on the treatment of non-stationary runs, not a revised estimate.

Two results are separated because they answer different questions. \emph{First, what does not change.} Hypothesis~1 holds in all 48 cell $\times$ output combinations under both screens: the top three inputs carry 0.658 to 0.998 of the summed $\mu^{*}$ under the locked screen (median 0.928) and 0.695 to 0.994 under the relaxed one (median 0.901), so the concentration on which H1 rests is not an artefact of run survival (its minimum even rises). The rank-1 input is also unchanged in all 30 combinations whose candidate set is the same under both screens.

\emph{Second, what does change.} Relaxing the screen makes FiO$_2$ estimable in Healthy-M, Healthy-F and Severe-F, where every FiO$_2$ pair had a flagged endpoint, so 18 of the 48 combinations gain a seventh candidate and their top three is no longer a like-for-like comparison. On the 30 combinations whose candidate set is unchanged, the top-three \emph{set} is preserved in 19 and its \emph{order} in 15. Those are reorderings, not ties: the median gap between the third and fourth $\mu^{*}$ is 39~\% of the third under the locked screen, and only one of the 30 combinations has a gap below 10~\%. Counted over all 48 combinations, with a changed candidate set counted as a change, the figures are 41, 26 and 21.

For scale, the design's own seed sensitivity is of the same order. Replaying cell Mild-M with an independent Morris sample seed (seed 44, \S\,\ref{sec:s3-resample}) preserves the rank-1 input in all 6 outputs, the top-three set in 4 and its order in 3; the screen change preserves 6, 2 and 2 on the same outputs. One cell and six outputs is a benchmark rather than an equivalence, but it places the screen effect alongside a perturbation the design already carries. The defensible reading is that, under both perturbations, rank~1 is more stable than ranks two and three, and the full ordering is not resolved by this design, which is why Figure~3 is presented as a map of where influence concentrates rather than as a stable ordering.

Re-deriving under the locked screen reproduces the published indices exactly (the same 318 (cell, output, input) rows with $\mu^{*}$ identical to double precision, the same H1 range and median, and the same twelve single-pair entries in the same cells, nine of them in a top three), so the comparison is between two screens, not two pipelines. \subsection{Physiological interpretation and Morris-EE implications}\label{sec:s15-mechanism}

In the Healthy scenario, mean output HR is about 74~bpm and still follows the requested input: input HR is the rank-1 input for output HR in every cell (Section~\mainref{sec:results-morris}{2.2} of the main manuscript). In Moderate (impairment 0.60) and Severe (0.95), mean output HR settles at 145--160~bpm despite identical input sampling, about 70~bpm above the midpoint of the $[50, 110]$~bpm input range. This is consistent with the engine's documented baroreceptor and chemoreceptor reflexes, although the atlas did not record or isolate their contribution. No scenario administers a sedative or a neuromuscular-blocking drug. ARDS severity therefore shifts output HR strongly upward, while input HR remains a dominant within-scenario determinant.

The overshoot is non-monotonic in severity: output HR peaks at Moderate and eases at Severe (Table~\ref{tab:s15-comphr}). Because Severe has markedly fewer clean Morris runs than Moderate, this apparent easing may partly reflect differential clean-run retention; the data cannot distinguish such a compositional effect from a change in the modelled physiological response.

In the ARDS scenarios, the measured $\mu^{*}$ of input HR on output HR therefore describes the response within a scenario whose compensatory state is already shifted, not the steady-state effect of a resting heart rate. The H1 result that cardiovascular outputs are HR-dominated remains robust, but it means that input HR is a dominant within-scenario determinant of output HR, not that ``the input HR is the steady-state HR.'' Users running Pulse for ARDS device validation should treat output HR as the engine's scenario-dependent steady-state response, not as a setpoint imposed by the input.

\section{Output stability across the 120-second measurement window}\label{sec:s16-window}

Each run is reduced to one value per output: the mean over the final 60~s of the 120~s recording. This reduction is meaningful only if the output has settled, i.e.\ is neither drifting, nor still oscillating, nor frozen against a physiological bound. A mean taken while an output is still moving records \emph{when} the run was sampled rather than \emph{how} the input acted, and a mean taken from an output frozen against a bound may reflect numerical pinning rather than physiology; where the bound is physiologically attainable, the classifier cannot distinguish the two (\S\ref{sec:s4}). Runs failing any of these tests are flagged rather than discarded (Section~\mainref{sec:methods-detector}{4.4} of the main manuscript), which keeps the assumption auditable.

Two channels are unsettled at the edges of the operational envelope: PaO$_2$ drifts at FiO$_2$ = 1.0 (Section~\ref{sec:s9}), and delivered tidal volume oscillates under the default VC-AC trigger (Section~\ref{sec:s8}). To test whether these localised instabilities contaminate the other steady-state channels, we computed, for every run in each vulnerable population, the drift $|\Delta_{60\mathrm{s}}|$ of each output over the final 60~s (the same preregistered ordinary-least-squares slope $\times$ 60~s used by the \texttt{not\_steady\_state} detector; Section~\ref{sec:s3-drift}) and compared it with the locked per-channel thresholds.

In the hyperoxia arm (FiO$_2 \geq 0.95$; 195 runs with valid output), drift stays within threshold for the cardiovascular and carbon-dioxide channels: heart rate exceeds its 5~bpm threshold in only 2.6~\% of runs (median 0.20~bpm), MAP in 0~\% (median 0.05~mmHg) and PaCO$_2$ in 3.6~\% (median 0.58~mmHg). Only oxygenation and tidal-volume delivery are materially affected: PaO$_2$ exceeds its 15~mmHg threshold in 19.5~\% of runs (median 4.3~mmHg, 95th percentile 40.2~mmHg) and delivered Vt in 8.7~\%. In the tidal-volume-oscillation arm (delivered-Vt CV $>$ 0.05; 303 runs), the oscillation likewise does not propagate: heart rate exceeds its drift threshold in 2.3~\% of runs, MAP in 0.7~\%, PaO$_2$ in 7.3~\% and PaCO$_2$ in 2.0~\%, so Pulse buffers the breath-to-breath volume swing upstream of the cardiovascular and gas-exchange loops.

In these two populations, window-length sensitivity was thus concentrated in PaO$_2$ and delivered Vt, and threshold exceedances were uncommon for HR, MAP and PaCO$_2$; the audit does not cover the rest of the sweep. The sensitive channels lie at the FiO$_2$ = 1.0 and high-oscillation extremes, precisely the conditions characterised in Sections~\ref{sec:s9} and~\ref{sec:s8}. The per-arm channel breakdown, the dual-vulnerability intersection and the underlying S012 experiment log are given in the companion technical monograph.
