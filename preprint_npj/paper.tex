\abstract{Pulse is an actively maintained open-source adult physiology simulator, yet no formal sensitivity analysis of its operational inputs has been published. We present the first reliability atlas across Pulse's critical-care envelope: Morris elementary-effects sampling of seven inputs, four severity scenarios, two bodies (1{,}280 simulations). Sensitivity is skewed: three inputs dominate each output (median 93~\% of total sensitivity). A four-flag classifier (clamping, divergence, implausible, non-steady-state) surfaces failure modes: two operator-actionable, a third sex-dependent. Tidal-volume instability (20.6~\% of runs) reflects patient--ventilator dyssynchrony; locking the VC-AC inspiration trigger to $-2$~cmH$_2$O makes Vt reproducible. At FiO$_2$~=~1.0 transient hyperoxia makes 120-second runs underestimate plateau PaO$_2$; 300-second runs give a median 643~mmHg. Solver divergence fell entirely on the male body (20/1{,}280); reducing its blood-volume baseline to the 4{,}082~mL floor eliminated it. The atlas maps operational sensitivity and review-priority regions, and releases an Apache-2.0-licensed curation layer for synthetic cohort construction.}

\keywords{Pulse Physiology Engine, digital twin, synthetic patient data, Morris elementary effects, sensitivity analysis, uncertainty quantification, ARDS, mechanical ventilation, VC-AC ventilation, critical care, machine learning training data, operational envelope, reproducibility}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Digital twins for health (virtual, often mechanistic models of human physiology used for in-silico trials, medical-device design, and synthetic-patient generation) are proliferating across healthcare~\cite{katsoulakis2024digitaltwins}. The Pulse Physiology Engine (Kitware, Apache-2.0) is one such actively maintained open-source whole-body human physiology simulator~\cite{bray2019}. It uses a lumped-parameter approach (modelling physiological systems as small networks of coupled compartments rather than full three-dimensional anatomy) to deliver real-time cardiovascular, respiratory, gas-exchange, and pharmacological simulation. The Pulse Physiology Engine has been applied to ventilator-safety what-if analysis~\cite{webb2020}, the validation of its own mechanical-ventilator model against a physical reference simulator~\cite{webb2022vent}, and as a general-purpose computational physiology platform~\cite{bray2019}, alongside external statistical assessments~\cite{cornish2023} and independent use of its respiratory dynamics as a source of simulated data for learning and benchmarking ventilator-control policies in Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)~\cite{ward2024ventilator}. Yet despite this footprint, no public map of how the engine responds across its user-facing \emph{input} envelope (which inputs dominate which outputs, where inputs are silently clamped, and where the solver diverges) has been published. Most of that work probes Pulse's \emph{outputs}: validating them against reference data, or, in one external study, falsifying intervention--outcome behaviour on a real cohort. Webb et al.'s ventilator-sharing study~\cite{webb2020} does sweep inputs (12{,}642 unique patient and ventilator combinations spanning respiratory compliance, diffusion impairment and positive end-expiratory pressure) and does rank parameters by outcome correlation. That ranking, however, is over the \emph{mismatch} between two equal-sized 70~kg bodies sharing one ventilator, scored against a single composite safety outcome, under pressure-mode ventilation with respiratory rate, I:E ratio and respiratory resistance held fixed. It resolves neither each input's influence on each physiological output, nor input clamping, nor solver divergence. Each downstream user re-encounters the same engine limitations independently, and each validation pipeline re-derives its own working envelope by trial and error.

The Pulse maintainers state on the project's public FAQ that ``we have not performed a systematic forward propagation or inverse quantification of model uncertainty, nor have we conducted a formal sensitivity analysis''~\cite{pulsefaq}. The upstream repository carries a single 2020 sensitivity study (\texttt{STUDY\_SENSITIVITY\_ANALYSIS\_1\_0\_0}), but it targets internal lumped-parameter circuit values (cardiovascular and respiratory resistances and compliances) for model calibration: a different parameter class from the user-facing operational inputs that downstream applications actually specify. The only prior external assessment of Pulse on a clinical dataset, by Cornish et al.~\cite{cornish2023}, applied a causal-falsification framework to MIMIC-III sepsis trajectories. They report intervention-response errors concentrated on electrolyte, metabolic, acid--base and dermal outputs, which lie largely outside the present atlas's six-output cardio-respiratory panel. What remains unaddressed is the complementary question: how Pulse's outputs and numerical stability behave when its primary user-facing inputs are swept across the full range of values the engine accepts.

Three implementation choices determine what a reliability atlas of this engine can resolve. Pulse represents each physiological system as a lumped-parameter circuit of compartments joined by paths carrying idealised elements (resistances, capacitances, diodes), and solves the resulting non-linear networks with one generic circuit solver shared across systems~\cite{pulsecircuit}. It advances on a fixed transient-analysis time step of 0.02~s (50~Hz), recalculating every system state at each step~\cite{pulsesystem,bray2019}. Execution is deterministic, so one configuration replays to one trajectory, which is what allows a per-cell curation map to be reproduced rather than merely reported (Supplement~\S\ref{sec:s12}). Patients and diseases enter at setup rather than continuously. Body dimensions drive allometric baselines (quantities that scale with body size), bounded by hard acceptance limits~\cite{pulsepatientmethod}. A single ARDS severity scalar on [0,\,1] then drives the respiratory modifiers, through piecewise-linear functions calibrated at five nodes: 0.0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9 and 1.0~\cite{pulserespmethod}. That is why the atlas samples severity at fixed points rather than sweeping it. The lung itself is five compartments: the carina, left and right anatomic dead space, and left and right alveoli~\cite{pulserespmethod}. Pulse describes its own approach as ``a low fidelity clinical approach\ldots not necessarily for high fidelity predictive purposes''~\cite{pulsesystem}, which is precisely why characterising how that approximation behaves across the operational envelope is worthwhile.

We address this gap with the first open reliability atlas across Pulse's adult critical-care operational envelope. We swept the seven user-facing inputs --- heart rate (HR), systolic and diastolic blood pressure (SBP, DBP), respiratory rate (RR), tidal volume (Vt), inspired oxygen fraction (FiO$_2$), and positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) --- by running the engine repeatedly while varying these inputs across their accepted ranges; this full batch of runs is what we call the \emph{sweep}. We tracked six steady-state outputs --- HR, mean arterial pressure (MAP), tidal volume delivered, peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO$_2$), arterial oxygen partial pressure (PaO$_2$), and arterial carbon dioxide partial pressure (PaCO$_2$) --- across four clinical scenarios (S1 healthy baseline, impairment fraction 0; and three ARDS severities: S2 mild, 0.26; S3 moderate, 0.60; S4 severe, 0.95) and two adult reference patient bodies (male: 18~y, 67.3~kg, 180~cm; female: 18~y, 56.2~kg, 163~cm). Each run is reduced to one value per output: the mean over the final 60~s of the 120~s simulation, a window in which the output has settled, meaning it is neither drifting, nor still oscillating, nor frozen against a physiological bound. A mean taken while an output is still moving would record when the run was sampled rather than how the input acted, and a mean taken from an output frozen against a bound records the bound rather than the physiology, so runs that fail any of these tests are flagged rather than discarded, which keeps the assumption auditable (Methods~\S\ref{sec:methods-detector}). Each of the three ARDS impairment fractions is calibrated so that, at a reference protective-ventilation setting, Pulse's arterial PaO$_2$/FiO$_2$ falls in the matching Berlin Definition band. An atlas cell is therefore anchored to a clinically recognisable state rather than to an arbitrary engine setting (Methods~\S\ref{sec:methods-sweep}; Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5-berlin}). Inputs are screened with Morris elementary-effects sampling~\cite{morris1991,campolongo2007}, a one-at-a-time perturbation design that ranks each input's influence on each output and flags non-linearity (Methods~\S\ref{sec:methods-sweep}). Every simulation is then classified by a four-flag steady-state detector. The four flags record whether Pulse silently clamped an out-of-range input, the solver diverged, a steady-state output fell outside hard physiological bounds, or the run never settled within the simulation window. Crucially, the detector categorizes failures rather than discarding them, so the atlas stays complete across the full input grid. Three findings emerge from the sweep. Two are operator-actionable corrections: a tidal-volume dyssynchrony lever for volume-controlled assist-control (VC-AC) ventilation, applied at the inspiration trigger, and a longer steady-state window that corrects hyperoxia simulation at FiO$_2$~=~1.0. The third is a previously uncharacterized, sex-stratified cluster of solver divergence: a distinct reliability axis, traced to a blood-volume mechanism.

The atlas is organised around three questions that decide whether a Pulse-generated cohort is fit for downstream use: \emph{how} strongly each input drives each output (and \emph{where} that influence turns non-linear), \emph{whether} the engine silently clamps an out-of-range setting, and \emph{whether} the solver diverges or never reaches steady state. Morris screening answers the first question (input influence and non-linearity), while the four-flag detector answers the other two: silent clamping and solver stability. We centre the sweep on the ARDS severity axis for two reasons. First, ventilating a patient with impaired lungs is one of the main things Pulse's respiratory model is used for, and it is also the regime whose downstream reliability is least documented. Second, mapping the adult engine's sensitivity and failure modes is the prerequisite for adapting it to pediatric ARDS, the setting in which we ultimately want simulated cohorts to support machine-learning prediction of ventilation-dependent blood gases in critically ill children: pediatric respiratory dynamics differ from adult ones and cannot be safely derived from an unmapped adult substrate. The result is therefore not a single benchmark score but a per-cell curation map and a reusable run-level classifier. Downstream users, including the machine-learning and clinical-AI pipelines that draw on synthetic patient cohorts~\cite{ward2024ventilator}, can use the run-level labels to identify simulations requiring exclusion, remediation or review, and the cell-level map to prioritise scenario-specific curation, without re-running the sweep. Neither layer constitutes external physiological validation, and neither is a distribution-neutral automatic filter (Table~\ref{tab:screening-retention}). The pipeline is preregistered, reproducible from a single Docker image with a fixed seed, and released open-source under Apache-2.0.

\subsection{Overview of the reliability atlas}\label{sec:results-overview}

The atlas comprises 1{,}280 simulations spanning eight cells of 160 runs each, where each \textit{cell} is a unique pairing of one clinical scenario --- S1 (healthy baseline), S2 (mild), S3 (moderate), or S4 (severe) --- with one patient sex (male or female), giving the $4 \times 2 = 8$ cells (Figure~\ref{fig:design} maps the full study design). Each simulation is classified by a four-flag steady-state detector: \texttt{clamped} flags whether Pulse refused a submitted setup input as given; \texttt{diverged} flags solver failure or non-finite output; \texttt{implausible} flags a run that completed with a real, finite value (no solver error) but whose steady-state mean lies outside the hard physiological bounds (a value no patient could hold in a stable steady state); and \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flags a run that never settled: over its final 60-second measurement window the output is still trending (\emph{drift}) or still oscillating (\emph{variance}) beyond the thresholds locked in preregistration (Table~\ref{tab:flag_defs} summarises the four flags). Two implausibility bounds carry nuance: an SpO$_2$ above 100~\% is impossible by construction, while a \emph{sustained} mean SpO$_2$ below 50~\%, though briefly survivable in a real patient, is incompatible with a stable simulated steady state over the evaluation window~\cite{severinghaus1979}; and the PaO$_2$ upper bound of 600~mmHg is deliberately conservative, set below the alveolar-gas-equation value of $\approx$663~mmHg at FiO$_2$ = 1.0~\cite{west2020respiratory}, so runs above it are revisited in the hyperoxia analysis (\S\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia}) rather than dismissed. The four flags are independent presence counts (a single run can fire several), and Table~\ref{tab:flag_summary} reports the per-cell breakdown. Three of the four preregistered hypotheses (H1--H4) are settled by this table alone. H2 predicted that at least 5~\% of the Morris sample would hit a silent clamp. Under its preregistered operational detector the observed rate is 0 of 1{,}280, so H2 is \emph{not} supported. That detector reads the setup log, so it identifies explicit rejection and cannot by itself establish the absence of a silent substitution. A separate post hoc audit, using a setup-time reading of ``silent clamp'', compared every value submitted to the engine with the value Pulse serialises after ingestion and found exact agreement for all 10{,}160 input contracts that reached ingestion. It points the same way as the preregistered result without retroactively changing what that detector measured. The remaining 80 ventilator contracts belong to 20 runs that failed during patient stabilisation before the ventilator action was submitted; they enter neither numerator nor denominator (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}). Both results are bounded by the sampled envelope: values submitted to Pulse were restricted to its verified setup envelope, with the pulse-pressure constraint applied by the wrapper before submission. The sweep therefore does not establish how Pulse handles the full clinically plausible adult-ICU range outside that envelope. H3 predicted at least one solver-divergence zone outside the previously characterised ARDS instability band (impairment 0.91--0.945); divergence occurs on 20 runs spread across all four scenarios, including the healthy baseline and both sub-severe ARDS settings, so H3 is supported. All 20 fall on the male reference body (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}), a sex stratification the hypothesis did not anticipate. That same asymmetry settles H4, whose preregistered criterion is a sex difference in clamping, divergence \emph{or} $\mu^{*}$ ranking: divergence alone satisfies it, and \S\ref{sec:results-sex} characterises how far the difference extends. H1 (skewed sensitivity) is evaluated in \S\ref{sec:results-morris}.

Three features of Table~\ref{tab:flag_summary} carry the headline results. First, silent clamping never happens (0 / 1{,}280 across every cell): Pulse rejects an out-of-range input loudly, with a setup error, instead of quietly bending it back into range. Second, solver divergence is sex-specific: 20 / 1{,}280 runs (1.6~\%) failed to converge, and all 20 were on the male reference body (we trace the cause in \S\ref{sec:results-sex}). Third, \texttt{not\_steady\_state} runs cluster in two regimes: healthy adults (S1), where large swings in the input tidal volume outrun Pulse's VC-AC controller, and severe ARDS (S4), where the fixed 120-second window is too short for the slow convergence of near-shunt physiology (both are detailed in \S\ref{sec:results-vcac} and \S\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia}). Overall, 663 of 1{,}280 runs (51.8~\%) are flag-clean under the locked classifier, and the per-cell traffic-light map (Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light}) gives the combined curation and review-priority picture at a glance. Beyond these headline results, the sweep yields a set of further analyses, each fully documented in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s17-summary}: a finer mapping of the ARDS solver-instability band, the closure of the preregistered optional Sobol analysis (no cell cleared its promotion trigger once sampling uncertainty was accounted for), a breakdown of which runs fail the \texttt{not\_steady\_state} test and why, six concrete engineering recommendations for the Pulse maintainers, and tables comparing each input's importance ranking between the male and female bodies (cross-sex Spearman $\rho$).

\noindent\textbf{Screening retention and cohort composition.}
Screening retention is strongly cell-dependent, and it depends on which screen is applied (Table~\ref{tab:screening-retention}). Under the strict four-flag definition, 663 of 1{,}280 simulations (51.8~\%) are clean, with per-cell retention ranging from 42/160 in S1\_F to 144/160 in S3\_M, a 3.43-fold spread. Treating \texttt{not\_steady\_state} as a review-and-remediation label rather than an automatic exclusion instead leaves 1{,}134 hard-failure-free simulations (88.6~\%), with per-cell retention ranging from 106/160 in S4\_M to 160/160 in S2\_F and S3\_F, a 1.51-fold spread. That second subset must not be called clean: it still contains non-stationary trajectories, usable only after review, window extension, or protocol correction.

The two screens deplete opposite ends of the severity axis. The four-flag screen is most restrictive on the healthy cells (S1\_F and S1\_M); the hard-failure-free screen is most restrictive on the severe-ARDS cells (S4\_M and S4\_F). Choosing a screening rule therefore chooses which clinical regimes a downstream cohort under-represents. Neither subset is distribution-neutral, so downstream studies should report per-cell retention and cohort composition before and after screening.

The 471 runs gained by moving from the strict-clean subset to the hard-failure-free subset are exactly those carrying \texttt{not\_steady\_state} as their sole flag; together with 20 \texttt{diverged} and 126 \texttt{implausible} runs they account for all 617 non-clean runs, which also establishes that \texttt{diverged} and \texttt{implausible} are disjoint in this sweep. \texttt{clamped} is a provided category that is empty here (0 / 1{,}280); had a clamped run occurred, it would have been unfit to represent the input point that was requested.

\subsection{Primary input-sensitivity rankings}\label{sec:results-morris}

Figure~\ref{fig:morris} shows the Morris $\mu^{*}$ (mean of absolute elementary effects, the input-importance metric) for each of the six outputs across the eight cells and the seven swept inputs. Our first hypothesis (H1) was preregistered before any data were collected: in every cell, the top-three inputs should account for at least 50~\% of the summed $\mu^{*}$ on each output. This is a ``skewed sensitivity'' pattern --- the influence is concentrated in a few dominant inputs (which differ by output --- e.g.\ inspired oxygen drives PaO$_2$, tidal volume drives PaCO$_2$) rather than spread evenly across all seven. The data support H1 on 48 of 48 cell $\times$ output combinations: for the typical output the three most influential inputs together account for about 93~\% of the summed $\mu^{*}$ (median top-3 fraction of 0.928, range 0.658--0.998), leaving the remaining inputs only a small share. This skewed ranking is independently corroborated by a SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis on the clean subset, which recovers the same dominant inputs and thus attributes the skew to the Pulse engine rather than to the elementary-effects design (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s13}).

Four cross-output patterns emerge directly from Figure~\ref{fig:morris}. Cardiovascular outputs are cardio-input dominated: output HR is driven by input HR in every cell, and output MAP is driven by SBP in every cell, with no measurable cross-talk from ventilator settings. Gas-exchange outputs are ventilator-driven. PaO$_2$ is dominated by FiO$_2$ in S2 / S3 (mild and moderate ARDS) and collapses to a near-flat dependence in S4 (severe ARDS), correctly reproducing the textbook ARDS regime in which shunt physiology pins arterial oxygenation independent of inspired oxygen. PaCO$_2$ is dominated by tidal volume in most cells, consistent with the alveolar-ventilation identity $\dot{V}_A = \mathrm{RR} \cdot (V_T - V_D)$. Output Vt tracks input Vt at far higher $\mu^{*}$ magnitudes than any other input--output pair, reflecting the near-deterministic coupling of VC-AC ventilation outside the dyssynchrony regime described in \S\ref{sec:results-vcac}. SpO$_2$ shows the smallest $\mu^{*}$ magnitudes overall because the output sits at the hemoglobin-saturation ceiling across most of the swept range. The lowest top-3 fractions all fall in severe-ARDS gas-exchange cells (SpO$_2$ and PaO$_2$ in S4, from 0.658 to 0.727), where the same shunt physiology that flattens PaO$_2$ blunts every oxygenation input's effect, spreading sensitivity more uniformly. The skewed-sensitivity property is weakest here, though it is never violated: all 48 cell $\times$ output combinations clear the 50~\% bar (minimum 0.658).

Influence rankings say which inputs matter. They do not say whether the response to those inputs is proportional. The Morris $\sigma / \mu^{*}$ ratio addresses that second question: above 0.6, a response is read as interaction-driven or non-linear rather than proportional. Applying the preregistered threshold to the top-three inputs of every cell, and keeping only combinations that clear it in all five bootstrap draws, leaves 23 of the 144. These spread over 17 of the 48 cell $\times$ output combinations.

Non-linearity is therefore common rather than exceptional, and it is not localised. All seven swept inputs appear, across all four scenarios, and the two reference bodies contribute almost equally: 12 combinations on the female body against 11 on the male. PEEP is the most frequent single input, in 8 of the 23, ahead of respiratory rate at 5. No input accounts for the pattern on its own.

Delivered tidal volume is the most affected output, in 8 of the 23. It is also the channel whose breath-to-breath oscillation dominates the \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flags (\S\ref{sec:results-vcac}), and the two findings cannot appear on the same runs. Where that oscillation is strongest, the detector flags the runs carrying it, which removes them from the clean subset the elementary-effect estimator uses. In S1 the delivered-Vt coefficient of variation exceeds the 0.05 threshold on 139 of 320 runs, the median top-three input retains 3 clean trajectories, and no Vt combination is estimable. In S3 the same three figures are 36 of 320 runs, 16 trajectories and 3 Vt combinations. The map of non-linearity is bounded by run survival: the regime that destabilises delivered volume most is the one in which that instability is least measurable.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{VC-AC tidal-volume dyssynchrony and the $-2$~cmH$_2$O lever}{VC-AC tidal-volume dyssynchrony and the -2 cmH2O lever}}\label{sec:results-vcac}

Across the full sweep, the single largest source of \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flags is tidal-volume (Vt) oscillation under VC-AC ventilation. We quantify it with the delivered Vt coefficient of variation (Vt CV $= \sigma_{V_T}/\bar{V}_T$), the breath-to-breath spread of delivered volume relative to its mean, over the final 60~s of the 120~s simulation. A run whose Vt CV crosses the 0.05 detector threshold is flagged \texttt{not\_steady\_state} for variance; Vt oscillation is the \emph{primary} such flag (the one that survives the detector's priority ordering) on 20.6~\% of runs. On these runs the delivered breath volume never settles to a single value but keeps changing from breath to breath: a genuinely oscillating output, not a solver failure: the simulation finishes normally with finite values. The effect concentrates in healthy adults and fades as ARDS severity rises: in our data it is strongest where the set respiratory rate is high and lung compliance is normal (the healthy S1 cells), and it subsides as the stiffening ARDS lung dampens the translation of breath-to-breath effort into volume swings. This is the in-silico signature of \emph{patient--ventilator asynchrony}---a mismatch between the patient's own breathing effort and the breaths the ventilator delivers. The asynchrony itself is well documented clinically in volume-control ventilation~\cite{figueroacasas2016}; the specific conditions that produce it here (a high set rate on a normal-compliance lung) are our own in-silico finding, not a claim drawn from that source.

We preregistered a follow-up experiment that isolated the mechanism. We re-ran 64 of the affected configurations with a single intervention: setting Pulse's \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerPressure} to $-2$~cmH$_2$O on the \texttt{SEMechanicalVentilatorVolumeControl} action (Pulse's volume-controlled ventilation action, which configures VC-AC mode; upstream source: \texttt{SEMechanicalVentilatorVolumeControl.cpp} lines 120--128, where the default patient trigger is configured). This disables the respiratory-model-driven patient trigger and replaces it with a fixed pressure-trigger threshold. The intervention collapses the median Vt CV from 0.351 to 0.016 (Figure~\ref{fig:corrections}a, dots well below the diagonal) and takes the single worst-affected configuration (S1\_F\_0117) from 1.826 to 0.018. After the lock, 50 of the 64 reruns have a Vt CV below the 0.05 steady-state threshold, clearing both of the experiment's preregistered acceptance gates: a median paired Vt CV drop of at least 0.04 (met, $+0.326$) and at least 75~\% of reruns below 0.05 (met here, 78.1~\%).

The practical consequence is best read as an exploration lever, not a clinical recommendation. Downstream users who need \emph{deterministic} Vt delivery (for example, generating stable synthetic cohorts for machine-learning pipelines) can set \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerPressure} explicitly to $-2$~cmH$_2$O on every action; this collapses the breath-to-breath variability and makes Vt reproducible across the sweep. The determinism carries a cost that is invisible in the steady-state outputs themselves. Locking the trigger to a fixed threshold decouples breath delivery from the engine's modelled patient respiratory drive: a simulated inspiratory effort that does not reach the $-2$~cmH$_2$O threshold no longer triggers an assist breath, while mandatory breaths continue at the set rate. In a real patient this is a shift from patient-responsive assist-control toward controlled ventilation, in which sub-threshold efforts go unrewarded---a form of patient--ventilator dyssynchrony~\cite{thille2006} that is clinically undesirable~\cite{blanch2015} and detectable on ventilator flow/pressure waveforms, though most directly confirmed with specialised monitoring such as esophageal pressure~\cite{akoumianaki2014} or diaphragm electrical activity~\cite{sinderby1999}.

Pulse's \emph{default} configuration, by contrast, couples triggering to the modelled respiratory drive, so it reproduces the effort-dependent breath-to-breath variability of patient--ventilator asynchrony: physiologically the more faithful regime, but, because the breath volume keeps varying, not summarisable by a single steady-state mean over the conventional final-60-second window. The $-2$~cmH$_2$O lock should therefore be understood as an instrument for controlled software exploration and deterministic synthetic-data generation, not as guidance for clinically realistic ventilation.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Transient hyperoxia regime at FiO$_2$~=~1.0}{Transient hyperoxia regime at FiO2 = 1.0}}\label{sec:results-hyperoxia}

A second cluster of preregistered detector flags concentrates on the highest inspired-oxygen settings. Within the locked 120-second simulation window, Pulse's steady-state mean PaO$_2$ exceeds the detector's locked upper bound of 600~mmHg on 34 of the 126 \texttt{implausible}-flagged runs, and 91~\% of these excursions occur at FiO$_2 \geq 0.95$. Two competing hypotheses fit this pattern. \textbf{Hypothesis A}: Pulse is over-delivering oxygenation at the FiO$_2 = 1.0$ boundary (an engine defect). \textbf{Hypothesis B}: the locked 120-second window is shorter than Pulse's PaO$_2$ relaxation time when shunt physiology is minimal and the alveolar partial pressure is climbing toward its theoretical ceiling (a window-length artefact rather than an engine defect).

A preregistered 60-run longer-rerun experiment discriminated between the two. Each configuration was re-executed from time zero with the recorded simulation duration raised from 120 to 300 seconds, and the plateau computed over the final 60 seconds of that window ($t = 240$--$300$~s; Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s9}). In the S1 (healthy) cohort, 41 of 44 reruns (93.2~\%) converged to a stable plateau under the preregistered drift criterion (Figure~\ref{fig:corrections}b, S1 cluster); the converged S1 plateau median was 643~mmHg, sitting approximately 20~mmHg \emph{below} the simple alveolar-gas-equation prediction at sea level of $\approx$~663~mmHg (FiO$_2 = 1.0$, $P_B = 760$, $P_{\mathrm{H_2O}} = 47$, $\mathrm{PaCO}_2 = 40$, $R = 0.8$)~\cite{west2020respiratory}. The $\approx$~20~mmHg gap is consistent with Pulse's modelled alveolar-arterial difference, and it rules out a runaway: an unbounded oxygenation defect would push PaO$_2$ \emph{above} the theoretical ceiling, not below it. It does not establish that the plateau level itself is right, which would take a validation against measured arterial data and is outside the scope of a curation map. The S3 and S4 reruns (Figure~\ref{fig:corrections}b, orange and red clusters) sit far below the 600~mmHg classifier bound, in the physiologically expected range for moderate and severe ARDS at FiO$_2 = 1.0$.

The practical consequence is again direct: hyperoxia studies on Pulse should record at least 300 seconds of simulation before extracting a steady-state plateau; on the healthy cohort, 95~\% of the increase from the $t = 0$ value to the late plateau is reached by $t = 180$~s. The 120-second window remains adequate for cardiovascular and ventilation outputs (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s9}) but not for oxygenation outputs at the FiO$_2 = 1.0$ extreme, where slow relaxation to the alveolar ceiling drives the apparent excursion above the conservative locked classifier bound. Extended-window simulations on healthy lungs at FiO$_2 = 1.0$ can additionally enter a separate solver-instability regime: six of the 44 S1 reruns relaxed to PaO$_2$ plateaus above 700~mmHg, four of them stabilising near 1{,}400~mmHg, above the sea-level barometric pressure. No input-side threshold screens for these: all six sit at a single tidal-volume level and none at the highest, so minute ventilation does not separate them. What does separate them is the reference body: across five input-identical male/female pairs the female body was affected in five of five and the male in one of five, the opposite direction to the all-male divergence cluster of \S\ref{sec:results-sex}. Users running extended-window hyperoxia simulations should therefore screen each PaO$_2$ trajectory individually (full characterisation in the companion technical monograph).

\subsection{Sex-stratified solver divergence and blood-volume mechanism}\label{sec:results-sex}

Across the full 1{,}280-run sweep, solver divergence is rare but strikingly sex-stratified. 20 runs (1.6~\%) failed to complete with finite output, and \emph{all 20 fell on the male reference body}: 4 in each of S1\_M, S2\_M, S3\_M and 8 in S4\_M, with zero divergences across the 640 runs on the female reference body (Figure~\ref{fig:sex_bv}a). The 20 divergence events are not spread uniformly through the input space. 16 of them concentrate on a specific compound input pattern --- low cardiovascular output (HR = 50, SBP = 90) combined with high respiratory rate (RR = 20) --- a clinically recognisable hypotensive--tachypneic configuration. The remaining 4 divergences all fall in the severe-ARDS cell (S4\_M) under different, higher-heart-rate configurations (HR 90--110), so severe ARDS destabilises the solver across a broader input range than the hypotensive--tachypneic pattern alone. FiO$_2$ was at its 1.0 boundary in 13 of the 20 cases (the other 7 near FiO$_2 \approx 0.74$, so the boundary is enriched but not necessary for divergence). Identical Morris input grids submitted to the female reference body (matched on age, condition string, and ventilator settings; differing only in patient mass, height, and sex flag) never trigger the same numerical edge case.

A preregistered follow-up experiment isolated the mechanism. Pulse's \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp} computes blood-volume baseline by the allometric relation $\text{BV} = 65.6 \cdot \text{weight}^{1.02}$ (BV in mL, weight in kg), yielding 4{,}803~mL for the 67.3~kg male reference body and 3{,}996~mL for the 56.2~kg female reference body. Because this allometric relation is sex-independent, the two bodies share essentially the same blood volume per kilogram ($\approx$71~mL\,kg$^{-1}$), within standard clinical estimates~\cite{nadler1962}; the resulting 16.8~\% difference is therefore an absolute-volume gap driven by body mass, not a sex-specific physiological difference (the formula does not reproduce the male--female difference reported clinically: applied to these same two bodies, Nadler's sex-specific equations give 73.0 and 63.8~mL\,kg$^{-1}$~\cite{nadler1962}). This absolute-volume difference nonetheless propagates into the cardiovascular circuit's operating point. We re-executed the 20 originally-diverged male inputs with an explicit \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline} override at three levels: the default (4{,}803~mL), an intermediate bracket (4{,}400~mL, a 8.4~\% reduction), and the engine's setup-time minimum for this body (4{,}082~mL, a 15.0~\% reduction). The divergence count fell from 20 / 20 at the default to 0 / 20 at both reductions (Figure~\ref{fig:sex_bv}b); even the smaller 8.4~\% reduction toward the female-equivalent range already eliminates the entire divergence cluster.

A separate, engine-level observation emerged from the same experiment. At patient setup, Pulse enforces a hard, per-patient floor on \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline}: on this male body the lowest accepted value is 4{,}082~mL, and any lower setting is rejected outright with an \texttt{[ERROR]} and exit. This floor sits 15.0~\% below the computed default: the point at which Pulse stops accepting a smaller blood volume as a baseline and instead requires it to be modelled as an explicit hypovolemia condition; that reduction is comparable to the upper bound of a Class~I hemorrhage in the Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS) trauma classification~\cite{atls2018} (a loss of up to 15~\% of blood volume). Reducing the male baseline toward this floor works by lowering the absolute volume the cardiovascular circuit must reconcile with the prescribed operating point, not by nearing a specific stability threshold, which is consistent with the divergence reflecting an \emph{ill-conditioned} circuit (numerically over-sensitive as a whole) rather than a single equation that diverges on its own. This carries a concrete consequence for sex-comparison studies: the female body's computed blood volume (3{,}996~mL) falls 86~mL below the male floor, so the two reference bodies cannot be given a common blood volume by a one-way transplant, and a genuinely sex-balanced synthetic cohort therefore takes more than matching patient attributes. For downstream users training models on Pulse-generated cohorts, the operational warning is equally concrete: pooling male and female runs from the input-extreme regime without flag-stratified filtering plants a \emph{spurious} sex covariate at the male body's divergence boundary: a structural artefact of Pulse's allometric blood-volume baseline, not a clinical phenomenon.

Divergence is one axis of sex-stratified behaviour; the steady-state values themselves are another. Across the 24 (scenario $\times$ output) comparisons, the Wasserstein distance between the male and female clean-run distributions exceeds its permutation null on five. Every one of them is a gas-exchange channel: PaCO$_2$ in S1, S2 and S3 (the latter two at $p < 0.001$), together with SpO$_2$ and PaO$_2$ in S4. Heart rate, MAP and delivered tidal volume separate no further than a random relabelling of the same pooled runs. One caveat governs how these five should be read. The comparison is made on clean runs, and how many runs stay clean itself differs by sex: 144 of 160 against 122 of 160 in S3, for instance. The two distributions are therefore drawn from different subsets of the input grid. A surviving distance therefore establishes that the choice of reference body matters, without separating an effect on the physiology from an effect on which configurations survive the flag filter. The two are entangled by construction. Cross-body gas-exchange contrasts additionally combine sex-dependent physiology and morphology with a systematic ventilation-dose difference: at every matched absolute tidal-volume setting, the female reference body receives 19.8~\% more tidal volume per kilogram of actual body weight and 36.1~\% more per kilogram of ideal body weight, computed using the sex-specific Devine relation that Pulse applies at patient setup (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s6-dose}). Telling these contributions apart would require a design in which both bodies retain the same grid points \emph{and} receive matched relative ventilation doses.

\subsection{Secondary observations}\label{sec:results-secondary}

Two additional findings sit at the boundary of the headline scope but matter for downstream interpretation. Each is summarised here in compact form and fully expanded in the Supplementary Information.

\textbf{First public characterisation of an ARDS impairment-fraction solver-instability band.} Pulse's own methodology documents ARDS severity as a continuous parameter on $[0, 1]$ and exemplifies the range with the round-tenths convention $\{0.0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.0\}$; published Pulse validation work and HowTo examples sample at most up to severity 0.9 and do not examine impairment fractions inside the interval 0.91--0.945. Three converging boundary probes within the companion technical monograph (full provenance in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5}) first identified 0.91--0.945 as a numerical bifurcation in the respiratory solver. The present atlas's 200-run continuous-severity sweep at midrange Morris inputs extends the empirical band to $[0.879, 0.945]$ on the low edge and distinguishes two divergence regimes. The first is an \emph{input-driven} regime concentrated on the male reference body at hypotensive--tachypneic--FiO$_2$~=~1.0 inputs (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}); the second is a \emph{severity-driven} regime in which both sexes diverge at midrange inputs across $[0.879, 0.945]$. The locked sweep places severe ARDS (S4) at impairment-fraction 0.95, intentionally above this band; downstream Pulse users should avoid $[0.879, 0.945]$ when stable steady-state outputs are required.

\textbf{Compensatory tachycardia decouples input HR from output HR under hypoxemia.} In S1 (healthy), the output HR settles near a normal resting value (mean 73.8~bpm, $\sigma = 20.4$~bpm across clean runs), whatever the input HR baseline. In S3 and S4, by contrast, the output HR climbs to 155.7~bpm and 147.1~bpm respectively, even though the inputs are still drawn from the same [50, 110]~bpm range: a large sympathetic overshoot: Pulse's modelled baroreceptor and chemoreceptor reflexes respond to the severe hypoxemia and push the HR up. This reflex is strong enough to override the HR explicitly set as input. That has a direct consequence for machine-learning pipelines: a model trained on Pulse synthetic cohorts will learn this reflex as a steady-state feature rather than an active physiological response (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s15}).
\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

This work delivers the first open, preregistered reliability atlas for the Pulse Physiology Engine across several adult critical-care operational scenarios. Two operator-actionable corrections and a sex-specific reliability axis emerge from the 1{,}280-run sweep. \textbf{First}, a single configuration setting (\S\ref{sec:results-vcac}) that makes VC-AC tidal-volume delivery consistent and repeatable for synthetic-cohort generation. One caveat comes with it: this same setting also removes the realistic, effort-dependent asynchrony (the breath-to-breath variation) that Pulse models, so it is a data-generation tool, not a clinical ventilation recommendation. \textbf{Second}, a steady-state-window correction for hyperoxia simulation at FiO$_2 = 1.0$ (\S\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia}). \textbf{Third}, on a separate reliability axis rather than as a configuration fix, a previously uncharacterised sex-stratified solver-divergence cluster with an identified blood-volume mechanism (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}). The accompanying per-cell traffic-light curation map (Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light}) is the principal deliverable for downstream Pulse users: it summarises which combinations of patient reference body, ARDS severity, and steady-state output are default-eligible under the stated criteria, which require flag-stratified handling, and which warrant per-channel justification before being ingested into downstream analyses. Those criteria carry a material post hoc deviation from the preregistered wording at the yellow--red boundary, declared with its sensitivity analysis in \S\ref{sec:methods-traffic}. It is a curation and review-priority instrument, not a general physiological validation of the engine. Frameworks for credibility assessment in biomedical modelling treat verification, sensitivity analysis, uncertainty quantification and a bounded domain of applicability as components of model assessment; validation itself requires comparator data~\cite{viceconti2021insilico}.

For the computational-physiology community, the atlas matters in two ways. First, it directly addresses the Pulse maintainers' own statement that no formal sensitivity or uncertainty quantification has been performed on user-facing operational inputs~\cite{pulsefaq}: the Morris screen shows input sensitivity is strongly skewed, with the top three inputs accounting for a median 93~\% of summed $\mu^{*}$ on 48 of 48 cell $\times$ output combinations, a result independently cross-verified by SHAP (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s13}). Second, the method itself carries over to other engines. It pairs a Morris elementary-effects screen of the operational inputs with a four-flag steady-state classifier that sorts every run into a category instead of discarding any. No comparable open atlas yet exists for related lumped-parameter engines such as BioGears (Pulse's predecessor) or HumMod. The same recipe, demonstrated here on Pulse v4.3.1, could be applied to them by wrapping each engine's own inputs and outputs, and would broaden the community's overall reliability picture for synthetic-physiology infrastructure as a whole. A recent credibility assessment of a ventilation patient--device model reports sensitivity and uncertainty analyses for parameters of its lung-mechanics, gas-exchange and respiratory-control submodels~\cite{hennigs2026validation}; the present atlas instead maps the behaviour of Pulse across its user-facing operational input envelope.

For machine-learning pipelines that train on Pulse-generated synthetic patient cohorts, the four-flag classifier doubles as a run-level data-quality contract. That contract rests on a null result bounded to the sampled envelope: within Pulse's accepted setup envelope, no submitted input was silently altered at ingestion. The preregistered rejection-log detector fired on 0 of 1{,}280 runs, and the post hoc instantiation audit found exact agreement between the value submitted to the engine and the value Pulse serialised after ingestion for every input contract that reached ingestion. This concerns setup-time input handling only: it does not imply that physiological states or controller targets stay fixed once the simulation runs, and it does not establish Pulse's behaviour for clinically plausible values outside the envelope sampled here. Green cells in Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light} are eligible for default inclusion under the stated criteria, subject to use-case-specific validation; yellow cells should be filtered to the clean subset with the per-cell flag rate reported in training-set metadata; red cells require per-channel and per-input justification. Green denotes default eligibility under the implemented rule and under the non-degenerate literal hard-flag interpretation; it does not establish physiological validity. Two specific traps documented here merit explicit attention. The sex-stratified divergence cluster (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}) means that pooling male and female runs from the input-extreme regime without flag-stratified filtering ingests a spurious sex covariate at the male reference body's divergence boundary: a structural artefact of Pulse's allometric blood-volume baseline rather than a clinical phenomenon. The compensatory-tachycardia decoupling reported in \S\ref{sec:results-secondary} means the input HR (the value you set) and the output HR (the value Pulse settles at) are not interchangeable in ARDS scenarios. If this is ignored, a model trained on Pulse cohorts will learn the engine's baroreceptor and chemoreceptor reflexes as a steady-state feature rather than as an active physiological response to hypoxemia (low oxygen).

\textbf{Complementarity with prior external falsification work on MIMIC-III.} Cornish et al.~\cite{cornish2023} applied a causal-falsification framework to Pulse on MIMIC-III sepsis trajectories and reported 85 total Hoeffding-inequality rejections (a distribution-free falsification test flagging each channel whose Pulse-versus-cohort discrepancy exceeds a statistical confidence bound) across 15 physiological quantities. Of these, 82 of 85 fall outside the four-channel overlap (HR, MAP, PaO$_2$, PaCO$_2$): 80 on channels the present atlas does not assess (electrolyte, metabolic, acid--base, dermal), plus 2 on partial-overlap pressure channels; only 3 of 85 fall on the four-channel overlap, all on PaCO$_2$. The two studies therefore expose different weaknesses of Pulse rather than the same one. Cornish et al.\ test its chemistry and thermoregulation outputs (electrolytes, metabolism, acid--base, and skin temperature) against a real patient cohort undergoing clinical interventions; the present atlas instead tests its cardio-respiratory outputs across the theoretical envelope of operationally accepted inputs. Neither study characterises the metabolic and renal channels that Cornish's test flags most often, so the natural next step is a metabolic-and-renal reliability atlas: applying the same Morris input sweep used here for the cardiopulmonary inputs to Pulse's endocrine (hormone-regulating) and electrolyte sub-models instead (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s7}).

Beyond guidance for downstream users, the atlas also serves the engine's maintainers: it provides the first public characterisation of an ARDS impairment-fraction solver-instability band ($[0.879, 0.945]$, \S\ref{sec:results-secondary}) and distils six concrete infrastructure recommendations for the Pulse maintainers (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s11}): (1)~structured, machine-readable input-rejection events (one typed record per rejected field); (2)~documentation of the male reference body's blood-volume floor; (3)~a maintained Morris/Sobol sensitivity-analysis recipe for the operational inputs; (4)~a setup-time warning for the $\sim$0.91--0.945 ARDS instability zone; (5)~an optional per-channel steady-state-drift column in the CSV output; and (6)~field-attributed setup-error messages. Each targets tooling, documentation, or validation infrastructure rather than Pulse's underlying physiology.

Six scope limits bound the conclusions. (i)~The atlas pins to Pulse v4.3.1 (results apply identically to the build-only v4.3.2 release); newer physiology branches will require re-mapping. (ii)~Patient anatomy is restricted to two standard adult reference bodies (male and female); the atlas does not characterise body-mass or height variation within sex. (iii)~The operational envelope covers adult critical-care ventilation in VC-AC mode only. Three things are out of scope by design: pediatric translation; pressure-targeted ventilator modes (such as pressure-controlled and pressure-support); and transient within-run dynamics, particularly in runs that never reach a stable steady state. The mode restriction also bounds transfer to the downstream use cited in the Discussion, which drives the engine through a pressure-targeted action space: of its six ventilator settings, only FiO$_2$, respiratory rate and PEEP are inputs this atlas sweeps. (iv)~The Morris elementary-effect estimator discards any input pair where either run is flagged (\S\ref{sec:methods-detector}). This eliminates all clean FiO$_2$ pairs in the healthy (S1) and severe-ARDS (S4\_F) cells, as every FiO$_2$ step contains at least one flagged endpoint: the high-FiO$_2$ runs in healthy lungs (driven \texttt{not\_steady\_state} or \texttt{implausible} by SpO$_2$-saturation pinning and, at FiO$_2 = 1.0$, transient hyperoxia, \S\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia}), or the low-FiO$_2$ runs in severe-ARDS S4\_F (driven \texttt{implausible} or \texttt{not\_steady\_state} by severe hypoxemia). As a result, the FiO$_2$ rows in Figure~\ref{fig:morris} are left blank, and the cross-sex Spearman comparison is limited to S2 and S3, where the male and female $\mu^{*}$ rankings largely agree (Spearman $\rho$ point estimates $0.75$--$1.00$ across the 12 scenario--output cells; the mild-ARDS delivered-tidal-volume cell is the sole cell whose confidence interval includes zero) (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s6-rho}). These effects are deterministic consequences of the preregistered implausibility bound and steady-state window applied at the edges of the operational envelope, not a Pulse defect. Adding more trajectories cannot recover the discarded pairs, and re-running the whole grid with the 300-second window that resolves the hyperoxia plateau would break preregistration integrity while changing nothing for the runs that already settle. (v)~The hard physiological bounds behind the \texttt{implausible} flag (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-hard-bounds}) judge whether a \emph{sustained steady-state mean} is biologically plausible over the evaluation window, not whether a value is instantaneously attainable; rare clinical extremes, often transient or peri-mortem, can legitimately exceed them. Rather than discarding such runs, the classifier keeps each one and records the flag it fired. The bounds are moreover treated as adjustable choices rather than fixed biological constants: the deliberately conservative PaO$_2$ ceiling (the most debatable bound) was itself re-examined by a threshold-sensitivity analysis (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-pao2-sensitivity}) that recomputes the \texttt{implausible} flag under a wider, physiologically anchored PaO$_2$ limit, confirming that the threshold is a tunable choice, not an absolute biological cutoff. The bounds therefore provide a run-level screening standard for synthetic training cohorts (defining which runs meet the stated inclusion criteria), not a claim that exceptional clinical trajectories are impossible. (vi)~Pulse enforces DBP/SBP $\leq$ 0.75, which makes the sampled SBP--DBP domain non-rectangular. Configuration-time clipping shortened 9 of 20 DBP steps and caused applied DBP to co-vary in 8 of 20 SBP steps, so those effects are not strict fixed-step, one-at-a-time Morris estimates. Nevertheless, SBP remained rank 1 for MAP in all eight cells under six sensitivity estimators (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-clip-cost}); the smallest SBP-to-DBP $\mu^{*}$ ratio was 1.22 in S2\_M under the most adverse combined estimator. These analyses establish ranking robustness, not causal isolation of the clipping contribution. Together, these limitations define the scope within which the atlas's conclusions should be interpreted.

The reliability atlas presented here is intended as foundational infrastructure rather than a terminal deliverable. The four-flag classifier and the per-cell traffic-light are reusable instruments that downstream Pulse users can apply to their own configurations without re-running the present sweep, and the atlas pipeline (preregistered, reproducible from a single Docker image with a fixed seed, released under Apache-2.0 alongside an open audit ledger of its quantitative claims~\cite{wilkinson2016, munafo2017}) is designed to be re-executable when Pulse releases new physiology branches. Several extensions follow naturally from this groundwork. By characterising the adult operational envelope first, the atlas provides the methodological basis a pediatric translation layer would need, since pediatric physiological dynamics may differ from adult dynamics and cannot be safely adapted from an unmapped substrate; a native pediatric Pulse fork could build on such a translation in turn. A complementary metabolic-and-renal atlas would cover the channels Cornish et al.~\cite{cornish2023} identified as most assessment-sensitive but that no operational-input sweep currently characterises. And validating the atlas's cell-level classifications against real-world clinical databases (for example adult intensive-care records such as MIMIC-IV) by cross-tabulating documented ventilation settings against the per-cell curation map would connect the map to bedside practice. Taken together, these directions (pediatric adaptation and clinical-database validation foremost) would broaden the cardiopulmonary-and-metabolic Pulse-reliability picture that the present work begins. Ultimately, because Pulse-generated cohorts are already used as machine-learning training data --- in one published benchmark, transitions sampled from Pulse train a latent dynamics model that then stands in for the engine, and that model is assessed against Pulse rather than against patient data~\cite{ward2024ventilator} --- understanding how that synthetic substrate behaves across its envelope is a prerequisite for building clinical decision-support tools that are safe to rely on. Ventilation automation is, moreover, already reaching the bedside: a recent multicentre randomised trial of closed-loop ventilation (ACTiVE~\cite{sinnige2026active}) improved overall ventilation quality yet showed no difference in ventilator-free days or mortality relative to protocolized conventional ventilation, a reminder of how hard the patient--machine control problem is, and of why the next generation of such controllers, which already draw on synthetic physiology for training and testing~\cite{ward2024ventilator}, will depend on exactly the kind of curation and review-priority map this atlas provides.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Pulse engine and configuration}\label{sec:methods-pulse}

All simulations used Pulse v4.3.1 (Kitware, Apache-2.0)~\cite{bray2019}, a real-time-capable lumped-parameter whole-body physiology engine that solves the coupled cardiovascular, respiratory, gas-exchange, endocrine, renal, and drug sub-models as a system of ordinary differential equations at a 20~ms fixed timestep. The respiratory sub-model is a 5-compartment lung (carina, left and right anatomic dead space, left and right alveoli)~\cite{pulserespmethod}. The engine was executed inside a single \texttt{pulse-atlas-engine} Docker image, pinned for the entire 1{,}280-run sweep so that every simulation runs against bit-identical engine binaries. Runs were orchestrated by a thin Python layer around Pulse's command-line interface. For each Morris input vector it writes a JSON configuration file (reference body, ARDS scenario condition, ventilator action set, and the seven operational inputs at their sampled values), then spawns a container against that image and captures both the resulting time-series CSV and the engine's setup log, the latter being what the \texttt{clamped} test reads. v4.3.1 was the most recent stable release with documented physiology updates at the time of the sweep; the subsequent v4.3.2 release (August 2025) is build-system-only and contains no physiology, solver, validation, or patient-setup changes, so all atlas results apply identically to v4.3.2 (full release diff in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s1}). The sweep was dispatched to 2--3 parallel Docker workers on commodity laptop hardware (a 6-core / 12-thread Intel i5-11400H laptop, 8~GB RAM, CPU-only; full environment in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s12-env}). Re-running the pipeline reproduces the canonical (reference) outputs bit-for-bit at three successive stages, each checked against its own committed artefact: the input sample (the 1{,}280-row table of input combinations, regenerated from the fixed sampler seed), the per-cell aggregate tables (one summary table per scenario--sex cell, obtained by re-executing the sweep against the pinned Docker image), and the derived statistics (sensitivity indices, confidence intervals, and hypothesis tests, recomputed from those tables). Mechanical ventilation was restricted to volume-controlled assist-control (VC-AC) mode so that all runs remain directly comparable; no run in the sweep uses any other mode, so pressure-targeted modes lie outside the atlas's scope by design rather than by omission.

\subsection{Patient reference bodies}\label{sec:methods-patients}

Two adult reference bodies were used: a male body (age 18, 67.3~kg, 180~cm) and a female body (age 18, 56.2~kg, 163~cm). Both weights are U.S.\ Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) growth-chart 50th-percentile values at 214 months (17~y 10~m) for each sex~\cite{kuczmarski2002cdc}; the corresponding CDC values at exactly 18.0 years are 67.64 and 56.41~kg, 0.34 and 0.21~kg above the values used. The preregistration's provenance erratum names 18.0 years for these weights; Supplement~\S\ref{sec:s3-bodies} documents the two-row lookup offset and confirms that the preregistered bodies are the ones simulated. Both heights are clinically standard adult values, but they were arrived at differently: the female height matches the CDC 50th percentile~\cite{kuczmarski2002cdc} (body mass index, BMI, 21.2) and Pulse's own \texttt{heightStandardFemale\_cm = 163.0}, whereas the male height is a researcher-chosen value that fixes the BMI at 20.8 and is not Pulse's \texttt{heightStandardMale\_cm = 177.0}.

Pulse also refuses some setup values outright. Two of those constraints act on inputs swept here: a gate on systolic and diastolic blood pressure, and acceptance bands on the initial heart and respiratory rates. Both were verified before the sweep, against the Pulse source and by boundary-probe runs (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s2}), and the sampler ranges were set inside them. A third constraint, Pulse's limit on the pulse-pressure ratio, is handled differently: it is deliberately not imposed on the Morris sample, because that would distort the sample geometry the elementary-effect estimator depends on. It is applied one step later, when each configuration is written, and every run records the requested value, the applied value and a clip flag (\S\ref{sec:methods-sweep}). Every configuration actually submitted to Pulse therefore lies inside the accepted envelope. One last constraint does not act on a swept input at all: a hard per-patient floor on the blood-volume baseline of the male reference body. The sweep never exercises it, because blood volume is not swept; it is characterised separately in \S\ref{sec:results-sex}.

\subsection{Sweep design}\label{sec:methods-sweep}

Seven user-facing operational inputs were swept across their verified Pulse acceptance ranges: heart rate (HR, 50--110~bpm), systolic and diastolic blood pressure (SBP 90--120~mmHg, DBP 60--80~mmHg with the post-clip pulse-pressure ratio DBP/SBP $\leq 0.75$ enforced), respiratory rate (RR, 8--20~bpm), tidal volume (Vt, 300--800~mL), inspired oxygen fraction (FiO$_2$, 0.21--1.0), and positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP, 0--20~cmH$_2$O). Each range is anchored to a primary verification source (Pulse source-code audit, Pulse FAQ, or empirical boundary-probe runs in the companion technical monograph), table-by-table in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s2}. Mean arterial pressure (MAP) was derived from SBP and DBP as $\mathrm{DBP} + (\mathrm{SBP} - \mathrm{DBP})/3$ rather than swept independently. Four scenarios spanned the adult critical-care severity range: a healthy baseline (S1, ARDS impairment fraction = 0) and three ARDS severities (S2, mild, impairment = 0.26; S3, moderate, impairment = 0.60; S4, severe, impairment = 0.95). At a reference ventilation setting based on the ARDSnet low-tidal-volume framework (VC-AC, tidal volume 6~mL/kg actual body weight, 403.8~mL, PEEP 10~cmH$_2$O, FiO$_2$ 0.4), these impairment fractions --- calibrated on the adult male reference body --- place Pulse's arterial PaO$_2$/FiO$_2$ near the centres of the Berlin Definition mild and moderate bands and just inside the upper boundary of the severe band, which is open-ended below and therefore has no centre~\cite{ards2012}; because that ratio is ventilation-dependent, the Berlin band a scenario occupies shifts with FiO$_2$ across the sweep, and both the reference-ventilation calibration and its sweep-wide FiO$_2$ dependence are tabulated in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5-berlin}. The three severities also bracket the 0.91--0.945 solver-instability zone documented at design time in the companion technical monograph (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5}); the atlas's own continuous-severity sweep subsequently widened that band to $[0.879, 0.945]$ (\S\ref{sec:results-secondary}). Sensitivity was screened with the Morris elementary-effects design~\cite{morris1991, campolongo2007}, a global screening method whose cost is a linear function of the number of inputs, against the order-$k^2$ cost of a resolution V fractional factorial design. The sample was generated with SALib~\cite{iwanaga2022,herman2017salib} using 20 trajectories at 4 grid levels per input and random seed 42. Each trajectory contributes $7 + 1 = 8$ model evaluations (one more than the number of inputs), giving 160 runs per (scenario $\times$ sex) cell and 1{,}280 runs across the full sweep. These 20 trajectories were fixed in preregistration: enough to give stable $\mu^{*}$ rankings, yet few enough to keep the full sweep computationally tractable on a single machine. Each run executed for 120 seconds of simulated time, with output channels sampled at 5-second intervals (13 samples in the final 60-second steady-state window). Pulse represents ARDS severity as a single per-lung impairment-fraction scalar. One such \texttt{Severity} value drives four hard-coded, adult-calibrated interpolation curves (distal airway resistance, alveolar compliance, alveolar diffusion surface area, and pulmonary shunt), which together produce the respiratory-mechanics and gas-exchange changes; the engine offers no independent handle on resistance, compliance, or diffusion. Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5-mapping} documents the mapping.

\subsection{Steady-state classifier}\label{sec:methods-detector}

Every simulation was classified by a four-flag steady-state detector before any statistical analysis. \texttt{clamped} is the preregistered label for a run whose Pulse setup log matches any of 4 documented input-rejection patterns (listed below). Operationally this log-based detector identifies \emph{explicit} setup rejection: it establishes that a submitted value was not accepted as given, and cannot by itself detect a silent substitution. A post hoc direct input-instantiation audit, reported in \S\ref{sec:results-overview}, directly addresses that measurement gap within the sampled accepted envelope by comparing each value submitted to the engine with the value Pulse serialises after ingestion. Values submitted to the engine are post-wrapper values: for DBP the preregistered pulse-pressure constraint can modify the sampled Morris value before submission (\S\ref{sec:methods-sweep}; Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-clip-cost}). \texttt{diverged} fires when the simulation failed to complete with finite output, the CSV is missing, or the engine exited with a non-zero status. \texttt{implausible} fires when any steady-state output mean exceeds the locked hard physiological bounds (HR [20, 220]~bpm, MAP [30, 180]~mmHg, Vt [50, 1{,}500]~mL, SpO$_2$ [50, 100]~\%, PaO$_2$ [20, 600]~mmHg, PaCO$_2$ [10, 120]~mmHg). Unlike the swept inputs, the six tracked outputs have no acceptance envelope in Pulse: the engine reports whatever its solver produces and never rejects an output value. The bounds above are therefore ours (a plausibility standard applied after the run), not a constraint the engine enforces. Each bound is anchored to primary critical-care and respiratory-physiology literature, per bound and with its ledger provenance, in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-hard-bounds}: the ARDS Network lung-protective ventilation trial~\cite{brower2000}, the Severinghaus oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve~\cite{severinghaus1979}, the Sepsis-3 and Surviving Sepsis Campaign hemodynamic thresholds~\cite{singer2016, evans2021sepsis}, the European Society of Hypertension (ESH) 2023 hypertensive emergency definition~\cite{mancia2023esh}, the observed distribution of maximum PaCO$_2$ in a multinational ARDS cohort~\cite{nin2017}, and West's respiratory physiology~\cite{west2020respiratory}. The PaO$_2$ upper bound of 600~mmHg is deliberately conservative relative to the alveolar-gas-equation sea-level ceiling of $\approx$~663~mmHg on FiO$_2$ = 1.0; a parallel sensitivity-analysis reclassification under the alternative \texttt{HARD\_BOUNDS\_PHYSIOLOGICAL} dictionary (PaO$_2$ upper raised to 700~mmHg) is reported in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-pao2-sensitivity} and characterised in the companion technical monograph. \texttt{not\_steady\_state} fires when, over the final 60-second window, any tracked output flatlines at a hard bound (standard deviation under 1~\% of the mean's magnitude, with the mean itself within 0.1~\% of that bound --- a pair of conditions that separates numerical pinning at a bound from genuine physiological saturation at it), varies too much (coefficient of variation above the locked threshold of 0.05 --- most often tidal volume), or drifts past its own locked per-channel threshold (HR 5~bpm, MAP 5~mmHg, Vt 50~mL, SpO$_2$ 2 percentage points, PaO$_2$ 15~mmHg, PaCO$_2$ 5~mmHg per 60~s; the per-channel rationale for those magnitudes is tabulated in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-drift}); drift is estimated by ordinary least-squares (OLS) regression on the 13 sample points in the [60, 120]~s window. The four flags are independent presence counts; a single simulation can fire several. Some analyses need each run assigned to a single category rather than several at once (partition-style analyses); for these, a fixed priority order resolves each run to one \texttt{primary\_flag}: \texttt{clamped} $>$ \texttt{diverged} $>$ \texttt{implausible} $>$ \texttt{not\_steady\_state}. The order follows the chronology of failure within a run (a breached input contract outranks a solver failure, which outranks a biologically impossible result, which outranks one that has simply not settled), and it is expanded flag by flag in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-detector-order}. A 22-run pilot on the healthy-male cell verified five preregistered gating criteria (success rate, detector self-consistency, 13-point steady-state window, clamp-regex reachability, and Pulse determinism within a $10^{-6}$ relative tolerance) before the full sweep launched; all thresholds were locked in preregistration before any sweep data was collected~\cite{munafo2017}.

\noindent The four \texttt{clamped} patterns, matched case-insensitively against the engine's setup log, are the set with which the 1{,}280-run sweep was classified:

\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\small, breaklines=true]
r"baseline of [\d\.]+ \S+ is too (?:high|low)"
r"maximum [\w\s]+ baseline allowed is"
r"must be modeled by adding/using a condition"
r"is outside the valid range"
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent They were calibrated against deliberate-clamp probes in the pilot study before the sweep launched, replacing an earlier set broad enough to match Pulse's ordinary solver-convergence messages and therefore to clamp every run. After the sweep, a fifth pattern and an optional unit token in the first were added to close a latent gap on the one Pulse rejection message of ten that omits its unit. Replaying both sets over every Pulse log on disk flips a single probe run and nothing in the published sweep, whose requested heart rates never leave [50, 110]~bpm (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-clamp}).

The [60, 120]~s steady-state window itself was preregistered before any sweep data was collected. Opening the evaluation window at $t = 60$~s (half-way through the 120~s simulated run) places it well past the initial ventilator-settling transient, and 13 sample points at the 5-second interval are the minimum that resolve the OLS-slope, coefficient-of-variation, and flatline diagnostics that gate the \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flag. A cross-output audit on the full sweep (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s16-window}) checked whether the two known edge-of-envelope instabilities (unsettled PaO$_2$ at high inspired oxygen, and oscillating delivered tidal volume under the default VC-AC trigger) spill over into the other channels. They do not. In the high-oxygen population (FiO$_2 \geq 0.95$, 195 runs) heart rate drifts past its threshold on 2.6~\% of runs, MAP on none, and PaCO$_2$ on 3.6~\%; in the tidal-volume-oscillation population (delivered-Vt coefficient of variation $>$ 0.05, 303 runs) the same three figures are 2.3~\%, 0.7~\% and 2.0~\%. The channels that do not settle are PaO$_2$ and delivered tidal volume themselves, characterised directly in \S\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia} and \S\ref{sec:results-vcac}.

\subsection{Traffic-light curation map}\label{sec:methods-traffic}

The per-cell curation map (Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light}) reduces each (scenario, sex, output) cell to one green / yellow / red verdict, built from two locked ingredients: the cell's combined diverged-plus-implausible flag rate, and the fractional deviation of its clean-run mean from a common baseline. The baseline for each output is that output's mean over the clean runs of the healthy-male (S1, M) cell, and the $10~\%$ and $30~\%$ deviation bands that separate the three colours were preregistered before any data were collected as clinical-tolerance thresholds: deviations below $10~\%$ fall within typical biological and measurement variation, whereas deviations above $30~\%$ mark a major departure from baseline. The full colour criteria are stated with the map in the legend to Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light}. Scoring every cell against a single (S1, M) baseline keeps male and female cells on one comparable scale, at the cost of folding legitimate male--female physiological differences into the female cells' deviation term. Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-traffic} reports the re-scoring check that verifies this choice does not drive the classification.

\textbf{Post hoc deviation from the preregistered traffic-light rule.} The four-flag run-level detector and its thresholds were preregistered, but the subsequent traffic-light aggregation was not implemented exactly as written. The preregistration stated that a cell would be red when its deviation exceeded $30~\%$ or when ``any flag'' was raised. The implemented rule instead treats \texttt{diverged} and \texttt{implausible} as hard flags, assigns yellow whenever their combined rate is greater than zero, and reserves red for a combined hard-flag rate above $30~\%$; the preregistered $10~\%$ and $30~\%$ deviation thresholds are unchanged. The repository establishes that this implementation was committed after preregistration, in the same commit as the tracked sweep artefacts, but does not establish whether it was defined before or after inspection of the sweep outputs. We therefore record the altered yellow--red boundary as a material deviation. Restricting ``flag'' to the two hard flags is reported separately as an operational clarification, because applying the phrase to all four flags would classify all 48 combinations red. The phrase ``cell baseline'' likewise admits both a cell-specific and an (S1, M)-reference reading, so the implemented (S1, M) reference is reported as an unresolved terminological ambiguity rather than an established deviation. Under the implemented rule the map contains 7 green / 29 yellow / 12 red combinations; under the literal hard-flag reading it would contain 7 green / 3 yellow / 38 red. The same seven combinations remain green, the same twelve remain red, and 26 combinations move from yellow to red (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-tl-sensitivity}).

We retain the implemented rule as the primary display because it preserves a graded distinction between any hard-flag occurrence and a hard-failure rate above $30~\%$, without creating a single additional green verdict: under the literal hard-flag rule even the healthy-male reference cell is red on all six outputs despite zero baseline deviation by construction, because 15 of its 160 runs carry a hard flag. Neither the classifier, the map, nor the reported counts were changed in response to this finding; the preregistered reading is reported alongside them as a sensitivity analysis, and the deviation is recorded in the preregistration's own append-only log.

\subsection{Statistical analyses}\label{sec:methods-stats}

The Morris sample was generated with the SALib implementation~\cite{iwanaga2022,herman2017salib}; the Morris mean-absolute-elementary-effect indices ($\mu^{*}$) and standard deviations ($\sigma$) were then computed per (scenario, sex, input, output) cell from the clean elementary-effect pairs. Each elementary effect divides the change in the output by the size of the input step expressed on that input's swept range mapped to $[0,1]$, so a $\mu^{*}$ is an output change per full-range move of its input and the seven inputs remain comparable despite their different physical units. Indices were computed with non-parametric 95~\% bootstrap confidence intervals on $\mu^{*}$ from 1{,}000 trajectory-level resamples. Hypothesis~1 (skewed sensitivity, the top-3 inputs jointly account for $\geq 50~\%$ of summed $\mu^{*}$) was evaluated per cell $\times$ output on the point-estimate top-3 fraction of summed $\mu^{*}$; the underlying $\mu^{*}$ values carry the trajectory-level bootstrap confidence intervals described above. (Hypotheses 2 and 3 need no inferential test: one is a rate, the other an existence claim. Both are resolved in \S\ref{sec:results-overview}.) Hypothesis~4 (sex-stratified reliability) is already satisfied by the divergence asymmetry of \S\ref{sec:results-overview}. To measure how far the difference extends, we used per-(scenario $\times$ output) Wasserstein distances (the minimum ``work'' needed to reshape one probability distribution into the other) between the male and female clean-run distributions. These distances were tested against a permutation null built from 2{,}000 random relabellings: the male and female clean runs are pooled, then re-split at the two original group sizes. The resulting 24-test family was corrected using Benjamini--Hochberg control of the false-discovery rate (the expected fraction of false positives among the rejected tests) at $q = 0.05$. With 2{,}000 permutations the smallest empirical $p$-value the null can resolve is 1/2{,}000; the two tests that reach that floor are reported as $p < 0.001$. Cross-sex agreement of input-importance rankings was quantified by the Spearman rank correlation ($+1$ when the two orderings agree, $-1$ when they are reversed) between male and female $\mu^{*}$ vectors per (scenario, output), with paired-input bootstrap 95~\% confidence intervals from 2{,}000 input-resamples. A Sobol bolt-on was preregistered as a conditional escalation: within each cell $\times$ output, the three highest-ranked inputs by $\mu^{*}$ are each tested against their $\sigma / \mu^{*}$ ratio, and any input reaching 0.6 is promoted to variance decomposition. Applied as preregistered, the point-estimate trigger fires on 72 of the 144 top-three inputs, spanning 43 of the 48 cell $\times$ output combinations. The same preregistration fixed the bolt-on's compute envelope at 1{,}500 additional runs. Sobol needs an independent sample per cell, so even the narrowest reading of the preregistered cost formula (a base sample of 64 with a single promoted input) puts 43 cells at 11{,}008 runs. The bolt-on was therefore infeasible within the budget its own protocol declared, and is reported here rather than executed.

The preregistration set that trigger on the point ratio alone, with no uncertainty qualifier attached. We add a post-hoc sensitivity analysis to separate the promotions that rest on evidence from those that do not. Trajectories are resampled 1{,}000 times per combination and $\sigma / \mu^{*}$ is recomputed jointly on each draw, so the interval covers the ratio itself rather than $\sigma$ with $\mu^{*}$ held fixed. Combinations whose lower bound clears 0.6 in all five independent draws are characterised in \S\ref{sec:results-morris}. The full decomposition and a ready-to-fire SALib implementation are given in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-sobol} and the released code base.

The full sampler, sweep driver, four-flag detector, statistical-analysis pipeline, and the audit ledger that re-derives the computed quantitative claims in this paper from the canonical output artefacts are released open-source (see \hyperlink{tgt:code-avail}{Code availability}) and were preregistered before data collection.

\subsection{Use of AI tools}\label{sec:methods-ai}

\noindent In the interest of full methodological transparency, the analysis pipeline code and the manuscript prose were drafted, edited, refactored, and debugged within a cross-checking loop between two large language models (LLMs), under direct supervision by S.N.\ at every stage. Prompt engineering instructed each model to critique and cross-correct the other's outputs at every iteration, reducing the risk of hallucinated or unsupported content by verifying claims against the audit ledger and the source code.

One side of that loop was always a Claude model, used locally through the Claude Code agent in the terminal at the \texttt{xhigh} thinking-effort default: Claude Opus 4.7 (Anthropic; model \texttt{claude-opus-4-7}) for the initial drafting, then Claude Opus 4.8 (\texttt{claude-opus-4-8}, released 28~May~2026, coinciding with the manuscript's supervisor-review phase), and from 24~July~2026 Claude Opus 5 (\texttt{claude-opus-5}). The other side changed once. Gemini Pro 3.1 (Google; extended-thinking mode), in a separate web session, filled that role up to and including 2~August~2026; from 3~August~2026 it was replaced by OpenAI ChatGPT (GPT-5.6 Sol, high intelligence mode, browser session), with no further use of Gemini thereafter. In that role the second model provided editorial assistance, consistency checks, and methodological discussion. The two-model loop was therefore maintained across every round, the revision rounds as well as the initial drafting.

Final manuscript formatting was performed by Claude Opus 4.7; global clinical review by the medical authors (A.W., C.S., P.J., and M.S.). The Opus 4.8 revision rounds responded to co-author and supervisor review: manuscript re-reading, copy-editing, and the associated text and reference modifications. The Opus 5 round covered four items: in-repository replication of the ARDS-severity-to-Berlin calibration; the heart-rate and respiratory-rate boundary probes; a primary-source audit of the reference list; and an audit of the statistical analyses. That audit restored the Sobol promotion trigger to its preregistered top-three form, added the post-hoc ratio-aware bootstrap on $\sigma / \mu^{*}$, and versioned the script that generates it.
