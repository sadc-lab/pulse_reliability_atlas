\abstract{Pulse is the most-cited open-source whole-body physiology simulator, widely used for synthetic clinical cohorts and digital-twin research, yet no formal sensitivity analysis on operational inputs has been published. We present the first reliability atlas across the adult critical-care envelope: Morris elementary-effects sampling of seven inputs across four ARDS severities and two patient surrogates (1{,}280 simulations). Tidal-volume instability in 20.6\% of runs is identified as patient-ventilator dyssynchrony and corrected by setting the VC-AC inspiration trigger to $-2$~cmH$_2$O. A transient hyperoxia regime at FiO$_2$~=~1.0 means 120-second simulations underestimate plateau PaO$_2$; 300-second runs converge into the alveolar-gas-equation band (median 643~mmHg). Solver divergence concentrated entirely on the male surrogate (20/1{,}280 runs); reducing the male blood-volume baseline toward its 4{,}082.24~mL floor monotonically eliminated divergence. The resulting per-cell traffic-light map provides a reusable data-quality filter for downstream users; all data and code are released open-source under Apache-2.0.}

\keywords{Pulse Physiology Engine, sensitivity analysis, digital twin, synthetic patient data, ICU simulation, Morris elementary effects, reproducibility, ARDS}
