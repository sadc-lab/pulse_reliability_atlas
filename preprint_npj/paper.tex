\abstract{Pulse is among the most-cited open-source whole-body physiology simulators, used for synthetic patient cohorts and computational physiology research, yet no formal sensitivity analysis on operational inputs has been published. We present the first reliability atlas across the adult critical-care envelope: Morris elementary-effects sampling of seven inputs across four Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) severities and two patient surrogates (1{,}280 simulations). Tidal-volume instability in 20.6~\% of runs is identified as patient-ventilator dyssynchrony and corrected by setting the volume-controlled assist-control (VC-AC) inspiration trigger to $-2$~cmH$_2$O. A transient hyperoxia regime at FiO$_2$~=~1.0 means 120-second simulations underestimate plateau PaO$_2$; 300-second runs converge to a plateau (median 643~mmHg) sitting $\sim$20~mmHg below the alveolar-gas-equation prediction of $\approx$663~mmHg, consistent with Pulse's modelled A-a gradient. Solver divergence concentrated entirely on the male surrogate (20/1{,}280 runs); reducing the male blood-volume baseline toward its 4{,}082.24~mL floor monotonically eliminated divergence. The resulting per-cell traffic-light map provides a reusable data-quality filter for downstream users; all data and code are released open-source under Apache-2.0.}

\keywords{Pulse Physiology Engine, sensitivity analysis, digital twin, synthetic patient data, ICU simulation, Morris elementary effects, reproducibility, ARDS}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The Pulse Physiology Engine (Kitware, Apache-2.0) is among the most actively maintained open-source whole-body human physiology simulators~\cite{bray2019}, providing real-time-capable, lumped-parameter modeling of the cardiovascular, respiratory, gas-exchange, and pharmacological systems. Pulse has been applied to ventilator-protocol safety analysis during the COVID-19 pandemic~\cite{webb2020}, to mechanical-ventilator implementation and validation against the ASL 5000 reference~\cite{webb2022vent}, and to external statistical assessment as a digital-twin candidate for retrospective clinical cohorts~\cite{cornish2023}. Across these settings, Pulse generates synthetic patient trajectories that downstream researchers use for hypothesis testing, device validation, and computational physiology studies. Yet despite this footprint, no public sensitivity, divergence, or input-clamping map of Pulse's user-facing operational envelope has been published. Each downstream user re-encounters the same engine limitations independently, and each validation pipeline re-derives its own working envelope by trial and error.

The Pulse maintainers state on the project's public FAQ that ``we have not performed a systematic forward propagation or inverse quantification of model uncertainty, nor have we conducted a formal sensitivity analysis''~\cite{pulsefaq}. The upstream code repository carries a single sensitivity-analysis study, tagged \texttt{STUDY\_SENSITIVITY\_ANALYSIS\_1\_0\_0} (2020), but that study targets internal vascular-circuit resistance multipliers for model calibration --- a different parameter class from the user-facing operational inputs (heart rate (HR), systolic and diastolic blood pressure (SBP and DBP), respiratory rate (RR), tidal volume (Vt), inspired oxygen fraction (FiO$_2$), and positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP)) that downstream applications must specify. The only prior external assessment of Pulse, by Cornish et al.~\cite{cornish2023}, applied a causal-falsification framework to MIMIC-III sepsis trajectories and identified intervention-outcome inaccuracies on specific clinical channels (with their Hoeffding-inequality rejections concentrating on electrolyte, metabolic, and acid--base outputs --- output classes that do not overlap the present atlas's six-output panel). The complementary operational-input reliability question --- how Pulse responds across its acceptance envelope when its primary user-facing controls are varied --- has remained unaddressed.

We close this gap with the first open reliability atlas across Pulse's adult critical-care operational envelope. We sweep the same seven user-facing inputs across four Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) severity scenarios (healthy baseline through severe ARDS) and two adult reference patient surrogates using Morris elementary-effects screening --- a one-input-at-a-time perturbation design that estimates each input's average influence on each output along randomized trajectories, and that flags non-linearity and interaction effects~\cite{morris1991,campolongo2007}. Every simulation is classified by a four-flag steady-state detector that records whether Pulse silently clamped an out-of-range input, the solver diverged, the steady-state output landed outside hard physiological bounds, or the trajectory failed to settle within the simulation window --- categorizing failures rather than discarding them, so that the resulting atlas remains complete across the full input grid. The atlas surfaces three operator-actionable findings (a volume-controlled assist-control (VC-AC) tidal-volume dyssynchrony correction at the inspiration trigger, a steady-state-window correction for hyperoxia simulation, and a previously uncharacterized sex-stratified solver-divergence cluster with an identified blood-volume mechanism) and a per-cell traffic-light reliability map that downstream Pulse users can apply as a data-quality filter on synthetic patient cohorts. Characterising the adult engine's operational envelope is also a methodological prerequisite for ongoing pediatric translation work: pediatric physiological dynamics differ fundamentally from adult dynamics, so any pediatric adaptation of Pulse must first establish where the adult engine itself is trustworthy. The pipeline is preregistered, fully reproducible from a single Docker image with a fixed seed, and released open-source under Apache-2.0.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Overview of the reliability atlas}\label{sec:results-overview}

The atlas comprises 1{,}280 simulations spanning eight (scenario $\times$ sex) cells of 160 runs each. Each simulation is classified by a four-flag steady-state detector: \texttt{clamped} flags whether Pulse silently bent any setup input out of its acceptance band; \texttt{diverged} flags solver failure or non-finite output; \texttt{implausible} flags steady-state means that fall outside hard physiological bounds (e.g.\ PaO$_2$ above 600~mmHg or SpO$_2$ below 50~\%); and \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flags runs whose final 60-second window fails preregistered drift or variance thresholds. The four flags are independent presence counts (a single run can fire several), and Table~\ref{tab:flag_summary} reports the per-cell breakdown.

Three features of Table~\ref{tab:flag_summary} are immediately load-bearing. First, silent clamping never occurs (0 / 1{,}280 across every cell), confirming that Pulse rejects out-of-range inputs loudly rather than silently bending them. Second, solver divergence is sex-stratified: 20 / 1{,}280 runs (1.6~\%) diverged, and all 20 fell on the male surrogate (the mechanism is the subject of \S\ref{sec:results-sex}). Third, \texttt{not\_steady\_state} concentrates on healthy adults (S1, where input tidal-volume swings outpace Pulse's VC-AC controller) and on severe ARDS (S4, where the locked simulation window is insufficient for slow convergence under near-shunt physiology) --- both regimes are characterised in \S\ref{sec:results-vcac} and \S\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia}. Across the full sweep, 663 of 1{,}280 simulations (51.8~\%) are flag-clean under the locked classifier; the per-cell reliability traffic-light map (Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light}) summarises the resulting reliability picture at a glance.

\subsection{Primary input-sensitivity rankings}\label{sec:results-morris}

Figure~\ref{fig:morris} shows the Morris $\mu^{*}$ (mean of absolute elementary effects, the input-importance metric) for each of the six outputs across the eight cells and the seven swept inputs. The preregistered first hypothesis was that, in every cell, the top-three inputs account for at least 50~\% of summed $\mu^{*}$ on each output --- a ``skewed sensitivity'' criterion that determines whether a small subset of inputs explains most of the output variability. The data support this on 48 of 48 cell $\times$ output combinations, with a median top-3 fraction of 0.928 (range 0.658--0.998) under a 1{,}000-resample bootstrap confidence interval.

Four cross-output patterns emerge directly from Figure~\ref{fig:morris} (full per-cell tables in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3}). Cardiovascular outputs are cardio-input dominated: output HR is driven by input HR in every cell, and output MAP is driven by SBP in every cell, with no measurable cross-talk from ventilator settings. Gas-exchange outputs are ventilator-driven: PaO$_2$ is dominated by FiO$_2$ in S2 / S3 (mild and moderate ARDS) and collapses to a near-flat dependence in S4 (severe ARDS), correctly reproducing the textbook ARDS regime in which shunt physiology pins arterial oxygenation independent of inspired oxygen; PaCO$_2$ is dominated by tidal volume in most cells, consistent with the alveolar-ventilation identity $\dot{V}_A = \mathrm{RR} \cdot (V_T - V_D)$. Output Vt tracks input Vt at far higher $\mu^{*}$ magnitudes than any other input--output pair, reflecting the near-deterministic coupling of VC-AC ventilation outside the dyssynchrony regime described in \S\ref{sec:results-vcac}. SpO$_2$ shows the smallest $\mu^{*}$ magnitudes overall because the output sits at the hemoglobin-saturation ceiling across most of the swept range. The lowest top-3 fractions all fall in severe-ARDS gas-exchange cells (S4\_F SpO$_2$ at 0.658, S4\_M SpO$_2$ at 0.703), where the saturation ceiling distributes sensitivity more uniformly across the inputs --- these being the only cells in the atlas where the skewed-sensitivity property weakens.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{VC-AC tidal-volume dyssynchrony and the $-2$~cmH$_2$O fix}{VC-AC tidal-volume dyssynchrony and the -2 cmH2O fix}}\label{sec:results-vcac}

The largest contributor to the \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flag count across the full sweep is tidal-volume oscillation under volume-controlled assist-control ventilation. Within the locked 120-second simulation window, the delivered tidal-volume coefficient of variation (Vt CV, the standard deviation of breath-by-breath delivered Vt divided by its mean over the final 60-second steady-state window) exceeds the 0.05 detector threshold on 20.6~\% of runs (Figure~\ref{fig:corrections}a, x-axis distribution). The pattern is concentrated on healthy adults (visible as the elevated S1 \texttt{not\_steady\_state} counts in Table~\ref{tab:flag_summary}) and fades as ARDS severity increases, consistent with a patient-ventilator-trigger interaction that activates when respiratory drive is high and lung compliance is normal --- a clinically documented form of patient-ventilator asynchrony~\cite{figueroacasas2016} rather than a Pulse-specific solver artefact.

We preregistered a follow-up experiment that isolated the mechanism. We re-ran 64 of the affected configurations with a single intervention: setting Pulse's \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerPressure} to $-2$~cmH$_2$O on the \texttt{SEMechanicalVentilatorVolumeControl} action (\texttt{SEMechanicalVentilatorVolumeControl.cpp} lines 120--128 in the upstream source), which disables the respiratory-model-driven patient trigger and replaces it with a clinically standard intensive-care unit (ICU) sensitivity setting. The intervention collapses the median Vt CV from 0.351 to 0.016 (Figure~\ref{fig:corrections}a, dots well below the diagonal) and reduces the worst-case Vt CV from 1.826 to 0.112; 78.1~\% of locked-trigger reruns drop below the 0.05 steady-state threshold, satisfying both preregistered acceptance gates.

The practical consequence for downstream Pulse users is direct: any VC-AC sensitivity study or virtual-cohort generation that requires deterministic tidal-volume delivery should set \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerPressure} explicitly to $-2$~cmH$_2$O (or another clinically standard value) on every action. Without this setting, Pulse's default VC-AC behaviour produces realistic patient-ventilator asynchrony at exactly the ventilator-stress points where validation matters most --- physiologically faithful, but not steady-state interpretable under the conventional final-60-second window.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Transient hyperoxia regime at FiO$_2$~=~1.0}{Transient hyperoxia regime at FiO2 = 1.0}}\label{sec:results-hyperoxia}

A second cluster of preregistered detector flags concentrates on the highest inspired-oxygen settings. Within the locked 120-second simulation window, Pulse's steady-state mean PaO$_2$ exceeds the detector's locked upper bound of 600~mmHg on 34 of the 126 \texttt{implausible}-flagged runs, and 91~\% of these excursions occur at FiO$_2 \geq 0.95$. Two competing hypotheses fit this pattern: either Pulse is over-delivering oxygenation at the FiO$_2 = 1.0$ boundary (an engine defect), or the locked 120-second window is shorter than Pulse's PaO$_2$ relaxation time when shunt physiology is minimal and the alveolar partial pressure is climbing toward its theoretical ceiling (a window-length artefact rather than an engine defect).

A preregistered 60-run extended-simulation experiment discriminated between the two. Each rerun resumed from Pulse's saved engine state at the end of the original 120-second run and continued for an additional 300 seconds, with the plateau computed from the final 60 seconds of the extension (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s9}). In the S1 (healthy) cohort, 41 of 44 reruns (93.2~\%) converged to a stable plateau under the preregistered drift criterion (Figure~\ref{fig:corrections}b, S1 cluster); the converged S1 plateau median was 643~mmHg, sitting approximately 20~mmHg \emph{below} the simple alveolar-gas-equation prediction at sea level of $\approx$~663~mmHg (FiO$_2 = 1.0$, $P_B = 760$, $P_{\mathrm{H_2O}} = 47$, $\mathrm{PaCO}_2 = 40$, $R = 0.8$)~\cite{west2020respiratory}. The $\approx$~20~mmHg gap is consistent with Pulse's modelled alveolar-arterial difference rather than an oxygenation defect, which would be expected to push PaO$_2$ \emph{above} the theoretical ceiling, not below it. The S3 and S4 reruns (Figure~\ref{fig:corrections}b, orange and red clusters) sit far below the 600~mmHg classifier bound, in the physiologically expected range for moderate and severe ARDS at FiO$_2 = 1.0$.

The practical consequence is again direct: hyperoxia studies on Pulse should be simulated for at least 300 seconds before extracting a steady-state plateau. The 120-second window remains adequate for cardiovascular and ventilation outputs (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s9}) but not for oxygenation outputs at the FiO$_2 = 1.0$ extreme, where slow relaxation to the alveolar ceiling drives the apparent excursion above the conservative locked classifier bound. Downstream users should additionally avoid Vt $\times$ RR combinations exceeding $\sim$12~L/min minute ventilation on healthy lungs at FiO$_2 = 1.0$, where extended-window simulations enter a separate solver-instability regime (full characterisation in the companion technical monograph).

\subsection{Sex-stratified solver divergence and blood-volume mechanism}\label{sec:results-sex}

Across the full 1{,}280-run sweep, solver divergence is rare but strikingly sex-stratified. 20 runs (1.6~\%) failed to complete with finite output, and \emph{all 20 fell on the male surrogate}: 4 in each of S1\_M, S2\_M, S3\_M and 8 in S4\_M, with zero divergences across the 640 female-surrogate runs (Figure~\ref{fig:sex_bv}a). The 20 divergence events are not spread uniformly through the input space; 16 of them concentrate on a specific compound input pattern --- low cardiovascular output (HR = 50, SBP = 90) combined with high respiratory rate (RR = 20) --- a clinically recognisable hypotensive--tachypneic configuration; the FiO$_2 = 1.0$ boundary co-occurs in 13 of the 20 cases. Identical Morris input grids submitted to the female surrogate (matched on age, condition string, and ventilator settings; differing only in patient mass, height, and sex flag) never trigger the same numerical edge case.

A preregistered follow-up experiment isolated the mechanism. Pulse's \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp} computes blood-volume baseline by the allometric relation $\text{BV} = 65.6 \cdot \text{weight}_{\text{kg}}^{1.02}$, yielding 4{,}802.63~mL for the 67.3~kg male reference body and 3{,}996.09~mL for the 56.2~kg female body --- a 17~\% sex gap that propagates into the cardiovascular circuit's operating point. We re-executed the 20 originally-diverged male inputs with an explicit \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline} override at three levels: the default (4{,}802.63~mL), an intermediate bracket (4{,}400~mL, a 8.4~\% reduction), and the engine's setup-time minimum for this body (4{,}082.24~mL, a 15.0~\% reduction). The divergence count fell monotonically: 20 / 20 $\rightarrow$ 2 / 20 $\rightarrow$ 0 / 20 (Figure~\ref{fig:sex_bv}b). Reducing male BV toward the female-equivalent range eliminates the divergence cluster.

A separate engine-level observation emerged from the same experiment: Pulse enforces a hard per-patient floor on \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline} at setup time --- 4{,}082.24~mL on this male body. Setting BV below the floor triggers an explicit \texttt{[ERROR]} and exit. The practical consequence for sex-comparison studies is that the female-computed BV (3{,}996.09~mL) sits 86~mL below the male floor, so the two surrogates cannot be made BV-equivalent by a one-way transplant; a true sex-balanced synthetic cohort requires more than just patient-attribute matching. For downstream Pulse users training models on synthetic patient cohorts, the operational implication is concrete: pooling male and female runs from the input-extreme regime without flag-stratified filtering will introduce a hidden sex covariate at the male surrogate's divergence boundary --- a structural artefact of Pulse's allometric BV baseline rather than a clinical phenomenon.

\subsection{Secondary observations}\label{sec:results-secondary}

Three additional findings sit at the boundary of the headline scope but matter for downstream interpretation. Each is summarised here in compact form and fully expanded in the Supplementary Information.

\textbf{Documented ARDS impairment-fraction instability zone.} The locked sweep places severe ARDS (S4) at impairment-fraction 0.95, intentionally above Pulse's documented 0.91--0.945 solver-instability band. A complementary 200-run continuous-severity sweep at midrange inputs (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5}) confirms divergence concentrates inside this band rather than at its edges, and that the same band is sex-balanced under midrange inputs (in contrast to the input-extreme regime of \S\ref{sec:results-sex}). Practical guidance for downstream Pulse users: avoid the 0.91--0.945 impairment-fraction range when stable steady-state outputs are required (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5}).

\textbf{Compensatory tachycardia decouples input HR from output HR under hypoxemia.} In S1 (healthy), output HR settles near a physiological norm (73.8~bpm mean, $\sigma = 20.4$~bpm across clean runs) regardless of the input HR baseline. By S3 and S4, output HR rises to 155.7~bpm and 147.1~bpm respectively, despite inputs drawn from the same [50, 110]~bpm range --- a substantial sympathetic overshoot driven by Pulse's modelled baroreceptor and chemoreceptor reflexes responding to severe hypoxemia. The compensation is strong enough to override the explicit input baseline, which has direct consequences for machine-learning pipelines: models trained on Pulse synthetic cohorts will internalise this reflex as a steady-state feature rather than an active physiological response (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s15}).

\textbf{Complementarity with prior external falsification work on MIMIC-III.} Cornish et al.~\cite{cornish2023} applied a causal-falsification framework to Pulse on MIMIC-III sepsis trajectories and reported 85 total Hoeffding-inequality rejections across 15 physiological quantities. Of these, 80 of 85 fall on channels the present atlas does not assess (electrolyte, metabolic, acid--base, dermal); only 3 of 85 fall on the four-channel overlap (HR, MAP, PaO$_2$, PaCO$_2$), all on PaCO$_2$. The two papers' failure surfaces are therefore complementary rather than redundant: Cornish covers chemistry-and-thermoregulation outputs under clinical-intervention sequences on a real cohort; the present atlas covers cardio-respiratory outputs across the theoretical operational acceptance envelope. The natural follow-up that neither paper does is a metabolic-and-renal atlas sweeping Pulse's endocrine and electrolyte sub-models the way this paper sweeps the cardiopulmonary inputs (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s7}).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

This work delivers the first open, preregistered reliability atlas for the Pulse Physiology Engine across the adult critical-care operational envelope. Three operator-actionable findings emerge from the 1{,}280-run sweep: a one-line configuration fix for VC-AC tidal-volume dyssynchrony (\S\ref{sec:results-vcac}); a steady-state-window correction for hyperoxia simulation at FiO$_2 = 1.0$ (\S\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia}); and a previously uncharacterised sex-stratified solver-divergence cluster with an identified blood-volume mechanism (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}). The accompanying per-cell traffic-light reliability map (Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light}) is the principal deliverable for downstream Pulse users: it summarises which combinations of patient surrogate, ARDS severity, and steady-state output are trustworthy as-is, which require flag-stratified handling, and which warrant per-channel justification before being ingested into downstream analyses.

For the computational-physiology community, the atlas matters in two ways. First, it directly addresses the Pulse maintainers' own statement that no formal sensitivity or uncertainty quantification has been performed on user-facing operational inputs~\cite{pulsefaq}. Second, the methodology --- Morris elementary-effects screening of operational inputs, combined with a four-flag steady-state classifier that categorises rather than discards every run --- is transferable. No comparable open atlas exists for sibling lumped-parameter engines such as BioGears or HumMod; the recipe demonstrated here on Pulse v4.3.1 can be applied to those engines with engine-specific I/O wrapping, and would meaningfully extend the community's collective reliability picture for synthetic-physiology infrastructure as a whole.

For machine-learning pipelines that train on Pulse-generated synthetic patient cohorts, the four-flag classifier doubles as a per-cell data-quality contract. Green cells in Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light} are safe to ingest; yellow cells should be filtered to the clean subset with the per-cell flag rate reported in training-set metadata; red cells require per-channel and per-input justification. Two specific traps documented here merit explicit attention. The sex-stratified divergence cluster (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}) means that pooling male and female runs from the input-extreme regime without flag-stratified filtering ingests a hidden sex covariate at the male surrogate's divergence boundary --- a structural artefact of Pulse's allometric blood-volume baseline rather than a clinical phenomenon. The compensatory tachycardia decoupling reported in \S\ref{sec:results-secondary} means that input HR and output HR are not interchangeable in ARDS scenarios: models trained on Pulse cohorts will otherwise internalise the engine's baroreceptor and chemoreceptor reflexes as steady-state features rather than active physiological responses to hypoxemia. The combined cardio-respiratory failure-surface mapped here is also complementary to, rather than redundant with, Cornish et al.'s causal-falsification framework on MIMIC-III sepsis trajectories~\cite{cornish2023}: their work flags chemistry-and-thermoregulation accuracy under real clinical-intervention sequences; this work flags cardio-respiratory reliability across the theoretical operational envelope.

Four scope limits bound the conclusions. (i)~The atlas pins to Pulse v4.3.1 (results apply identically to the build-only v4.3.2 release); newer physiology branches will require re-mapping. (ii)~Patient anatomy is restricted to two standard adult reference surrogates (male and female); the atlas does not characterise body-mass or height variation within sex. (iii)~The operational envelope is adult-critical-care VC-AC ventilation; pediatric translation, alternative ventilator modes (pressure-controlled, pressure-support), and within-run non-steady-state dynamics are out of scope by construction. (iv)~At FiO$_2 = 1.0$ in S1 and at FiO$_2 = 0.21$ / $1.0$ in S4\_F, every Morris OAT step has at least one endpoint flagged (\texttt{not\_steady\_state} or \texttt{implausible}), leaving these cells without clean elementary-effect pairs for FiO$_2$; the cross-sex Spearman comparison of input-importance rankings is consequently restricted to S2 and S3, where every input has clean pairs in both sexes. Each of these is bounded rather than open-ended; none undermines the atlas's reliability claims within its declared scope.

The reliability atlas presented here is intended as foundational infrastructure rather than a terminal deliverable. The four-flag classifier and the per-cell traffic-light are reusable instruments that downstream Pulse users can apply to their own configurations without re-running the present sweep, and the atlas pipeline (preregistered, reproducible from a single Docker image with a fixed seed, released under Apache-2.0 alongside an audit ledger of all quantitative claims~\cite{wilkinson2016, munafo2017}) is designed to be re-executable when Pulse releases new physiology branches. Three concrete extensions are immediate priorities. The present adult-envelope characterisation is the methodological prerequisite for a pediatric translation layer (in preparation, validation underway), because pediatric physiological dynamics differ fundamentally from adult dynamics and cannot be safely adapted from an unmapped substrate; a native pediatric Pulse fork (planned) follows from that translation work; and a complementary metabolic-and-renal atlas covers the channels Cornish et al.~\cite{cornish2023} identified as most assessment-sensitive but that no operational-input sweep currently characterises. Together, these would broaden the cardiopulmonary-and-metabolic Pulse-reliability picture that the present work begins.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Pulse engine and configuration}\label{sec:methods-pulse}

All simulations used Pulse v4.3.1 (Kitware, Apache-2.0)~\cite{bray2019}, a real-time-capable lumped-parameter whole-body physiology engine that solves the coupled cardiovascular, respiratory (5-compartment lung: carina, left and right anatomic dead space, left and right alveoli), gas-exchange, endocrine, renal, and drug sub-models as a system of ordinary differential equations at a 20~ms fixed timestep. The engine was executed inside a single \texttt{pulse-atlas-engine} Docker image, pinned for the entire 1{,}280-run sweep so that every simulation runs against bit-identical engine binaries. The sweep was dispatched to 2--3 parallel Docker workers on commodity laptop hardware, and bit-exact reproduction of the canonical sweep output was verified at three independent layers (the canonical sample, the per-cell aggregate parquets, and the derived statistical artefacts). Mechanical ventilation was restricted to volume-controlled assist-control (VC-AC) mode for cross-run comparability; alternative modes are out of scope by construction.

\subsection{Patient surrogates}\label{sec:methods-patients}

Two adult reference surrogates were used: a male body (age 18, 67.3~kg, 180~cm) and a female body (age 18, 56.2~kg, 163~cm). Weights are the U.S.\ Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) growth-chart 50th-percentile values for 18-year-olds of each sex; heights are clinically standard adult values: the male case fixes the body mass index (BMI) at 20.8 and the female case matches CDC P50 (BMI 21.1) and Pulse's own \texttt{heightStandardFemale\_cm = 163.0} reference. Pulse's setup-time constraints on each surrogate --- the blood-volume baseline floor (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}), the systolic/diastolic blood-pressure gate, and the heart-rate / respiratory-rate initialisation bands --- were verified empirically (\S\ref{sec:methods-sweep}) and respected by the sampler so that the Morris design never proposes inputs outside Pulse's accepted envelope.

\subsection{Sweep design}\label{sec:methods-sweep}

Seven user-facing operational inputs were swept across their verified Pulse acceptance ranges: heart rate (HR, 50--110~bpm), systolic and diastolic blood pressure (SBP 90--120~mmHg, DBP 60--80~mmHg with the post-clip pulse-pressure ratio DBP/SBP $\leq 0.75$ enforced), respiratory rate (RR, 8--20~bpm), tidal volume (Vt, 300--800~mL), inspired oxygen fraction (FiO$_2$, 0.21--1.0), and positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP, 0--20~cmH$_2$O). Mean arterial pressure (MAP) was derived from SBP and DBP as $\mathrm{DBP} + (\mathrm{SBP} - \mathrm{DBP})/3$ rather than swept independently. Four scenarios spanned the adult critical-care severity range: a healthy baseline (S1, ARDS impairment fraction = 0) and three Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome severities (S2, mild, impairment = 0.26; S3, moderate, impairment = 0.60; S4, severe, impairment = 0.95) calibrated against the Berlin Definition PaO$_2$/FiO$_2$ bands~\cite{ards2012} and bracketing Pulse's documented 0.91--0.945 solver-instability zone. The Morris elementary-effects design~\cite{morris1991, campolongo2007} used 20 trajectories at 4 grid levels per input, generated via SALib~\cite{iwanaga2022} with random seed 42; each trajectory contributes $(\mathrm{num\_inputs} + 1) = 8$ model evaluations, yielding 160 runs per (scenario $\times$ sex) cell and 1{,}280 runs across the full sweep. Each run executed for 120 seconds of simulated time, with output channels sampled at 5-second intervals (13 samples in the final 60-second steady-state window).

\subsection{Steady-state classifier}\label{sec:methods-detector}

Every simulation was classified by a four-flag steady-state detector before any statistical analysis. \texttt{clamped} fires when Pulse's setup-rejection log contains any of 4 documented input-rejection patterns, indicating that Pulse silently bent a setup input out of its acceptance band. \texttt{diverged} fires when the simulation failed to complete with finite output, the CSV is missing, or the engine exited with a non-zero status. \texttt{implausible} fires when any steady-state output mean exceeds the locked hard physiological bounds (representative bounds: HR [20, 220]~bpm, PaO$_2$ [20, 600]~mmHg, SpO$_2$ [50, 100]~\%; the complete table is reproduced in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3} with per-bound clinical rationale anchored to standard ICU reference texts). \texttt{not\_steady\_state} fires when the steady-state output flatlines at a physiological extreme, exhibits variance above the locked coefficient-of-variation threshold (0.05 for tidal volume), or drifts more than the per-channel locked threshold (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3}) over the final 60-second window, where drift is estimated by ordinary least-squares regression on the 13 sample points in the [60, 120]~s window. The four flags are independent presence counts; a single simulation can fire several. For partition-style analyses the priority order \texttt{clamped > diverged > implausible > not\_steady\_state > clean} resolves each run to a single \texttt{primary\_flag}. A 22-run pilot on the healthy-male cell verified five preregistered gating criteria (success rate, detector self-consistency, 13-point steady-state window, clamp-regex reachability, and Pulse determinism within a $10^{-6}$ relative tolerance) before the full sweep launched; all thresholds were locked in preregistration before any data was collected~\cite{munafo2017}.

\subsection{Statistical analyses}\label{sec:methods-stats}

Morris mean-absolute-elementary-effect indices ($\mu^{*}$) and standard deviations ($\sigma$) were computed per (scenario, sex, input, output) cell using the SALib implementation~\cite{iwanaga2022}, with non-parametric 95~\% bootstrap confidence intervals on $\mu^{*}$ from 1{,}000 trajectory-level resamples. Hypothesis~1 (skewed sensitivity, the top-3 inputs jointly account for $\geq 50~\%$ of summed $\mu^{*}$) was evaluated on the bootstrap CI rather than the point estimate. Hypothesis~4 (sex-stratified reliability) used per-cell-output Wasserstein distances between the male and female clean-run distributions, tested against a within-sex permutation null built from 2{,}000 random within-sex splits, and corrected for the 24-test family using Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate control at $q = 0.05$. Cross-sex agreement of input-importance rankings was quantified by the Spearman rank correlation between male and female $\mu^{*}$ vectors per (scenario, output), with paired-input bootstrap 95~\% confidence intervals from 2{,}000 input-resamples. The full sampler, sweep driver, four-flag detector, statistical-analysis pipeline, and the audit ledger that re-derives every quantitative claim in this paper from the canonical output artefacts are released open-source (\S Code availability) and were preregistered before data collection.
