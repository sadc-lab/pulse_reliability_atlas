\abstract{Pulse is an actively maintained open-source adult-physiology simulator, yet no formal sensitivity analysis of its operational inputs has been published. We present the first reliability atlas across its critical-care envelope: Morris elementary-effects sampling of seven inputs, four severity scenarios, male and female bodies (1{,}280 simulations). Sensitivity is skewed: three inputs carry a median 93~\% of each output's total. A four-flag classifier surfaces failure modes: two operator-actionable, one sex-dependent. Tidal-volume instability (20.6~\% of runs) reflects patient--ventilator dyssynchrony; locking the VC-AC trigger to $-2$~cmH$_2$O collapses that variability. At FiO$_2$~=~1.0 transient hyperoxia makes 120-second runs underestimate plateau PaO$_2$; 300-second healthy-lung reruns give a median 643~mmHg. Solver divergence fell entirely on the male body (20/1{,}280); reducing its blood-volume baseline to the 4{,}082~mL floor eliminated it. All runs and flags are released openly, so users can see which regions need checking before using Pulse output as training data.}

\keywords{Pulse Physiology Engine, digital twin, synthetic patient data, Morris elementary effects, sensitivity analysis, uncertainty quantification, ARDS, mechanical ventilation, VC-AC ventilation, critical care, machine learning training data, operational envelope, reproducibility}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Longitudinal health data of the kind needed to represent a patient's evolving state are often scarce and incomplete~\cite{katsoulakis2024digitaltwins}, and digital twins for health (virtual, often mechanistic models of human physiology used for in-silico trials, medical-device design, and synthetic-patient generation) are proliferating across healthcare~\cite{katsoulakis2024digitaltwins}. Mechanistic simulators supply physiological time series from explicitly specified inputs, with model-internal states accessible by construction; mechanistic origin does not, however, make their outputs automatically reliable. The Pulse Physiology Engine (Kitware, Apache-2.0) is one such actively maintained open-source whole-body simulator~\cite{bray2019}. It models each physiological system as a small network of coupled compartments rather than full three-dimensional anatomy (a lumped-parameter approach), delivering real-time cardiovascular, respiratory, gas-exchange, and pharmacological simulation (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s1}). Pulse has been applied to (1)~ventilator-safety what-if analysis~\cite{webb2020}, (2)~validation of its own mechanical-ventilator model against a physical reference simulator~\cite{webb2022vent}, (3)~medical education and simulation training on virtual-patient and emergency-medicine platforms~\cite{pulseinaction}, and (4)~general-purpose computational physiology~\cite{bray2019}; its respiratory dynamics have also been used independently as a source of simulated data for learning and benchmarking ventilator-control policies in Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)~\cite{ward2024ventilator}.

Yet no public map of how the engine responds across its user-facing \emph{input} envelope (which inputs dominate which outputs, where inputs are silently clamped, and where the solver diverges) has been published: most of that work probes Pulse's \emph{outputs}, validating them against reference data, or, in one external study, falsifying intervention--outcome behaviour on a real cohort. Webb et al.'s ventilator-sharing study~\cite{webb2020} does sweep inputs (12{,}642 unique patient and ventilator combinations spanning respiratory compliance, diffusion impairment and positive end-expiratory pressure) and rank parameters by outcome correlation, but over the \emph{mismatch} between two equal-sized 70~kg bodies sharing one ventilator, scored against a single composite safety outcome under pressure-mode ventilation with respiratory rate, I:E ratio and respiratory resistance held fixed; it resolves neither each input's influence on each physiological output, nor input clamping, nor solver divergence. The maintainers state on the project's public FAQ that ``we have not performed a systematic forward propagation or inverse quantification of model uncertainty, nor have we conducted a formal sensitivity analysis''~\cite{pulsefaq}. The upstream repository does carry a single 2020 sensitivity study in its source tree, tagged \texttt{STUDY\_SENSITIVITY\_ANALYSIS\_1\_0\_0}, but it targets internal lumped-parameter circuit values --- cardiovascular and respiratory resistances and compliances, which cannot be measured clinically --- for model calibration: a different parameter class from the user-facing operational inputs downstream applications actually specify. A prior external assessment of Pulse on a clinical dataset, by Cornish et al.~\cite{cornish2023}, applied a causal-falsification framework to MIMIC-III sepsis trajectories and reported intervention-response errors concentrated on electrolyte, metabolic, acid--base and dermal outputs, largely outside the present atlas's six-output cardio-respiratory panel. Each downstream user therefore re-encounters the same engine limitations independently, and each validation pipeline re-derives its own working envelope by trial and error.

We centre this work on the ARDS severity axis for two reasons. First, ventilating an impaired lung is a main use of Pulse's respiratory model, and a regime whose downstream reliability remains incompletely characterised. Second, mapping the adult engine's sensitivity and failure modes is the prerequisite for adapting it to pediatric ARDS, the setting in which we ultimately want simulated cohorts to support machine-learning prediction of ventilation-dependent blood gases in critically ill children; an adaptation cannot be assessed against an adult substrate whose own sensitivity and failure modes are unmapped.

The objective of this study was therefore to build the first open reliability atlas across Pulse's adult critical-care operational envelope, organised around three questions: \emph{how} strongly each input drives each output and where that influence turns non-linear; \emph{whether} the engine runs a submitted setting as given; and \emph{whether} the solver diverges or never reaches steady state. Seven user-facing operational inputs --- heart rate (HR), systolic and diastolic blood pressure (SBP, DBP), respiratory rate (RR), tidal volume (Vt), inspired oxygen fraction (FiO$_2$) and positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) --- were varied across their accepted ranges with a Morris elementary-effects design~\cite{morris1991,campolongo2007}, a one-at-a-time screening method; the resulting batch of runs is what we call the \emph{sweep}. Six steady-state outputs were tracked: HR, mean arterial pressure (MAP), delivered tidal volume, peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO$_2$), arterial oxygen partial pressure (PaO$_2$) and arterial carbon dioxide partial pressure (PaCO$_2$). Four clinical scenarios (a healthy baseline and three ARDS severities) crossed with two adult reference patient bodies, male and female, gave 1{,}280 simulations. Every run was then labelled by a four-flag steady-state classifier recording a refused setup input, a diverged solver, a steady-state output outside hard physiological bounds, or a run that never settled within the simulation window; failures are categorised rather than discarded, so the atlas stays complete. The deliverable is not a benchmark score but a per-cell curation and review-priority map with a reusable run-level classifier. Neither layer constitutes external physiological validation, and neither is a distribution-neutral automatic filter. The pipeline is preregistered, reproducible from a single Docker image with a fixed seed, and released open-source under Apache-2.0.

\subsection{Overview of the reliability atlas}\label{sec:results-overview}

The atlas comprises 1{,}280 simulations spanning eight cells of 160 runs each, one cell per clinical scenario (S1 healthy, S2 mild, S3 moderate, S4 severe ARDS) and patient sex (Figure~\ref{fig:design}). Every run carries the four preregistered flags defined in Methods~\S\ref{sec:methods-detector}, where the hard physiological bounds behind \texttt{implausible} are listed.

Three of the four preregistered hypotheses are settled by Table~\ref{tab:flag_summary} alone. H2, silent input clamping, is \emph{not supported}: the preregistered \texttt{clamped} category remained empty at setup (0 / 1{,}280 across every cell). That detector reads the setup log, so it establishes explicit rejection rather than the absence of a silent substitution; a separate post hoc audit compared every submitted value with the value Pulse serialises after ingestion and found exact agreement for all 10{,}160 input contracts that reached ingestion. The remaining 80 ventilator contracts belong to 20 runs that failed during patient stabilisation before the ventilator action was submitted; they enter neither numerator nor denominator. Both results are bounded by the sampled envelope and establish nothing about the clinically plausible adult-ICU range outside it. Pulse does make one explicit downstream adjustment: where FiO$_2$~=~1.0 was requested and the ventilator action was reached, the ventilator model caps the inspired oxygen fraction at 0.9996 --- the ambient mixture already carries CO$_2$, so exactly 1.0 cannot be composed --- with a logged error on all 195 runs concerned (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-clamp}). H3 is supported: solver divergence appears outside the previously characterised ARDS instability band, on 20 / 1{,}280 runs whose sex stratification is the subject of \S\ref{sec:results-sex}. H4 is supported: its preregistered criterion is a sex difference in clamping, divergence or $\mu^{*}$ ranking, and that divergence asymmetry alone satisfies it. H1 is settled in \S\ref{sec:results-morris}.

Retention is strongly cell-dependent and depends on which screen is applied. Under the four-flag screen, 663 of 1{,}280 runs (51.8~\%) are clean, ranging 3.43-fold across cells, from 144 of 160 in moderate ARDS on the male body down to 42 of 160 in the healthy female cell. Under the hard-failure-free screen, which admits runs flagged only \texttt{not\_steady\_state}, 1{,}134 of 1{,}280 (88.6~\%) survive, a 1.51-fold spread; the two screens deplete opposite ends of the severity axis. The per-cell breakdown is in Table~\ref{tab:screening-retention} and Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s4} decomposes what each screen removes.

\subsection{Primary input-sensitivity rankings}\label{sec:results-morris}

Figure~\ref{fig:morris} shows the Morris $\mu^{*}$, the input-importance metric, for the six outputs across the eight cells and seven swept inputs. H1 is supported in 48 of 48 cell $\times$ output combinations: the top-three inputs account for 0.658--0.998 of the summed $\mu^{*}$, with a median top-3 fraction of 0.928. The lowest values, from 0.658 to 0.727, all fall in severe-ARDS gas-exchange cells, where shunt physiology blunts every oxygenation input at once. Cardiovascular outputs are cardio-input dominated, output HR by input HR and MAP by SBP in every cell with no measurable ventilator cross-talk, while gas-exchange outputs are ventilator-driven: FiO$_2$ dominates PaO$_2$ in mild and moderate ARDS and collapses to a near-flat dependence in severe ARDS.

Influence is not proportionality. Applying the preregistered $\sigma / \mu^{*} > 0.6$ threshold to each cell's top-three inputs, and retaining only combinations that clear it in all five bootstrap draws, leaves 23 of the 144, spread over 17 of the 48 combinations; all seven inputs and all four scenarios appear, so non-linearity is common rather than localised. Delivered tidal volume is the most affected output, in 8 of the 23, and is also the channel whose oscillation dominates the \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flags (\S\ref{sec:results-vcac}), so the two findings cannot appear on the same runs: in S1 the delivered-Vt coefficient of variation exceeds the 0.05 threshold on 139 of 320 runs and no Vt combination is estimable, against 36 of 320 runs and 3 estimable combinations in S3. The map of non-linearity is therefore bounded by run survival --- the regime that destabilises delivered volume most is the one in which that instability is least measurable. The ranking is cross-verified against a SHAP surrogate in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s13}.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{VC-AC tidal-volume dyssynchrony and the $-2$~cmH$_2$O lever}{VC-AC tidal-volume dyssynchrony and the -2 cmH2O lever}}\label{sec:results-vcac}

Across the full sweep, the single largest source of \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flags is tidal-volume (Vt) oscillation under VC-AC ventilation, quantified as the delivered Vt coefficient of variation over the final 60~s of the 120~s simulation. Vt oscillation is the \emph{primary} such flag on 20.6~\% of runs: the delivered breath volume never settles to a single value but keeps changing from breath to breath, a genuinely oscillating output rather than a solver failure. The effect concentrates in healthy adults and falls markedly with increasing ARDS severity, strongest where the set respiratory rate is high and lung compliance is normal. This is the in-silico signature of \emph{patient--ventilator dyssynchrony}, well documented clinically in volume-control ventilation~\cite{figueroacasas2016}; the specific conditions that produce it here are our own in-silico finding.

A preregistered follow-up experiment isolated the mechanism. Re-running 64 of the affected configurations with Pulse's \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerPressure} set to $-2$~cmH$_2$O collapses the median Vt CV from 0.351 to 0.016, and 78.1~\% of the locked-trigger reruns drop below the 0.05 steady-state threshold (Figure~\ref{fig:corrections}a). The lock is an exploration lever for deterministic Vt delivery, not a clinical recommendation: it decouples breath delivery from the engine's modelled respiratory drive, whose coupling is what reproduces effort-dependent breath-to-breath variability in Pulse's default configuration. In a real patient the same shift would leave sub-threshold inspiratory efforts unrewarded, a form of patient--ventilator dyssynchrony~\cite{thille2006} that is clinically undesirable~\cite{blanch2015}. The full protocol, the worst-affected configuration and the per-run values are in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s8}.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Transient hyperoxia regime at FiO$_2$~=~1.0}{Transient hyperoxia regime at FiO2 = 1.0}}\label{sec:results-hyperoxia}

A second cluster of preregistered classifier flags concentrates on the highest inspired-oxygen settings. Within the locked 120-second simulation window, Pulse's steady-state mean PaO$_2$ exceeds the classifier's upper bound of 600~mmHg on 34 of the 126 \texttt{implausible}-flagged runs, and 91~\% of these excursions occur at FiO$_2 \geq 0.95$. A preregistered 60-run rerun --- each configuration re-executed from time zero with the recorded duration raised from 120 to 300 seconds and the plateau taken over the final 60 seconds ($t = 240$--$300$~s) --- discriminated an engine defect from a window-length artefact, and supports the latter: in the S1 (healthy) cohort, 40 of 44 reruns (90.9~\%) converged to a stable plateau (Figure~\ref{fig:corrections}b), and the converged S1 plateau median was 643~mmHg, sitting approximately 20~mmHg \emph{below} the alveolar-gas-equation prediction~\cite{west2020respiratory} rather than above it, which rules out a cohort-wide oxygenation runaway. It does not establish that the plateau level itself is right, which would take validation against measured arterial data and is outside the scope of a curation map.

The practical consequence is direct: hyperoxia studies on Pulse should record at least 300 seconds of simulation before extracting a steady-state plateau. The 120-second window remains adequate for cardiovascular and ventilation outputs. Extended-window runs on healthy lungs at FiO$_2$ = 1.0 can additionally enter a separate solver-instability regime, affecting the female reference body far more often than the male, so users running them should screen each PaO$_2$ trajectory individually. The full protocol, the time-to-plateau statistic and the instability characterisation are in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s9}.

\subsection{Sex-stratified solver divergence and blood-volume mechanism}\label{sec:results-sex}

Across the full 1{,}280-run sweep, solver divergence is rare but strikingly sex-stratified. 20 runs (1.6~\%) failed to complete with finite output, and \emph{all 20 fell on the male reference body}, against zero divergences across the 640 runs on the female reference body (Figure~\ref{fig:sex_bv}a). The 20 divergence events are not spread uniformly through the input space: they span only 8 distinct operational-input vectors, and FiO$_2$ sat at its 1.0 boundary in 13 of the 20.

A preregistered follow-up experiment isolated the mechanism. Pulse's \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp} computes blood-volume baseline from a sex-independent allometric relation on body weight, yielding 4{,}803~mL for the 67.3~kg male reference body and 3{,}996~mL for the 56.2~kg female body. The two bodies therefore share essentially the same blood volume per kilogram ($\approx$71~mL\,kg$^{-1}$, within standard clinical estimates~\cite{nadler1962}), so what separates them is absolute volume rather than sex-specific physiology. Re-executing the 20 originally-diverged male inputs with an explicit \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline} override took the divergence count from 20 / 20 at the default to 0 / 20 at both an intermediate 4{,}400~mL bracket and the engine's own setup floor of 4{,}082~mL (Figure~\ref{fig:sex_bv}b). Divergence is one axis of sex-stratified behaviour; the steady-state values separate far less. Across the 24 (scenario $\times$ output) comparisons, the Wasserstein distance between the male and female clean-run distributions exceeds its permutation null on six, of which five survive Benjamini--Hochberg correction (Methods~\S\ref{sec:methods-stats}); all five are gas-exchange channels --- PaCO$_2$ in S1--S3, with SpO$_2$ and PaO$_2$ in S4. The full override protocol is in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s10}.

One caveat governs how any cross-body contrast should be read. The comparison is made on clean runs, and how many runs stay clean itself differs by sex --- 144 of 160 against 122 of 160 in S3, for instance --- so the two distributions are drawn from different subsets of the input grid, and a surviving distance cannot separate an effect on the physiology from an effect on which configurations survive the flag filter. Gas-exchange contrasts add a systematic ventilation-dose difference: at every matched absolute tidal-volume setting, the female reference body receives 19.8~\% more tidal volume per kilogram of actual body weight and 36.1~\% more per kilogram of ideal body weight, computed using the sex-specific Devine relation that Pulse applies at patient setup (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s6-dose}). Switching Pulse's sex flag also switches body composition, basal metabolic rate, haemoglobin, cardiovascular sizing and, through ideal body weight, anatomic dead space at once (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s10-breadth}), so no cross-body distance can be attributed to any single physiological pathway. None of this weakens the divergence mechanism above, which is an absolute-blood-volume effect demonstrated by direct intervention rather than inferred from a cross-body contrast.

\subsection{Secondary observations}\label{sec:results-secondary}

Four further observations bear on downstream interpretation --- Pulse's ARDS impairment-fraction solver-instability band, a compensatory tachycardia that decouples output from input heart rate under hypoxemia, a preregistered Sobol bolt-on that was triggered but not executed because the triggered design exceeded its locked compute budget, and a limitation of the flatline rule itself --- are stated in full in the Supplementary Information and indexed in \S\,\ref{sec:s17-summary}.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

This work delivers the first open, preregistered reliability atlas for the Pulse Physiology Engine across several adult critical-care operational scenarios. Two operator-actionable corrections and a sex-specific reliability axis emerge from the 1{,}280-run sweep: a single configuration setting that makes VC-AC tidal-volume delivery consistent and repeatable for synthetic-cohort generation, at the cost of removing the effort-dependent dyssynchrony Pulse models, so it is a data-generation tool rather than a clinical ventilation recommendation (\S\ref{sec:results-vcac}); a steady-state-window correction for hyperoxia simulation at FiO$_2 = 1.0$ (\S\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia}); and, on a separate axis, a previously uncharacterised sex-stratified solver-divergence cluster with an identified blood-volume mechanism (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}). The accompanying per-cell traffic-light curation map (Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light}) is the principal deliverable for downstream users, and its criteria carry a material post hoc deviation from the preregistered wording at the yellow--red boundary, declared with its sensitivity analysis in \S\ref{sec:methods-traffic}. It is a curation and review-priority instrument, not a general physiological validation of the engine: credibility frameworks in biomedical modelling treat sensitivity analysis and a bounded domain of applicability as components of assessment, while validation itself requires comparison against measured data this work does not perform.

For machine-learning pipelines that train on Pulse-generated synthetic patient cohorts, the four-flag classifier doubles as a run-level data-quality contract. That contract rests on a null result bounded to the sampled envelope: within Pulse's accepted setup envelope, no submitted input was silently altered at ingestion. The preregistered rejection-log detector fired on 0 of 1{,}280 runs, and the post hoc instantiation audit found exact agreement between the value submitted and the value Pulse serialised. This concerns setup-time input handling only; it does not imply that physiological states stay fixed once the simulation runs, nor establish Pulse's behaviour outside the sampled envelope. Green cells in Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light} are eligible for default inclusion under the stated criteria, subject to use-case-specific validation; yellow cells should be filtered to the clean subset. Green denotes eligibility under both non-degenerate readings of the rule; it does not establish physiological validity.

Three traps documented here merit explicit attention. The sex-stratified divergence cluster (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}) means that pooling male and female runs from the input-extreme regime without flag-stratified filtering ingests a spurious sex covariate. The compensatory-tachycardia decoupling (\S\ref{sec:results-secondary}) means the input heart rate and the output heart rate are not interchangeable in ARDS scenarios: a model trained on Pulse cohorts would otherwise learn a compensatory response as a steady-state feature rather than as an active response to hypoxemia. The third survives flag-based curation altogether: sex-conditioned distributional differences remain detectable even among clean runs (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}), without a single mechanism or a scenario-independent direction of effect.

For the computational-physiology community, the atlas matters in two ways. First, it directly addresses the Pulse maintainers' own statement that no formal sensitivity or uncertainty quantification has been performed on user-facing operational inputs~\cite{pulsefaq}: the Morris screen shows sensitivity concentrated in a few inputs, with the top-three inputs carrying at least half of the summed $\mu^{*}$ in 48 of 48 cell $\times$ output combinations (median 93~\%). Second, the method carries over to other engines. It pairs a Morris screen of the operational inputs with a four-flag classifier that sorts every run into a category instead of discarding any. To our knowledge no comparable publicly available reliability atlas combining Morris screening with explicit clamping analysis has been published for either BioGears or HumMod~\cite{hester2011hummod}, and the same recipe could be applied to them by wrapping each engine's own inputs and outputs.

Prior external falsification work on MIMIC-III is complementary rather than overlapping: Cornish et al.~\cite{cornish2023} applied a causal-falsification framework to Pulse on MIMIC-III sepsis trajectories and reported 85 total Hoeffding-inequality rejections. Of these, 82 of 85 fall outside the four-channel overlap with this atlas (HR, MAP, PaO$_2$, PaCO$_2$): 80 on channels the present atlas does not assess, plus 2 on partial-overlap pressure channels; only 3 of 85 fall inside it. The two studies therefore expose different weaknesses of Pulse rather than the same one. Cornish et al.\ test its chemistry and thermoregulation outputs against a real patient cohort undergoing clinical interventions; the present atlas tests its cardio-respiratory response to operational inputs. Neither maps how operational inputs drive the electrolyte channels Cornish's test flags most often, so a metabolic-and-renal reliability atlas is the natural next step.

Beyond guidance for downstream users, the atlas also serves the engine's maintainers: it provides, to our knowledge, the first systematic characterisation of an ARDS impairment-fraction solver-instability band ($[0.879, 0.945]$), together with further engine-level observations that target tooling, documentation and validation infrastructure rather than Pulse's underlying physiology.

Six scope limits bound the conclusions; each is set out with its evidence in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s2-scope}. (i)~The atlas pins to Pulse v4.3.1, results applying identically to the build-only v4.3.2. (ii)~Patient anatomy is two adult reference bodies, so mass and height variation within sex is uncharacterised. (iii)~The envelope covers adult VC-AC ventilation only; pediatric translation, pressure-targeted modes and transient within-run dynamics are out of scope by design. (iv)~The Morris estimator discards any input pair containing a flagged run, which eliminates every clean FiO$_2$ pair in the healthy and severe-ARDS female cells, so the FiO$_2$ rows of Figure~\ref{fig:morris} are left blank. (v)~The hard physiological bounds judge whether a \emph{sustained} steady-state mean is plausible, not whether a value is instantaneously attainable; they are a screening standard, not a claim that exceptional clinical trajectories are impossible. (vi)~Pulse enforces DBP/SBP $\leq$ 0.75, and configuration-time clipping shortened 9 of 20 DBP steps, so some SBP and DBP effects are not strict fixed-step Morris estimates. Two further caveats are of evidence rather than scope: no trajectory-count convergence analysis was performed, and shunt fraction was not recorded, so shunt-mediated interpretations throughout this work remain physiologically motivated rather than directly tested. Together, these limitations define the scope within which the atlas's conclusions should be interpreted.

The atlas is intended as foundational infrastructure rather than a terminal deliverable. The four-flag classifier and the per-cell traffic-light are reusable instruments that downstream users can apply to their own configurations without re-running the present sweep, and the pipeline is preregistered, reproducible and openly licensed. By characterising the adult operational envelope first, the atlas provides the methodological basis a pediatric translation layer would need, though whether these adult-model reliability patterns transfer to pediatric physiology remains an open question. A complementary metabolic-and-renal atlas would cover the electrolyte channels Cornish et al.~\cite{cornish2023} flag most often, and validating the cell-level classifications against real-world clinical databases such as MIMIC-IV would test them against recorded care. Simulated and recorded cohorts are complementary rather than interchangeable: mechanistic simulation provides controlled inputs and directly accessible model states, whereas recorded clinical data retain the messiness of real practice.

Ultimately, because Pulse-generated cohorts are already used as machine-learning training data, and because ventilation automation is already reaching the bedside --- a recent multicentre randomised trial of closed-loop ventilation improved overall ventilation quality yet showed no difference in patient-centred outcomes~\cite{sinnige2026active} --- knowing where a simulator is reliable is a precondition for using it to train the models that will inform such care.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Pulse engine and configuration}\label{sec:methods-pulse}

All simulations used Pulse v4.3.1 (Kitware, Apache-2.0)~\cite{bray2019}, a real-time-capable lumped-parameter whole-body physiology engine whose respiratory sub-model is a 5-compartment lung~\cite{pulserespmethod}. The engine was executed inside a single \texttt{pulse-atlas-engine} Docker image, pinned for the entire 1{,}280-run sweep so that every simulation runs against bit-identical engine binaries. Runs were orchestrated by a thin Python layer around Pulse's command-line interface, which writes one JSON configuration per Morris input vector --- reference body, ARDS scenario condition, ventilator action set, and the seven operational inputs at their sampled values --- and spawns a container against that image.

Re-running the pipeline reproduces the canonical outputs bit-for-bit at three successive stages, each checked against its own committed artefact: the input sample, the per-cell aggregate tables, and the derived statistics. Mechanical ventilation was restricted to volume-controlled assist-control (VC-AC) mode so that all runs remain directly comparable; pressure-targeted modes lie outside the atlas's scope. The dispatch hardware and the full environment are given in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s12-env}.

\subsection{Patient reference bodies}\label{sec:methods-patients}

Two adult reference bodies were used: a male body (age 18, 67.3~kg, 180~cm) and a female body (age 18, 56.2~kg, 163~cm). Both weights are U.S.\ Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) growth-chart 50th-percentile weight-for-age values for each sex~\cite{kuczmarski2002cdc}, taken from the CDC row at \texttt{Agemos}~=~216.5. The female height matches the CDC 50th percentile and Pulse's own standard female height; the male height is researcher-fixed. Supplement~\S\ref{sec:s3-bodies} carries the full provenance.

Pulse also refuses some setup values outright. Two of those constraints act on inputs swept here, a gate on systolic and diastolic blood pressure and acceptance bands on the initial heart and respiratory rates; both were verified against the Pulse source and by boundary-probe runs before the sweep (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s2}), and the sampler ranges were set inside them. A third, Pulse's limit on the pulse-pressure ratio, is deliberately not imposed on the Morris sample, because that would distort the sample geometry the elementary-effect estimator depends on; it is applied one step later, when each configuration is written, and every run records the requested value, the applied value and a clip flag (\S\ref{sec:methods-sweep}). Every configuration actually submitted to Pulse therefore lies inside the accepted envelope. A fourth constraint, a hard per-patient floor on the male body's blood-volume baseline, is never exercised by the sweep and is characterised separately in \S\ref{sec:results-sex}.

\subsection{Sweep design}\label{sec:methods-sweep}

Seven user-facing operational inputs were swept across their verified Pulse acceptance ranges: heart rate (HR, 50--110~bpm), systolic and diastolic blood pressure (SBP 90--120~mmHg, DBP 60--80~mmHg with the post-clip pulse-pressure constraint), respiratory rate (RR, 8--20~breaths\,min$^{-1}$), tidal volume (Vt, 300--700~mL), inspired oxygen fraction (FiO$_2$, 0.21--1.0) and positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP, 0--15~cmH$_2$O). Each range is anchored to a primary verification source, table by table, in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s2}. Mean arterial pressure was derived as $\mathrm{DBP} + (\mathrm{SBP} - \mathrm{DBP})/3$ rather than swept independently. The swept heart-rate and blood-pressure values are setup interventions honoured at ingestion rather than setpoints maintained against the engine's own reflexes: Pulse re-baselines those fields to the stabilised state at the end of stabilisation, and no action used here rewrites them afterwards.

Four scenarios spanned the adult critical-care severity range: a healthy baseline (S1, ARDS impairment fraction = 0) and three ARDS severities (S2, mild, impairment = 0.26; S3, moderate, impairment = 0.60; S4, severe, impairment = 0.95). At a reference ventilation setting based on the ARDSnet low-tidal-volume framework, these impairment fractions place each scenario in a Berlin P/F band, and both that calibration and its sweep-wide FiO$_2$ dependence are tabulated in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5-berlin}. An atlas cell is therefore anchored to a clinically recognisable state rather than to an arbitrary engine setting. The three severities also bracket the 0.91--0.945 solver-instability zone documented at design time (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5}); the atlas's own continuous-severity sweep subsequently widened that band to $[0.879, 0.945]$ (\S\ref{sec:results-secondary}). Pulse represents severity as a single per-lung scalar driving four hard-coded, adult-calibrated interpolation curves, mapped in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5-mapping}.

Sensitivity was screened with the Morris elementary-effects design~\cite{morris1991, campolongo2007}, a global screening method whose cost is linear in the number of inputs. The sample was generated with SALib~\cite{iwanaga2022,herman2017salib} using 20 trajectories at 4 grid levels per input and random seed 42. Each trajectory contributes $7 + 1 = 8$ model evaluations, giving 160 runs per (scenario $\times$ sex) cell and 1{,}280 runs across the full sweep. The trajectory count was fixed at 20 in preregistration as a computationally tractable design; it was not selected through a trajectory-count convergence analysis. Each run executed for 120 seconds of simulated time, with output channels sampled at 5-second intervals (13 samples in the final 60-second steady-state window).

\subsection{Steady-state classifier}\label{sec:methods-detector}

Every simulation was classified by a four-flag steady-state classifier before any statistical analysis. \texttt{clamped} is the preregistered label for a run whose Pulse setup log matches any of 4 documented input-rejection patterns. This log-based detector identifies \emph{explicit} setup rejection and cannot by itself detect a silent substitution; a post hoc input-instantiation audit closes that gap and is reported in \S\ref{sec:results-overview}. \texttt{diverged} fires when the simulation failed to complete with finite output, the CSV is missing, or the engine exited with a non-zero status.

\texttt{implausible} fires when any steady-state output mean exceeds the locked hard physiological bounds: HR [20, 220]~bpm, MAP [30, 180]~mmHg, Vt [50, 1{,}500]~mL, SpO$_2$ [50, 100]~\%, PaO$_2$ [20, 600]~mmHg, PaCO$_2$ [10, 120]~mmHg. Unlike the swept inputs, the six tracked outputs have no acceptance envelope in Pulse: the engine reports whatever its solver produces and never rejects an output value. These bounds are therefore ours, a plausibility standard applied after the run rather than a constraint the engine enforces, and each is anchored to primary critical-care and respiratory-physiology literature in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-hard-bounds}.

\texttt{not\_steady\_state} fires when, over the final 60-second window, any tracked output flatlines, drifts beyond its per-channel clinical threshold, or varies beyond its coefficient-of-variation threshold. A flatline is variability below 1~\% of the mean \emph{and} a mean within 0.1~\% of a hard bound --- $\geq$~99.9~\% for the SpO$_2$ ceiling --- a rule written to catch a solver that has pinned an output and stopped advancing; the drift thresholds are tabulated in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-drift}.

The four flags are independent presence counts, so a single simulation can fire several. Analyses that need one category per run resolve it by a fixed priority order, \texttt{clamped} $>$ \texttt{diverged} $>$ \texttt{implausible} $>$ \texttt{not\_steady\_state} $>$ \texttt{clean}, following the chronology of failure within a run. The [60, 120]~s window was preregistered before any sweep data was collected, and a 22-run pilot on the healthy-male cell verified five preregistered gating criteria, among them detector self-consistency and Pulse determinism.

\subsection{Traffic-light curation map}\label{sec:methods-traffic}

The per-cell curation map (Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light}) reduces each (scenario, sex, output) cell to one green / yellow / red verdict, built from two locked ingredients: the cell's combined diverged-plus-implausible flag rate, and its deviation from a baseline. The baseline for each output is that output's mean over the clean runs of the healthy-male (S1, M) cell, and the $10~\%$ and $30~\%$ deviation bands were preregistered before any data were collected; the full colour criteria are stated in the legend to Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light}. Scoring every cell against a single (S1, M) baseline keeps male and female cells on one comparable scale, at the cost of folding legitimate male--female differences into the female cells' deviation term; Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-traffic} reports the re-scoring check that verifies this choice does not drive the classification.

\textbf{Post hoc deviation from the preregistered traffic-light rule.} The four-flag run-level classifier and its thresholds were preregistered, but the traffic-light aggregation was not implemented exactly as written. The preregistration stated that a cell would be red when its deviation exceeded $30~\%$ or when ``any flag'' was raised; the implemented rule treats \texttt{diverged} and \texttt{implausible} as hard flags, assigns yellow whenever their combined rate exceeds zero, and reserves red for a combined hard-flag rate above $30~\%$. The repository establishes that this implementation was committed after preregistration but not whether it was defined before or after the sweep outputs were inspected, so we record the altered yellow--red boundary as a material deviation. Under the implemented rule the map contains 7 green / 29 yellow / 12 red combinations; under the literal reading it would contain 7 green / 3 yellow / 38 red, with the same seven green and the same twelve red. We retain the implemented rule as the primary display because it preserves a graded distinction without creating a single additional green verdict, and neither the classifier, the map nor the reported counts were changed in response to this finding. The preregistered reading is reported alongside them as a sensitivity analysis in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-tl-sensitivity}.

\subsection{Statistical analyses}\label{sec:methods-stats}

Morris $\mu^{*}$ and $\sigma$ were computed per (scenario, sex, input, output) from the SALib sample~\cite{iwanaga2022,herman2017salib}. Each elementary effect divides the change in the output by the size of the input step expressed on that input's swept range mapped to $[0,1]$, so a $\mu^{*}$ is an output change per full-range move of its input. Indices carry non-parametric 95~\% bootstrap confidence intervals from 1{,}000 trajectory-level resamples. Four hypotheses were preregistered before data collection: H1, the top-three inputs account for at least half of the summed $\mu^{*}$ for each output; H2, at least 5~\% of the Morris sample hits at least one silent clamp; H3, at least one solver-divergence zone exists outside the previously characterised 0.91--0.945 band within the seven-input adult envelope; H4, the male and female reference bodies differ in clamping, divergence or $\mu^{*}$ ranking on at least one output, with no direction prespecified. Hypothesis~1 was evaluated per cell $\times$ output on the point-estimate top-3 fraction of summed $\mu^{*}$; hypotheses 2, 3 and 4 are resolved in \S\ref{sec:results-overview}.

Sex differences in the steady-state distributions were measured by per-(scenario $\times$ output) Wasserstein distances between the male and female clean-run distributions, tested against a permutation null of 2{,}000 random relabellings at the two original group sizes. The resulting 24-test family was corrected by Benjamini--Hochberg control of the false-discovery rate at $q = 0.05$; with 2{,}000 relabellings the smallest non-zero empirical tail proportion is 1/2{,}000, and two tests reaching zero are reported conservatively as $p < 0.001$. Cross-sex agreement of input-importance rankings was quantified by the Spearman rank correlation between male and female $\mu^{*}$ vectors per (scenario, output).

A Sobol bolt-on was preregistered as a conditional escalation on any top-three input whose $\sigma / \mu^{*}$ ratio reaches 0.6. Applied as preregistered, the trigger fires on 72 of the 144 top-three inputs, spanning 43 of the 48 combinations, while the same preregistration fixed the bolt-on's compute envelope at 1{,}500 additional runs; because Sobol needs an independent sample per cell, even the narrowest reading of the cost formula puts 43 cells at 11{,}008 runs. The bolt-on was therefore infeasible within the budget its own protocol declared, and is reported rather than executed. Because the preregistered trigger rests on the point ratio alone, we add a post hoc sensitivity analysis in which trajectories are resampled 1{,}000 times per combination and $\sigma / \mu^{*}$ is recomputed jointly on each draw; combinations whose lower bound clears 0.6 in all five draws are characterised in \S\ref{sec:results-morris}, and the full decomposition is in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-sobol}.

The full sampler, sweep driver, four-flag classifier, statistical-analysis pipeline and audit ledger are released open-source. The sampling design, the input ranges, the four flags and their thresholds, and the analysis plan were preregistered before data collection; the audit ledger was developed afterwards, to re-derive the computed claims in this paper from the canonical output artefacts.

\subsection{Use of AI tools}\label{sec:methods-ai}

\noindent In the interest of full methodological transparency, large language models (LLMs) were used substantially throughout this work for drafting, editing, refactoring, and debugging both the analysis-pipeline code and the manuscript prose, under direct supervision by S.N. A second LLM, in a separate session, was repeatedly prompted to critique the first model's outputs during iterative review. That loop is reported as an additional source of challenge on text and code. The safeguards on that use are external to the models and are set out at the end of this subsection.

One side of the loop was always a Claude model (Anthropic), used locally through the Claude Code agent in the terminal, configured at the \texttt{xhigh} thinking-effort default: Claude Opus 4.7 (\texttt{claude-opus-4-7}) for the initial drafting, Opus 4.8 (\texttt{claude-opus-4-8}) through the first supervisor-review phase, and Opus 5 (\texttt{claude-opus-5}) for the audit and synthesis rounds that followed. The other side was a separate web session, first Google Gemini Pro 3.1 (extended-thinking mode) and, from 3~August~2026, OpenAI ChatGPT (GPT-5.6 Sol, High reasoning level); in that role the second model provided editorial assistance, consistency checks, and methodological discussion.

A manuscript-formatting pass was performed by Claude Opus 4.7; global clinical review by the medical authors (A.W., C.S., P.J., and M.S.). The Opus 4.8 revision rounds responded to the first round of co-author and supervisor review: manuscript re-reading, copy-editing, and the associated text and reference modifications. The Opus 5 rounds covered in-repository replication of the ARDS-severity-to-Berlin calibration, the heart-rate and respiratory-rate boundary probes, and primary-source audits of the reference list, of the statistical analyses, and of the paper's quantitative claims against their source artefacts; that work restored the Sobol promotion trigger to its preregistered top-three form, added the post-hoc ratio-aware bootstrap on $\sigma / \mu^{*}$, and versioned the script that generates it. They also covered the September 2026 condensation pass, in which the Introduction, Results, Discussion, Methods, figure legends and abstract were rewritten to about half their length with the displaced material migrated to the Supplementary Information, together with the audits that followed that pass and the corrections those audits produced.

Responsibility rests with the authors. All AI-assisted outputs, including editorial revisions, consistency checks, and methodological interpretations, were personally reviewed and verified by S.N.; every AI-assisted code change was reviewed, tested, and committed by S.N.; and all preregistered thresholds, hypotheses, statistical-test choices, and experimental decisions were authored and locked by S.N.\ before the corresponding analyses were run. Quantitative claims are traceable, as applicable, to primary sources, the preregistration, or entries in the open audit ledger released alongside the code.
