\abstract{Pulse is an actively maintained open-source adult physiology simulator, yet no formal sensitivity analysis of its operational inputs has been published. We present the first reliability atlas across Pulse's critical-care envelope: Morris elementary-effects sampling of seven inputs, four ARDS severities, two bodies (1{,}280 simulations). A four-flag classifier (clamping, divergence, implausible, non-steady-state) surfaces failure modes: two are operator-actionable; a third shows reliability varies with sex. Tidal-volume instability (20.6~\% of runs) reflects patient--ventilator dyssynchrony; locking the VC-AC inspiration trigger to $-2$~cmH$_2$O makes Vt deterministic. At FiO$_2$~=~1.0 transient hyperoxia makes 120-second runs underestimate plateau PaO$_2$, which 300-second runs resolve near the alveolar-gas ceiling (median 643~mmHg). Solver divergence fell entirely on the male body (20/1{,}280); reducing its blood-volume baseline to the 4{,}082~mL floor eliminated it. They map where Pulse is trustworthy and provide an Apache-2.0 data-quality filter for synthetic patient cohorts used as machine-learning training data.}

\keywords{Pulse Physiology Engine, digital twin, synthetic patient data, Morris elementary effects, ARDS, machine learning training data, operational envelope, reproducibility}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The Pulse Physiology Engine (Kitware, Apache-2.0) is an actively maintained open-source whole-body human physiology simulator~\cite{bray2019}. It uses a lumped-parameter approach (modelling physiological systems as small networks of coupled compartments rather than full three-dimensional anatomy) to deliver real-time cardiovascular, respiratory, gas-exchange, and pharmacological simulation. Pulse has been applied to ventilator-protocol safety analysis during the COVID-19 pandemic~\cite{webb2020}, to mechanical-ventilator implementation and validation against the ASL 5000 breathing simulator (IngMar Medical)~\cite{webb2022vent}, and to external statistical assessment as a digital-twin candidate for retrospective clinical cohorts~\cite{cornish2023}. Across these settings, Pulse generates synthetic patient trajectories that downstream researchers use for hypothesis testing~\cite{webb2020}, device validation~\cite{webb2022vent}, and computational physiology studies~\cite{bray2019}. Yet despite this footprint, prior work has probed Pulse's \emph{outputs} --- validating them against reference data, or, in one external study, falsifying intervention--outcome behaviour on a real cohort --- while no public map of how the engine responds across its user-facing \emph{input} envelope (sensitivity, silent clamping, solver divergence) has been published. Each downstream user re-encounters the same engine limitations independently, and each validation pipeline re-derives its own working envelope by trial and error.

The Pulse maintainers state on the project's public FAQ that ``we have not performed a systematic forward propagation or inverse quantification of model uncertainty, nor have we conducted a formal sensitivity analysis''~\cite{pulsefaq}. The upstream repository carries a single 2020 sensitivity study (\texttt{STUDY\_SENSITIVITY\_ANALYSIS\_1\_0\_0}), but it targets internal lumped-parameter circuit values (cardiovascular and respiratory resistances and compliances) for model calibration --- a different parameter class from the user-facing operational inputs that downstream applications actually specify. The only prior external assessment of Pulse, by Cornish et al.~\cite{cornish2023}, applied a causal-falsification framework to MIMIC-III sepsis trajectories. Their findings identified intervention-outcome inaccuracies concentrating on electrolyte, metabolic, and acid--base outputs that lie largely outside the present atlas's six-output cardio-respiratory panel. The complementary operational-input reliability question --- how Pulse responds across its acceptance envelope when its primary user-facing controls are varied --- has remained unaddressed.

We address this gap with the first open reliability atlas across Pulse's adult critical-care operational envelope. We swept the seven user-facing inputs: heart rate (HR), systolic and diastolic blood pressure (SBP, DBP), respiratory rate (RR), tidal volume (Vt), inspired oxygen fraction (FiO$_2$), and positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP). We tracked six steady-state outputs --- HR, mean arterial pressure (MAP), tidal volume delivered, peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO$_2$), arterial oxygen partial pressure (PaO$_2$), and arterial carbon dioxide partial pressure (PaCO$_2$) --- across four Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) severity scenarios (S1 healthy baseline, impairment fraction 0; S2 mild, 0.26; S3 moderate, 0.60; S4 severe, 0.95) and two adult reference patient bodies (male: 18~y, 67.3~kg, 180~cm; female: 18~y, 56.2~kg, 163~cm). Inputs are screened with Morris elementary-effects sampling~\cite{morris1991,campolongo2007}, a one-at-a-time perturbation design that ranks each input's influence on each output and flags non-linearity (Methods~\S\ref{sec:methods-sweep}). Every simulation is then classified by a four-flag steady-state detector. The four flags record whether Pulse silently clamped an out-of-range input, the solver diverged, a steady-state output fell outside hard physiological bounds, or the run never settled within the simulation window. Crucially, the detector categorizes failures rather than discarding them, so the atlas stays complete across the full input grid. Three findings emerge from the sweep. Two are operator-actionable corrections: a tidal-volume dyssynchrony lever for volume-controlled assist-control (VC-AC) ventilation, applied at the inspiration trigger, and a longer steady-state window that corrects hyperoxia simulation at FiO$_2$~=~1.0. The third is a previously uncharacterized, sex-stratified cluster of solver divergence --- a distinct reliability axis, traced to a blood-volume mechanism.

The atlas is organised around three questions that decide whether a Pulse-generated cohort is fit for downstream use: how strongly each input drives each output (and where that influence turns non-linear), whether the engine silently clamps an out-of-range setting, and whether the solver diverges or never reaches steady state. Morris screening answers the first; the four-flag detector answers the other two. We anchor the sweep on the ARDS severity axis for two reasons: ventilation under respiratory impairment is a primary use of Pulse's respiratory sub-model and the regime where downstream reliability is least documented, and mapping where the adult engine is trustworthy is the prerequisite for the pediatric ARDS translation work this programme builds toward. The result is therefore not a single benchmark score but a per-cell reliability map and a reusable run classifier. Downstream users --- including the machine-learning and clinical-AI pipelines that increasingly draw on synthetic patient cohorts --- can apply it as a data-quality filter without re-running the sweep. The pipeline is preregistered, reproducible from a single Docker image with a fixed seed, and released open-source under Apache-2.0.

\subsection{Overview of the reliability atlas}\label{sec:results-overview}

The atlas comprises 1{,}280 simulations spanning eight cells of 160 runs each, where each \textit{cell} is a unique pairing of one ARDS severity scenario --- S1 (healthy baseline), S2 (mild), S3 (moderate), or S4 (severe) --- with one patient sex (male or female), giving the $4 \times 2 = 8$ cells (Figure~\ref{fig:design} maps the full study design). Each simulation is classified by a four-flag steady-state detector: \texttt{clamped} flags whether Pulse silently bent any setup input out of its acceptance band; \texttt{diverged} flags solver failure or non-finite output; \texttt{implausible} flags steady-state means outside hard physiological bounds; and \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flags runs whose final 60-second window fails preregistered drift or variance thresholds (Table~\ref{tab:flag_defs} summarises the four flags). Two implausibility bounds carry nuance: an SpO$_2$ above 100~\% is impossible by construction, while a \emph{sustained} mean SpO$_2$ below 50~\%---though briefly survivable in a real patient---is incompatible with a stable simulated steady state over the evaluation window~\cite{severinghaus1979,marini2018critical}; and the PaO$_2$ upper bound is set deliberately conservatively at 600~mmHg~\cite{west2020respiratory}, revisited in \S\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia}, because healthy-lung PaO$_2$ legitimately approaches the alveolar-gas ceiling ($\approx$663~mmHg) at FiO$_2$ = 1.0. The four flags are independent presence counts (a single run can fire several), and Table~\ref{tab:flag_summary} reports the per-cell breakdown.

Three features of Table~\ref{tab:flag_summary} carry the headline results. First, silent clamping never happens (0 / 1{,}280 across every cell): Pulse rejects an out-of-range input loudly, with a setup error, instead of quietly bending it back into range. Second, solver divergence is sex-specific --- 20 / 1{,}280 runs (1.6~\%) failed to converge, and all 20 were on the male reference body (we trace the cause in \S\ref{sec:results-sex}). Third, \texttt{not\_steady\_state} runs cluster in two regimes: healthy adults (S1), where large swings in the input tidal volume outrun Pulse's VC-AC controller, and severe ARDS (S4), where the fixed 120-second window is too short for the slow convergence of near-shunt physiology (both are detailed in \S\ref{sec:results-vcac} and \S\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia}). Overall, 663 of 1{,}280 runs (51.8~\%) are flag-clean under the locked classifier, and the per-cell traffic-light map (Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light}) gives the reliability picture at a glance. Beyond these headline results, the sweep yields a set of further analyses, each fully documented in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s16}: a finer mapping of the ARDS solver-instability band, the closure of the preregistered optional Sobol analysis (no cell cleared its promotion trigger once sampling uncertainty was accounted for), a breakdown of which runs fail the \texttt{not\_steady\_state} test and why, six concrete engineering recommendations for the Pulse maintainers, and tables comparing each input's importance ranking between the male and female bodies (cross-sex Spearman $\rho$).

\subsection{Primary input-sensitivity rankings}\label{sec:results-morris}

Figure~\ref{fig:morris} shows the Morris $\mu^{*}$ (mean of absolute elementary effects, the input-importance metric) for each of the six outputs across the eight cells and the seven swept inputs. Our first hypothesis (H1) was preregistered before any data were collected: in every cell, the top-three inputs should account for at least 50~\% of the summed $\mu^{*}$ on each output. This is a ``skewed sensitivity'' pattern --- the influence is concentrated in a few dominant inputs rather than spread evenly across all seven. The data support H1 on 48 of 48 cell $\times$ output combinations: for the typical output the three most influential inputs together account for about 93~\% of the summed $\mu^{*}$ (median top-3 fraction of 0.928, range 0.658--0.998), leaving the remaining inputs only a small share.

Four cross-output patterns emerge directly from Figure~\ref{fig:morris} (full per-cell tables in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3}). Cardiovascular outputs are cardio-input dominated: output HR is driven by input HR in every cell, and output MAP is driven by SBP in every cell, with no measurable cross-talk from ventilator settings. Gas-exchange outputs are ventilator-driven. PaO$_2$ is dominated by FiO$_2$ in S2 / S3 (mild and moderate ARDS) and collapses to a near-flat dependence in S4 (severe ARDS), correctly reproducing the textbook ARDS regime in which shunt physiology pins arterial oxygenation independent of inspired oxygen. PaCO$_2$ is dominated by tidal volume in most cells, consistent with the alveolar-ventilation identity $\dot{V}_A = \mathrm{RR} \cdot (V_T - V_D)$. Output Vt tracks input Vt at far higher $\mu^{*}$ magnitudes than any other input--output pair, reflecting the near-deterministic coupling of VC-AC ventilation outside the dyssynchrony regime described in \S\ref{sec:results-vcac}. SpO$_2$ shows the smallest $\mu^{*}$ magnitudes overall because the output sits at the hemoglobin-saturation ceiling across most of the swept range. The lowest top-3 fractions all fall in severe-ARDS gas-exchange cells (S4\_F SpO$_2$ at 0.658, S4\_M SpO$_2$ at 0.703), where the saturation ceiling distributes sensitivity more uniformly across the inputs --- these being the only cells in the atlas where the skewed-sensitivity property weakens.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{VC-AC tidal-volume dyssynchrony and the $-2$~cmH$_2$O lever}{VC-AC tidal-volume dyssynchrony and the -2 cmH2O lever}}\label{sec:results-vcac}

The largest contributor to the \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flag count across the full sweep is Vt oscillation under VC-AC ventilation. Within the locked 120-second simulation window, the delivered Vt coefficient of variation (Vt CV $= \sigma_{V_T}/\bar{V}_T$, the standard deviation of breath-by-breath delivered Vt divided by its mean over the final 60-second steady-state window) exceeds the 0.05 detector threshold on 20.6~\% of runs (Figure~\ref{fig:corrections}a, x-axis distribution). The pattern is concentrated on healthy adults (visible as the elevated S1 \texttt{not\_steady\_state} counts in Table~\ref{tab:flag_summary}) and fades as ARDS severity increases, consistent with a patient-ventilator-trigger interaction that activates when respiratory drive is high and lung compliance is normal --- a clinically documented form of patient-ventilator asynchrony~\cite{figueroacasas2016} rather than a Pulse-specific solver artefact.

We preregistered a follow-up experiment that isolated the mechanism. We re-ran 64 of the affected configurations with a single intervention: setting Pulse's \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerPressure} to $-2$~cmH$_2$O on the \texttt{SEMechanicalVentilatorVolumeControl} action (Pulse's volume-controlled ventilation action, which configures VC-AC mode; upstream source: \texttt{SEMechanicalVentilatorVolumeControl.cpp} lines 120--128). This disables the respiratory-model-driven patient trigger and replaces it with a fixed pressure-trigger threshold. The intervention collapses the median Vt CV from 0.351 to 0.016 (Figure~\ref{fig:corrections}a, dots well below the diagonal) and reduces the worst-case Vt CV from 1.826 to 0.018; 78.1~\% of locked-trigger reruns drop below the 0.05 steady-state threshold, satisfying both preregistered acceptance gates.

The practical consequence is best read as an exploration lever, not a clinical recommendation. Downstream users who need \emph{deterministic} Vt delivery---for example, generating stable synthetic cohorts for machine-learning pipelines---can set \texttt{InspirationPatientTriggerPressure} explicitly to $-2$~cmH$_2$O on every action to collapse the breath-to-breath variability and make Vt reproducible across the sweep. This determinism carries a cost that is invisible in the steady-state outputs themselves: locking the trigger to a fixed threshold decouples breath delivery from the engine's modelled patient respiratory drive, so simulated inspiratory efforts that do not reach the $-2$~cmH$_2$O threshold no longer trigger an assist breath (mandatory breaths continue at the set rate). In a real patient this corresponds to a shift from patient-responsive assist-control toward controlled ventilation, in which sub-threshold efforts go unrewarded---a form of patient--ventilator dyssynchrony~\cite{thille2006} that is clinically undesirable~\cite{blanch2015} and detectable on ventilator flow/pressure waveforms but most directly confirmed with specialised monitoring such as esophageal pressure~\cite{akoumianaki2014} or diaphragm electrical activity~\cite{sinderby1999}. Pulse's \emph{default} configuration, by contrast, couples triggering to the modelled respiratory drive and so reproduces effort-dependent breath-to-breath variability consistent with patient--ventilator asynchrony: physiologically the more faithful regime, but not steady-state-interpretable under the conventional final-60-second window. The $-2$~cmH$_2$O lock should therefore be understood as an instrument for controlled software exploration and deterministic synthetic-data generation, not as guidance for clinically realistic ventilation.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Transient hyperoxia regime at FiO$_2$~=~1.0}{Transient hyperoxia regime at FiO2 = 1.0}}\label{sec:results-hyperoxia}

A second cluster of preregistered detector flags concentrates on the highest inspired-oxygen settings. Within the locked 120-second simulation window, Pulse's steady-state mean PaO$_2$ exceeds the detector's locked upper bound of 600~mmHg on 34 of the 126 \texttt{implausible}-flagged runs, and 91~\% of these excursions occur at FiO$_2 \geq 0.95$. Two competing hypotheses fit this pattern. \textbf{Hypothesis A} --- Pulse is over-delivering oxygenation at the FiO$_2 = 1.0$ boundary (an engine defect). \textbf{Hypothesis B} --- the locked 120-second window is shorter than Pulse's PaO$_2$ relaxation time when shunt physiology is minimal and the alveolar partial pressure is climbing toward its theoretical ceiling (a window-length artefact rather than an engine defect).

A preregistered 60-run extended-simulation experiment discriminated between the two. Each rerun resumed from Pulse's saved engine state at the end of the original 120-second run and continued for an additional 300 seconds (420 seconds of total simulated time), with the plateau computed from the final 60 seconds of the extension (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s9}). In the S1 (healthy) cohort, 41 of 44 reruns (93.2~\%) converged to a stable plateau under the preregistered drift criterion (Figure~\ref{fig:corrections}b, S1 cluster); the converged S1 plateau median was 643~mmHg, sitting approximately 20~mmHg \emph{below} the simple alveolar-gas-equation prediction at sea level of $\approx$~663~mmHg (FiO$_2 = 1.0$, $P_B = 760$, $P_{\mathrm{H_2O}} = 47$, $\mathrm{PaCO}_2 = 40$, $R = 0.8$)~\cite{west2020respiratory}. The $\approx$~20~mmHg gap is consistent with Pulse's modelled alveolar-arterial difference rather than an oxygenation defect, which would be expected to push PaO$_2$ \emph{above} the theoretical ceiling, not below it. The S3 and S4 reruns (Figure~\ref{fig:corrections}b, orange and red clusters) sit far below the 600~mmHg classifier bound, in the physiologically expected range for moderate and severe ARDS at FiO$_2 = 1.0$.

The practical consequence is again direct: hyperoxia studies on Pulse should be run for at least 300 seconds of total simulated time---the point by which PaO$_2$ reaches approximately 95~\% of its plateau---before extracting a steady-state plateau. The 120-second window remains adequate for cardiovascular and ventilation outputs (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s9}) but not for oxygenation outputs at the FiO$_2 = 1.0$ extreme, where slow relaxation to the alveolar ceiling drives the apparent excursion above the conservative locked classifier bound. Downstream users should additionally avoid Vt $\times$ RR combinations exceeding $\sim$12~L/min minute ventilation on healthy lungs at FiO$_2 = 1.0$, where extended-window simulations enter a separate solver-instability regime (full characterisation in the companion technical monograph).

\subsection{Sex-stratified solver divergence and blood-volume mechanism}\label{sec:results-sex}

Across the full 1{,}280-run sweep, solver divergence is rare but strikingly sex-stratified. 20 runs (1.6~\%) failed to complete with finite output, and \emph{all 20 fell on the male reference body}: 4 in each of S1\_M, S2\_M, S3\_M and 8 in S4\_M, with zero divergences across the 640 runs on the female reference body (Figure~\ref{fig:sex_bv}a). The 20 divergence events are not spread uniformly through the input space. 16 of them concentrate on a specific compound input pattern --- low cardiovascular output (HR = 50, SBP = 90) combined with high respiratory rate (RR = 20) --- a clinically recognisable hypotensive--tachypneic configuration. The remaining 4 divergences all fall in the severe-ARDS cell (S4\_M) under different, higher-heart-rate configurations (HR 90--110), so severe ARDS destabilises the solver across a broader input range than the hypotensive--tachypneic pattern alone. FiO$_2$ was at its 1.0 boundary in 13 of the 20 cases (the other 7 near FiO$_2 \approx 0.74$, so the boundary is enriched but not necessary for divergence). Identical Morris input grids submitted to the female reference body (matched on age, condition string, and ventilator settings; differing only in patient mass, height, and sex flag) never trigger the same numerical edge case.

A preregistered follow-up experiment isolated the mechanism. Pulse's \texttt{SetupPatient.cpp} computes blood-volume baseline by the allometric relation $\text{BV} = 65.6 \cdot \text{weight}_{\text{kg}}^{1.02}$, yielding 4{,}803~mL for the 67.3~kg male reference body and 3{,}996~mL for the 56.2~kg female reference body. Because this allometric relation is sex-independent, the two bodies share essentially the same blood volume per kilogram ($\approx$71~mL\,kg$^{-1}$), within standard clinical estimates~\cite{nadler1962}; the resulting 17~\% difference is therefore an absolute-volume gap driven by body mass, not a sex-specific physiological difference (the formula does not reproduce the modest male--female difference in volume per kilogram reported clinically). This absolute-volume difference nonetheless propagates into the cardiovascular circuit's operating point. We re-executed the 20 originally-diverged male inputs with an explicit \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline} override at three levels: the default (4{,}803~mL), an intermediate bracket (4{,}400~mL, a 8.4~\% reduction), and the engine's setup-time minimum for this body (4{,}082~mL, a 15.0~\% reduction). The divergence count fell from 20 / 20 at the default to 0 / 20 at both reductions (Figure~\ref{fig:sex_bv}b); even the smaller 8.4~\% reduction toward the female-equivalent range already eliminates the entire divergence cluster.

A separate engine-level observation emerged from the same experiment: Pulse enforces a hard per-patient floor on \texttt{BloodVolumeBaseline} at setup time --- 4{,}082~mL on this male body. Setting BV below the floor triggers an explicit \texttt{[ERROR]} and exit. That floor marks where Pulse requires hypovolemia to be modelled by an explicit condition rather than a baseline override --- a 15.0~\% reduction below the computed default, comparable to the upper bound of a Class~I hemorrhage in the standard ATLS trauma classification~\cite{atls2018} (up to 15~\% of blood volume). Reducing the male baseline toward it lowers the absolute volume the circulation must reconcile with the prescribed operating point, rather than approaching a stability threshold --- consistent with the divergence reflecting an ill-conditioned circuit rather than a single divergent equation. The practical consequence for sex-comparison studies is that the female-computed BV (3{,}996~mL) sits 86~mL below the male floor, so the two reference bodies cannot be made BV-equivalent by a one-way transplant; a true sex-balanced synthetic cohort requires more than just patient-attribute matching. For downstream Pulse users training models on synthetic patient cohorts, the operational implication is concrete: pooling male and female runs from the input-extreme regime without flag-stratified filtering will introduce a spurious sex covariate at the male reference body's divergence boundary --- a structural artefact of Pulse's allometric BV baseline rather than a clinical phenomenon.

\subsection{Secondary observations}\label{sec:results-secondary}

Two additional findings sit at the boundary of the headline scope but matter for downstream interpretation. Each is summarised here in compact form and fully expanded in the Supplementary Information.

\textbf{First public characterisation of an ARDS impairment-fraction solver-instability band.} Pulse's own methodology documents ARDS severity as a continuous parameter on $[0, 1]$ and exemplifies the range with the round-tenths convention $\{0.0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.0\}$; published Pulse validation work and HowTo examples sample at most up to severity 0.9 and do not examine impairment fractions inside the interval 0.91--0.945. Three converging boundary probes within the companion technical work (full provenance in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5}) first identified 0.91--0.945 as a numerical bifurcation in the respiratory solver. The present atlas's 200-run continuous-severity sweep at midrange Morris inputs extends the empirical band to $[0.879, 0.945]$ on the low edge and distinguishes two divergence regimes. The first is an \emph{input-driven} regime concentrated on the male reference body at hypotensive--tachypneic--FiO$_2$~=~1.0 inputs (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}); the second is a \emph{severity-driven} regime in which both sexes diverge at midrange inputs across $[0.879, 0.945]$. The locked sweep places severe ARDS (S4) at impairment-fraction 0.95, intentionally above this band; downstream Pulse users should avoid $[0.879, 0.945]$ when stable steady-state outputs are required.

\textbf{Compensatory tachycardia decouples input HR from output HR under hypoxemia.} In S1 (healthy), the output HR settles near a normal resting value (mean 73.8~bpm, $\sigma = 20.4$~bpm across clean runs), whatever the input HR baseline. In S3 and S4, by contrast, the output HR climbs to 155.7~bpm and 147.1~bpm respectively, even though the inputs are still drawn from the same [50, 110]~bpm range --- a large sympathetic overshoot: Pulse's modelled baroreceptor and chemoreceptor reflexes respond to the severe hypoxemia and push the HR up. This reflex is strong enough to override the HR explicitly set as input. That has a direct consequence for machine-learning pipelines: a model trained on Pulse synthetic cohorts will learn this reflex as a steady-state feature rather than an active physiological response (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s15}).
\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

This work delivers the first open, preregistered reliability atlas for the Pulse Physiology Engine across several adult critical-care operational scenarios. Two operator-actionable corrections and a sex-specific reliability axis emerge from the 1{,}280-run sweep. \textbf{First}, a single configuration setting (\S\ref{sec:results-vcac}) that makes VC-AC tidal-volume delivery consistent and repeatable for synthetic-cohort generation. One caveat comes with it: this same setting also removes the realistic, effort-dependent asynchrony (the breath-to-breath variation) that Pulse models, so it is a data-generation tool, not a clinical ventilation recommendation. \textbf{Second}, a steady-state-window correction for hyperoxia simulation at FiO$_2 = 1.0$ (\S\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia}). \textbf{Third}, on a separate reliability axis rather than as a configuration fix, a previously uncharacterised sex-stratified solver-divergence cluster with an identified blood-volume mechanism (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}). The accompanying per-cell traffic-light reliability map (Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light}) is the principal deliverable for downstream Pulse users: it summarises which combinations of patient reference body, ARDS severity, and steady-state output are trustworthy as-is, which require flag-stratified handling, and which warrant per-channel justification before being ingested into downstream analyses.

For the computational-physiology community, the atlas matters in two ways. First, it directly addresses the Pulse maintainers' own statement that no formal sensitivity or uncertainty quantification has been performed on user-facing operational inputs~\cite{pulsefaq}: the Morris screen shows input sensitivity is strongly skewed, with the top three inputs accounting for a median 93~\% of summed $\mu^{*}$ on 48 of 48 cell $\times$ output combinations, a result independently cross-verified by a SHAP analysis on the clean subset (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s13}). Second, the method itself carries over to other engines. It pairs a Morris elementary-effects screen of the operational inputs with a four-flag steady-state classifier that sorts every run into a category instead of discarding any. No comparable open atlas yet exists for related lumped-parameter engines such as BioGears or HumMod. The same recipe, demonstrated here on Pulse v4.3.1, could be applied to them by wrapping each engine's own inputs and outputs, and would broaden the community's overall reliability picture for synthetic-physiology infrastructure as a whole.

For machine-learning pipelines that train on Pulse-generated synthetic patient cohorts, the four-flag classifier doubles as a per-cell data-quality contract. That contract rests on a clean null result --- silent input clamping never occurs (0 of 1{,}280 runs) --- so Pulse always rejects an out-of-envelope setup input loudly rather than quietly bending it into range: a bad configuration fails visibly instead of contaminating a cohort undetected. Green cells in Figure~\ref{fig:traffic_light} are safe to ingest; yellow cells should be filtered to the clean subset with the per-cell flag rate reported in training-set metadata; red cells require per-channel and per-input justification. Two specific traps documented here merit explicit attention. The sex-stratified divergence cluster (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}) means that pooling male and female runs from the input-extreme regime without flag-stratified filtering ingests a spurious sex covariate at the male reference body's divergence boundary --- a structural artefact of Pulse's allometric blood-volume baseline rather than a clinical phenomenon. The compensatory-tachycardia decoupling reported in \S\ref{sec:results-secondary} means the input HR (the value you set) and the output HR (the value Pulse settles at) are not interchangeable in ARDS scenarios. Otherwise, a model trained on Pulse cohorts will learn the engine's baroreceptor and chemoreceptor reflexes as a steady-state feature rather than as an active physiological response to hypoxemia (low oxygen).

\textbf{Complementarity with prior external falsification work on MIMIC-III.} Cornish et al.~\cite{cornish2023} applied a causal-falsification framework to Pulse on MIMIC-III sepsis trajectories and reported 85 total Hoeffding-inequality rejections (a distribution-free falsification test flagging each channel whose Pulse-versus-cohort discrepancy exceeds a statistical confidence bound) across 15 physiological quantities. Of these, 82 of 85 fall outside the four-channel overlap (HR, MAP, PaO$_2$, PaCO$_2$): 80 on channels the present atlas does not assess (electrolyte, metabolic, acid--base, dermal), plus 2 on partial-overlap pressure channels; only 3 of 85 fall on the four-channel overlap, all on PaCO$_2$. The two papers' failure surfaces are therefore complementary rather than redundant: Cornish covers chemistry-and-thermoregulation outputs under clinical-intervention sequences on a real cohort; the present atlas covers cardio-respiratory outputs across the theoretical operational acceptance envelope. The natural follow-up that neither paper does is a metabolic-and-renal atlas sweeping Pulse's endocrine and electrolyte sub-models the way this paper sweeps the cardiopulmonary inputs (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s7}).

Beyond guidance for downstream users, the atlas also serves the engine's maintainers: it provides the first public characterisation of an ARDS impairment-fraction solver-instability band ($[0.879, 0.945]$, \S\ref{sec:results-secondary}) and distils six concrete infrastructure recommendations for the Pulse maintainers (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s11}).

Four scope limits bound the conclusions. (i)~The atlas pins to Pulse v4.3.1 (results apply identically to the build-only v4.3.2 release); newer physiology branches will require re-mapping. (ii)~Patient anatomy is restricted to two standard adult reference bodies (male and female); the atlas does not characterise body-mass or height variation within sex. (iii)~The operational envelope is adult-critical-care VC-AC ventilation; pediatric translation, alternative ventilator modes (pressure-controlled, pressure-support), and within-run non-steady-state dynamics are out of scope by construction. (iv)~The Morris elementary-effect estimator excludes any one-at-a-time perturbation pair with a flagged endpoint (\S\ref{sec:methods-detector}), so at the FiO$_2 = 1.0$ envelope edge every FiO$_2$ pair is dropped---its FiO$_2 = 1.0$ endpoint is reliably \texttt{implausible} in healthy lungs (transient hyperoxia, \S\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia}) and \texttt{not\_steady\_state} in severe-ARDS S4\_F---which blanks the FiO$_2$ rows in Figure~\ref{fig:morris} and restricts the cross-sex Spearman comparison to S2 and S3 (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s6}). These are deterministic consequences of the preregistered implausibility-bound and steady-state-window contract at the operational envelope's edges, not a Pulse defect: additional trajectories cannot recover the pairs, and re-running the full grid at the 300-second window that resolves the hyperoxia plateau would forfeit preregistration integrity for no change in the settled bulk. Each of these is bounded rather than open-ended; none undermines the atlas's reliability claims within its declared scope.

The reliability atlas presented here is intended as foundational infrastructure rather than a terminal deliverable. The four-flag classifier and the per-cell traffic-light are reusable instruments that downstream Pulse users can apply to their own configurations without re-running the present sweep, and the atlas pipeline (preregistered, reproducible from a single Docker image with a fixed seed, released under Apache-2.0 alongside an audit ledger of all quantitative claims~\cite{wilkinson2016, munafo2017}) is designed to be re-executable when Pulse releases new physiology branches. Four concrete extensions are immediate priorities. The present adult-envelope characterisation is the methodological prerequisite for a pediatric translation layer (in preparation, validation underway), because pediatric physiological dynamics may differ from adult dynamics and cannot be safely adapted from an unmapped substrate. A native pediatric Pulse fork (planned) follows from that translation work. A complementary metabolic-and-renal atlas would cover the channels Cornish et al.~\cite{cornish2023} identified as most assessment-sensitive but that no operational-input sweep currently characterises. A further priority is to validate the atlas's reliability cells against real-world clinical databases---for example adult intensive-care records such as MIMIC-IV---by cross-tabulating documented ventilation settings against the per-cell reliability map. Together, these next steps---pediatric adaptation and clinical-database validation foremost---would broaden the cardiopulmonary-and-metabolic Pulse-reliability picture that the present work begins.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Pulse engine and configuration}\label{sec:methods-pulse}

All simulations used Pulse v4.3.1 (Kitware, Apache-2.0)~\cite{bray2019}, a real-time-capable lumped-parameter whole-body physiology engine that solves the coupled cardiovascular, respiratory, gas-exchange, endocrine, renal, and drug sub-models as a system of ordinary differential equations at a 20~ms fixed timestep. The respiratory sub-model is a 5-compartment lung (carina, left and right anatomic dead space, left and right alveoli). The engine was executed inside a single \texttt{pulse-atlas-engine} Docker image, pinned for the entire 1{,}280-run sweep so that every simulation runs against bit-identical engine binaries. v4.3.1 was the most recent stable release with documented physiology updates at the time of the sweep; the subsequent v4.3.2 release (August 2025) is build-system-only and contains no physiology, solver, validation, or patient-setup changes, so all atlas results apply identically to v4.3.2 (full release diff in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s1}). The sweep was dispatched to 2--3 parallel Docker workers on commodity laptop hardware, and bit-exact reproduction of the canonical sweep output was verified at three independent layers (the canonical sample, the per-cell aggregate parquets, and the derived statistical artefacts). Mechanical ventilation was restricted to volume-controlled assist-control (VC-AC) mode for cross-run comparability; alternative modes are out of scope by construction.

\subsection{Patient reference bodies}\label{sec:methods-patients}

Two adult reference bodies were used: a male body (age 18, 67.3~kg, 180~cm) and a female body (age 18, 56.2~kg, 163~cm). Weights are the U.S.\ Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) growth-chart 50th-percentile values for 18-year-olds of each sex; heights are clinically standard adult values: the male case fixes the body mass index (BMI) at 20.8 and the female case matches CDC P50 (BMI 21.1) and Pulse's own \texttt{heightStandardFemale\_cm = 163.0} reference. Pulse's setup-time constraints on each reference body --- the blood-volume baseline floor (\S\ref{sec:results-sex}), the systolic/diastolic blood-pressure gate, and the heart-rate / respiratory-rate initialisation bands --- were verified empirically (\S\ref{sec:methods-sweep}) and respected by the sampler so that the Morris design never proposes inputs outside Pulse's accepted envelope.

\subsection{Sweep design}\label{sec:methods-sweep}

Seven user-facing operational inputs were swept across their verified Pulse acceptance ranges: heart rate (HR, 50--110~bpm), systolic and diastolic blood pressure (SBP 90--120~mmHg, DBP 60--80~mmHg with the post-clip pulse-pressure ratio DBP/SBP $\leq 0.75$ enforced), respiratory rate (RR, 8--20~bpm), tidal volume (Vt, 300--800~mL), inspired oxygen fraction (FiO$_2$, 0.21--1.0), and positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP, 0--20~cmH$_2$O). Each range is anchored to a primary verification source --- Pulse source-code audit, Pulse FAQ, or empirical boundary-probe runs in the companion technical work --- table-by-table in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s2}. Mean arterial pressure (MAP) was derived from SBP and DBP as $\mathrm{DBP} + (\mathrm{SBP} - \mathrm{DBP})/3$ rather than swept independently. Four scenarios spanned the adult critical-care severity range: a healthy baseline (S1, ARDS impairment fraction = 0) and three Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome severities (S2, mild, impairment = 0.26; S3, moderate, impairment = 0.60; S4, severe, impairment = 0.95) calibrated against the Berlin Definition PaO$_2$/FiO$_2$ bands~\cite{ards2012} and bracketing the 0.91--0.945 solver-instability zone characterised in the companion technical work (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5}). The Morris elementary-effects design~\cite{morris1991, campolongo2007} used 20 trajectories at 4 grid levels per input, generated via SALib~\cite{iwanaga2022} with random seed 42; each trajectory contributes $(\mathrm{num\_inputs} + 1) = 8$ model evaluations, yielding 160 runs per (scenario $\times$ sex) cell and 1{,}280 runs across the full sweep. These 20 trajectories were fixed in preregistration: enough to give stable $\mu^{*}$ rankings, yet few enough to keep the full sweep feasible on commodity laptop hardware. Each run executed for 120 seconds of simulated time, with output channels sampled at 5-second intervals (13 samples in the final 60-second steady-state window). How Pulse translates this single per-lung impairment-fraction scalar into respiratory-mechanics and gas-exchange changes --- one \texttt{Severity} value driving four hard-coded, adult-calibrated interpolation curves (distal airway resistance, alveolar compliance, alveolar diffusion surface area, and pulmonary shunt) --- is documented in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s5-mapping}.

\subsection{Steady-state classifier}\label{sec:methods-detector}

Every simulation was classified by a four-flag steady-state detector before any statistical analysis. \texttt{clamped} fires when Pulse's setup-rejection log contains any of 4 documented input-rejection patterns, indicating that Pulse silently bent a setup input out of its acceptance band. \texttt{diverged} fires when the simulation failed to complete with finite output, the CSV is missing, or the engine exited with a non-zero status. \texttt{implausible} fires when any steady-state output mean exceeds the locked hard physiological bounds (representative bounds: HR [20, 220]~bpm, PaO$_2$ [20, 600]~mmHg, SpO$_2$ [50, 100]~\%). The complete bound table is reproduced in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3}, where each bound is anchored to primary critical-care and respiratory-physiology literature: the ARDS Network lung-protective ventilation trial~\cite{brower2000}, the Severinghaus oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve~\cite{severinghaus1979}, the Sepsis-3 and Surviving Sepsis Campaign hemodynamic thresholds~\cite{singer2016, evans2021sepsis}, the ESH 2023 hypertensive emergency definition~\cite{mancia2023esh}, and West's respiratory physiology~\cite{west2020respiratory}. The PaO$_2$ upper bound of 600~mmHg is deliberately conservative relative to the alveolar-gas-equation sea-level ceiling of $\approx$~663~mmHg on FiO$_2$ = 1.0; a parallel sensitivity-analysis reclassification under the alternative \texttt{HARD\_BOUNDS\_PHYSIOLOGICAL} dictionary (PaO$_2$ upper raised to 700~mmHg) is reported in Section~\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia} and characterised in the companion technical monograph. \texttt{not\_steady\_state} fires when the steady-state output flatlines at a physiological extreme, exhibits variance above the locked coefficient-of-variation threshold (0.05 for tidal volume), or drifts more than the per-channel locked threshold (Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3}) over the final 60-second window, where drift is estimated by ordinary least-squares regression on the 13 sample points in the [60, 120]~s window. The four flags are independent presence counts; a single simulation can fire several. For partition-style analyses, the priority order (ordered by simulation failure chronology, see Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3}) resolves each run to a single \texttt{primary\_flag}. A 22-run pilot on the healthy-male cell verified five preregistered gating criteria (success rate, detector self-consistency, 13-point steady-state window, clamp-regex reachability, and Pulse determinism within a $10^{-6}$ relative tolerance) before the full sweep launched; all thresholds were locked in preregistration before any data was collected~\cite{munafo2017}.

The [60, 120]~s steady-state window itself was preregistered before any sweep data was collected. Opening the evaluation window at $t = 60$~s --- half-way through the 120~s simulated run --- places it well past the initial ventilator-settling transient, and 13 sample points at the 5-second interval are the minimum that resolve the OLS-slope, coefficient-of-variation, and flatline diagnostics that gate the \texttt{not\_steady\_state} flag. A cross-output audit on the full sweep (companion technical work, S012) verifies that drift above the locked per-channel thresholds is contained at $\leq$2.6~\% for HR, $\leq$0.7~\% for MAP, and $\leq$2.0~\% for PaCO$_2$ across the F-003 hyperoxia and F-004 Vt-oscillation populations; the only exception --- PaO$_2$ and Vt at FiO$_2 \geq 0.95$ --- is the transient hyperoxia regime fully characterised in \S\ref{sec:results-hyperoxia}.

\subsection{Statistical analyses}\label{sec:methods-stats}

The Morris sample was generated with the SALib implementation~\cite{iwanaga2022}; the Morris mean-absolute-elementary-effect indices ($\mu^{*}$) and standard deviations ($\sigma$) were then computed per (scenario, sex, input, output) cell from the clean elementary-effect pairs, with non-parametric 95~\% bootstrap confidence intervals on $\mu^{*}$ from 1{,}000 trajectory-level resamples. Hypothesis~1 (skewed sensitivity, the top-3 inputs jointly account for $\geq 50~\%$ of summed $\mu^{*}$) was evaluated per cell $\times$ output on the point-estimate top-3 fraction of summed $\mu^{*}$; the underlying $\mu^{*}$ values carry the trajectory-level bootstrap CIs described above. Hypothesis~4 (sex-stratified reliability) used per-cell-output Wasserstein distances between the male and female clean-run distributions. These distances were tested against a within-sex permutation null built from 2{,}000 random within-sex splits, and corrected for the 24-test family using Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate control at $q = 0.05$. Cross-sex agreement of input-importance rankings was quantified by the Spearman rank correlation between male and female $\mu^{*}$ vectors per (scenario, output), with paired-input bootstrap 95~\% confidence intervals from 2{,}000 input-resamples. A preregistered Sobol bolt-on was specified to fire on any cell whose dominant input exhibited a $\sigma / \mu^{*} \geq 0.6$ point estimate. CI-aware re-evaluation using the trajectory-level bootstrap finds that zero of the 48 cells satisfy this trigger when the lower bootstrap bound on $\sigma / \mu^{*}$ is required to sit at or above 0.6, so the bolt-on is closed by evidential redundancy rather than compute shortage; the full bucket decomposition (31~/~48 CI-robustly linear, 12~/~48 straddling 0.6, 5~/~48 with no estimable CI) is reported in Supplement~\S\,\ref{sec:s3-sobol} and a ready-to-fire SALib implementation is preserved in the released code base. The full sampler, sweep driver, four-flag detector, statistical-analysis pipeline, and the audit ledger that re-derives every quantitative claim in this paper from the canonical output artefacts are released open-source (\S Code availability) and were preregistered before data collection.
