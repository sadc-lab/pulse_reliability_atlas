------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TITLE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An open reliability atlas for the Pulse Physiology Engine: sensitivity,
divergence, and sex-stratified failure modes

(Running title / short title: Reliability atlas: Pulse Physiology Engine)

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ABSTRACT  (149 words)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pulse is one of the most widely used open-source physiology simulators, yet no formal sensitivity
analysis of its operational inputs has been published. We present the first
reliability atlas across Pulse's adult critical-care envelope: Morris
elementary-effects sampling of seven inputs across four ARDS
severities and two reference bodies (1,280
simulations). A four-flag classifier (clamping, divergence, implausible,
non-steady-state) surfaces three failure modes. Tidal-volume instability in
20.6 % of runs reflects patient–ventilator dyssynchrony; locking the VC-AC
inspiration trigger to −2 cmH2O makes Vt deterministic for cohort generation.
At FiO2 = 1.0 a transient hyperoxia regime makes 120-second runs underestimate
plateau PaO2, which 300-second runs resolve near the alveolar-gas ceiling
(median 643 mmHg), consistent with Pulse's modelled A-a gradient. Solver
divergence fell entirely on the male body (20/1,280); reducing its
blood-volume baseline to the 4,082 mL floor eliminated it. The per-cell
traffic-light map is a data-quality filter for Pulse cohorts, released
open-source under Apache-2.0.
