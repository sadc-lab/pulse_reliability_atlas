TITLE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An Open Reliability Atlas of Sensitivity and Failure Modes in the Pulse Physiology Engine

(Running title / short title: Reliability Atlas: Pulse Physiology Engine)

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ABSTRACT  (149 words, npj cap 150)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pulse is an actively maintained open-source adult-physiology simulator, yet
its maintainers report no formal sensitivity analysis of its operational
inputs. We present the first open reliability atlas across its critical-care
envelope: Morris elementary-effects sampling of seven inputs, four severity
scenarios, male and female bodies (1,280 simulations). Sensitivity is skewed:
three inputs carry a median 93 % of each output's total. A four-flag
classifier reveals failure modes: two operator-actionable, one sex-dependent.
Tidal-volume instability (20.6 % of runs) reflects patient–ventilator
dyssynchrony; locking the VC-AC trigger to −2 cmH2O collapses that
variability. At FiO2 = 1.0, transient hyperoxia makes 120-second runs
underestimate plateau PaO2; 300-second healthy-lung reruns give a median 643
mmHg. Solver divergence was all-male in an input-extreme regime (20/1,280);
reducing the male blood-volume baseline to the 4,082 mL floor eliminated it.
Runs and flags are released openly, so users can see which regions need
checking before training on Pulse output.
