------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TITLE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An open reliability atlas for the Pulse Physiology Engine: sensitivity,
divergence, and sex-stratified failure modes

(Running title / short title: Reliability atlas: Pulse Physiology Engine)

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ABSTRACT  (148 words)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pulse is a widely used open-source adult physiology simulator, yet no formal
sensitivity analysis of its operational inputs has been published. We present
the first reliability atlas across Pulse's critical-care envelope: Morris
elementary-effects sampling of seven inputs across four severity levels
(healthy through severe ARDS) in two bodies (1,280 simulations). A four-flag
classifier (clamping, divergence, implausible, non-steady-state) surfaces
several failure modes, three operator-actionable. Tidal-volume instability in
20.6 % of runs reflects patient–ventilator dyssynchrony; locking the VC-AC
inspiration trigger to −2 cmH2O makes Vt deterministic for cohort generation.
At FiO2 = 1.0 a transient hyperoxia regime makes 120-second runs underestimate
plateau PaO2, which 300-second runs resolve near the alveolar-gas ceiling
(median 643 mmHg). Solver divergence fell entirely on the male body
(20/1,280); reducing its blood-volume baseline to the 4,082 mL floor
eliminated it. These results map where Pulse is trustworthy and provide an
open-source data-quality filter (Apache-2.0) for cohorts.
