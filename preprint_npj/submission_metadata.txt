------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TITLE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An open reliability atlas for synthetic patient cohorts: sensitivity,
divergence, and sex-stratified failure modes in the Pulse Physiology Engine
v4.3.1

(Running title / short title: Reliability atlas: Pulse Physiology Engine)

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ABSTRACT  (≈145 words)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pulse is among the most-cited open-source whole-body physiology simulators,
used for synthetic patient cohorts and computational physiology research. Yet
no formal sensitivity analysis of its operational inputs has been published. We
present the first reliability atlas across the adult critical-care envelope:
Morris elementary-effects sampling of seven inputs across four Acute
Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) severities and two patient reference
bodies (1,280 simulations). Tidal-volume instability in 20.6 % of runs is
identified as patient-ventilator dyssynchrony and corrected by setting the
volume-controlled assist-control (VC-AC) inspiration trigger to −2 cmH2O. A
transient hyperoxia regime at FiO2 = 1.0 means 120-second simulations
underestimate plateau PaO2; 300-second runs converge to a plateau (median
643 mmHg) sitting ~20 mmHg below the alveolar-gas-equation prediction of
≈663 mmHg, consistent with Pulse's modelled A-a gradient. Solver divergence
concentrated entirely on the male reference body (20/1,280 runs); reducing the
male blood-volume baseline toward its 4,082.24 mL floor monotonically
eliminated divergence. The resulting per-cell traffic-light map provides a
reusable data-quality filter for downstream users—including machine-learning
and clinical-AI pipelines that rely on such synthetic patient cohorts; all data
and code are released open-source under Apache-2.0.
